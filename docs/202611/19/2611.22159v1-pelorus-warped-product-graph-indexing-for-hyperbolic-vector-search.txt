## ABSTRACT 

Hyperbolic embeddings in the Poincaré ball and Lorentz hyper-boloid represent hierarchical, taxonomic, and entailment relations with substantially lower distortion than flat Euclidean spaces, sup-porting retrieval-augmented generation, vision-language align-ment, and knowledge-base search. Serving million-scale approxi-mate nearest neighbor (ANN) queries over curved manifolds, how-ever, exposes a fundamental tension between geometric fidelity and memory efficiency: flat inner-product indices distort geodesic order across radial shells, whereas native hyperbolic graph indices either fetch full-precision coordinates at every traversal hop or quantize ambient coordinates without decoupling conformal scales. Transplanting flat proximity-graph pruning and vector compression to hyperbolic space also fails. Because negative curvature bends geodesics toward the origin and scales directional inner products by the singular conformal factor (1 − 𝑐 ∥𝑥 ∥22)−1, interior hubs geodesi-cally occlude entire spherical neighborhoods during Vamana prun-ing, shared-query quantization errors fail to cancel across radial shells, and tight boundary clusters suffer margin-to-noise ratio (MNR) collapse. We present Pelorus , a warped-product proximity-graph in-dex for hyperbolic vector search. Pelorus decouples exact single-precision radial and conformal scalars in a 32-byte vertex header from scale-invariant spherical directional codes—instantiated with training-free 2-bit Sign-Magnitude (SM) bitplanes—deferring full-vector double-precision geodesic evaluation to a compact reranking tail. Analyzing how hyperbolic conformal weights and geodesic curvature interact with polar directional quantization, we establish query boundary-depth invariance on isobathic shells, characterize cross-shell query-noise non-cancellation, derive a deterministic parameter-free orthogonal-projection estimator, and introduce a purity-gated local tangent-cone residual code with Conformal Ja-cobian gating (CJ-Gate) that attenuates intra-cone tangent error with zero per-vertex byte overhead and without global random rota-tions while keeping diffuse manifolds on the zero-overhead global 2-bit path. Guided by these geometric properties, we construct a warped-product fiber-bundle graph inside-out using QuadriPrune— partitioning each vertex’s out-degree across four geometric chan-nels (stem-excess geodesic fibers, radially shielded isobathic rings restoring same-shell error cancellation, and inward/outward ra-dial elevators)—paired with degree-preserving spanning-tree chord swaps that achieve full directed BFS reachability from entry hubs across all measured graphs. An execution engine co-locates polar headers, variance-stabilized 256-bit early beam sketches, and 2-bit bitplanes within single 4096 B OS pages to avoid cross-array sec-ondary DTLB walks per accessed record and evaluates bitplane inner products via a 3-to-2 carry-save AVX2 kernel. Across eight real and synthetic hyperbolic benchmarks on AMD EPYC 9654 (Zen 4), Pelorus builds indices 5 .44 × faster (35 .0 s vs. 190 .2 s on 32 threads on Cohere-1M) than full-precision polar graphs (1 .91 × faster than 8-bit Polar-SQ8 at 66 .9 s) and shrinks hot naviga-tion memory to 517 .0 MB page-co-located (2 .44 × below Polar-SQ8, or 485 .0 MB / 2 .60 × in split form). At 99% Recall@10 on Cohere-1M, Pelorus improves throughput over full-precision polar traversal by 4.30 × on 1 thread (1 ,930 vs. 448 QPS) and 6 .31 × on 24 threads (42 ,466 vs. 6 ,735 QPS); on this benchmark, HyperspaceDB (3 .23 GB) peaks at 96 .78% Recall@10 (290 vs. 4 ,939 QPS on 1 thread at 95% recall) and centered Faiss peaks at 98 .91% (127 vs. 2 ,907 QPS on 1 thread at 98% recall). On native hyperbolic MERU-1M, Pelorus delivers 53 ,483 QPS (99 .25% Recall@10, 3 .37 ×–3 .88 × over HyperspaceDB FP32) and reaches 65 ,405 QPS (99 .36%) on WordNet-Hazy on the global 2-bit path (0 cones across diffuse real sets), while activating 256 cones on clustered shells to raise Clustered-Narrow recall from 34 .22% to 99 .385% at 𝐿 = 256 (99 .80% at 𝐿 = 384; 1 .84 ×–4 .56 ×

higher 99%-recall QPS than HyperspaceDB FP32) and delineating recall limits on isotropic narrow shells.

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Representation models that embed text, images, and structured enti-ties into negatively curved Riemannian manifolds—specifically the Poincaré ball B𝐷 𝑐 and the Lorentz hyperboloid L𝐷 𝑐 —provide a prin-cipled geometric inductive bias for hierarchical retrieval. Because geodesic ball volume in hyperbolic space grows exponentially with radius rather than polynomially, trees, ontologies, and asymmetric entailment cones embed with far lower metric distortion than in flat Euclidean space [24, 32, 47, 52]. Recent encoders scale curved representations across graph neural networks [10, 48], fully hyper-bolic Transformers and vision backbones [15, 21, 40], million-vector multimodal alignment (MERU [19]), and hyperbolic dense retrieval for retrieval-augmented generation (HypRAG [42, 43]). Serving low-latency, high-recall approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) queries over curved manifolds nevertheless remains a severe database systems bottleneck. In flat Euclidean and cosine spaces, proximity graphs such as HNSW [45], NSG [23], and Vamana (DiskANN) [5, 55] define the latency–recall frontier. Industrial and academic compressed graph engines—including locally-adaptive scalar quantization (LVQ) [1], binary lookup tables (BBQ) [59], ran-domized extended codes (SymphonyQG) [31], and training-free arXiv:2611.22159v1 [cs.DB] 17 Nov 2026 2-bit Sign-Magnitude (SM) indexing in QuIVer [66]—further accel-erate search by reading full FP32 vectors only during tail rerank-ing. In hyperbolic spaces, existing approaches force an unsatisfac-tory trade-off. Data-oblivious hyperbolic locality-sensitive hash-ing (LSH) [17] requires wide multi-table probing at 99% + recall, while flattening B𝐷 𝑐 or L𝐷 𝑐 into Euclidean or inner-product prox-ies inside production vector databases (Faiss [20, 39], Milvus [60], AnalyticDB-V [64], VBASE [69]) conflates radial hierarchy depth with angular proximity and inflates candidate pools across radial shells. Conversely, native hyperbolic graph indices [7, 29, 50] either evaluate geodesic distances over full-precision coordinates at every graph hop or quantize ambient coordinates without decoupling conformal scales under single-metric pruning, incurring compute, memory, or distortion stalls: under exact traversal near the manifold boundary ( ∥𝑥 ∥2 → 1/√𝑐 ), the conformal factor 𝑓 𝑥 = 2/( 1 − 𝑐 ∥𝑥 ∥22)

diverges and requires IEEE-754 double-precision accumulation to prevent catastrophic cancellation, while random 3–6 KB vector fetches across millions of vertices amplify random memory traffic and data TLB (DTLB) pressure.

## 1.2 Warped-Product Polar Co-Design in Pelorus 

We address these obstructions with Pelorus —named after the mar-itime bearing dial that decouples angular direction from radial course—an in-memory hyperbolic proximity-graph index grounded in warped-product polar decoupling . ## 6 EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATION 

Our evaluation examines how warped-product graph indexing in Pelorus performs across real and synthetic curved manifolds. We first measure construction speed, hot navigation memory, and matched-recall throughput relative to full-precision and 8-bit polar graphs (§6.2), compare against exact hyperbolic graph indices and flat-proxy vector databases (§6.3), and test multi-threaded scaling from 1 to 24 hardware threads (§6.4). We then isolate each topolog-ical and execution mechanism via controlled ablations (§6.5) and evaluate external baselines on four synthetic distributions, using a four-stage recall-loss audit to characterize the measured traversal, beam-retention, and rerank-admission losses (§6.6). 

## 6.1 Experimental Setup 

Hardware and Protocol. Experiments execute in two isolated Linux containers on AMD EPYC 9654 (Zen 4) hosts, each restricted via cgroups to 32 logical CPUs and 60 GiB RAM. The CPU sets are 

64-95 and 128-159 . Swap is disabled and transparent huge pages (THP) are set to never , using standard 4 KiB pages for DTLB trans-lation. Each instance runs one timed benchmark at a time, and mea-surement manifests record instance placement and source/binary fingerprints. All Rust binaries are compiled in release mode target-ing native AVX-512F and AVX2+FMA. Unless otherwise stated, graph indices use base degree 𝑀 = 16 (maximum out-degree 

𝑀 0 = 32), construction beam 𝐿 𝑐 = 128, geodesic slack 𝛼 = 1.2, and 32 build threads. ## 7.1 Hyperbolic Representations and Non-Euclidean Indexing

## 7.2 Proximity-Graph Topology and Skewed Routing 

Flat Proximity Graphs and Benchmarks. Navigable small-world graphs (NSW [44] and hierarchical HNSW [45]), approximate relative-neighborhood and satellite-system graphs (NSG [23] and NSSG [22]), and Microsoft’s 𝛼 -slack Vamana index (DiskANN) [55] define the standard in-memory and SSD-resident ANN paradigms, as established across standardized community benchmarks [4, 5, 53, 62]. Production extensions of DiskANN support streaming up-dates in FreshDiskANN [54], centroid-partitioned disk posting lists in SPANN [14], and structured predicate filtering in Filtered-DiskANN [30]. Transplanting these pruning rules from their as-sumed flat ambient metrics to B𝐷 𝑐 permits radial occlusion of entire spherical neighborhoods by interior vertices. The angular escape

condition characterizes when a candidate improves on the origin (Theorem 3). 

Skewed Distributions, Hub Occlusion, and Block Locality. Recent database indices examine routing pathologies and block layout from complementary angles. RoarGraph [13] shows that cross-modal out-of-distribution (OOD) queries suffer hub occlusion and long detours on flat inner-product graphs, mitigating shift by building a bipartite graph over historical query projections. In negatively curved spaces, hub occlusion arises even for in-distribution queries as an intrinsic geometric consequence of sinh (√𝑐𝑅 ) fiber expansion rather than query-modality shift. Pelorus controls radial occlusion through Inside-Out depth seeding, stem-excess angle-guarded dom-inance, shielded isobathic rings, spanning-tree chord-swap island repair, and routing via the closed-form radial minimizer, with an-gular ring pruning on constant-depth data. For storage locality, Starling [61] reorders disk-resident graph neighborhoods into 4 KiB SSD segments to reduce physical block reads per hop. Addressing the in-memory DTLB translation bottleneck rather than SSD block I/O, Pelorus’s HotSmPage co-locates polar headers, 256-bit sketches, and complete 2-bit bitplanes of 𝐾 consecutive vertex IDs inside one 4096 B OS page ( 𝐾 = 16 at 𝐷 = 768, Table 4), avoiding cross-array secondary page-table walks without graph-isomorphism reorder-ing. 

## 7.3 Low-Bit Vector Quantization in Academia and Industry 

Subspace and Anisotropic Codebooks. Product quantization (PQ [38] and OPQ [28]) partitions flat vectors into learned sub-spaces, while PQ Fast Scan [2] and Quick ADC [3] pack 4-bit lookup tables into SIMD registers via vpshufb shuffles. In indus-trial maximum inner-product search (MIPS), Google’s ScaNN [34] observes that quantization error parallel to a candidate vector perturbs rankings more severely than orthogonal error, motivating anisotropic codebook losses and spilled orthogonal-residual assign-ment in SOAR [56]. Subspace codebooks, however, require offline iterative clustering and assume fixed flat inner-product weights; similarly, HiHPQ [51] learns end-to-end trainable hyperbolic product quantization codebooks during representation training rather than serving a training-free post-hoc graph index.
