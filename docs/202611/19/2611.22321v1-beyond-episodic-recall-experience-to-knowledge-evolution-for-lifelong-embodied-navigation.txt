# Beyond Episodic Recall: Experience-to-Knowledge Evolution for Lifelong Embodied Navigation 

## Weitao An Xidian University Xi’an, China Chenghao Xu Hohai University Nanjing, China Xu Yang Hohai University Nanjing, China Cheng Deng Xidian University Xi’an, China 

## Abstract 

Lifelong embodied navigation requires agents to ac-cumulate experience through continuous interaction and reuse it for subsequent goal searches. Existing memory-enhanced methods typically retain historical observations or construct structured scene representations. However, storing observations or high-dimensional semantic embed-dings incurs growing storage costs. Meanwhile, struc-tured representations support retrieval and semantic rea-soning but do not explicitly evolve accumulated experi-ence into environment-specific knowledge. To address these limitations, we propose Experience-Evolving Mem-ory (EvoNav), a novel two-timescale memory framework. It organizes semantic observations, spatial relations, and task-feedback cues into sparse experience atoms. During online operation, experience atoms continually consolidate repeated observations and form dynamic links based on transitions. At the slower timescale, EvoNav distills ac-cumulated category co-occurrences and feedback signals into environment-specific structural knowledge without re-lying on predefined object–room relations. For each new goal, EvoNav combines direct goal evidence from expe-rience atoms with learned structural knowledge to guide goal-directed search on the current traversability map. Ex-periments on single-goal, sequential multi-floor, and mul-timodal navigation demonstrate effective experience reuse and improved path efficiency with compact persistent mem-ory, together with successful transfer to a real robot. 

## 1. Introduction 

Zero-shot ObjectNav systems combine semantic map-ping with goal-directed exploration to search unfamiliar scenes [44, 45, 51]. Practical agents, however, often ex-ecute successive goals in the same multi-floor environ-ment, where past observations and traversal can inform later search. This calls for accumulating reusable knowledge across tasks [6, 38].

Existing memory-enhanced navigation methods repre-sent past experience in several forms. Image-based meth-ods directly retain visual snapshots or multi-view obser-vations for later retrieval [36, 41]. Feature-based meth-ods project language-aligned semantic embeddings into 3D space, producing spatial feature maps that support open-vocabulary queries [19, 21, 31]. Other methods adopt struc-tured representations, organizing perceived entities, spatial topology, and interaction history into object-relation, hier-archical, or persistent memory graphs for semantic reason-ing and decision-making [9, 17, 29, 33, 39]. In multi-floor environments, 3D traversability modeling and floor con-nectivity further make cross-floor navigation decisions exe-cutable [26, 50]. Together, these directions establish impor-tant foundations for experience reuse, structured reasoning, and cross-floor sequential navigation. Despite this progress, a gap remains, as illustrated in Fig-ure 1, between lightweight experience accumulation and learning environment-specific structural knowledge. Ac-cumulating visual observations and high-dimensional spa-tial features increases storage and maintenance costs as in-1arXiv:2611.22321v1 [cs.RO] 17 Nov 2026 teraction grows [41], but their immediate value is often limited to relocating a previously observed target. Scene graphs are commonly designed to organize perceptual en-tities or provide task-time relational context, focusing on semantic reasoning and decision-making rather than dis-tilling repeated traversal and task outcomes into compact, feedback-calibrated memory [17, 20, 42, 43]. Likewise, ex-ternal commonsense priors can guide exploration [45, 51], but may not reflect the structure of the current environ-ment [49]. In contrast, repeated object co-occurrences, behavior-supported transitions, and task feedback provide direct signals for learning how a particular environment is organized. We therefore ask: how can an agent re-tain reusable navigation experience compactly, and evolve it into environment-specific knowledge that guides subse-quent searches? Inspired by two-timescale learning [27], we formulate a memory-learning paradigm coupling fast online consolidation with slow environment-specific knowl-edge evolution. Accordingly, we propose EvoNav , a two-timescale experience-to-knowledge framework for lifelong embodied navigation. Drawing on the atomic notes and dynamic links of Zettelkasten [22, 40], EvoNav represents local interac-tions as sparse experience atoms containing semantic, spa-tial, relational, and feedback cues without imposing a fixed object=room hierarchy. At the fast timescale, online experi-ence consolidation merges repeated observations into exist-ing atoms and maintains perceptual and behavior-supported transition links. At the slow timescale, experience-to-knowledge evolution transforms these atom-level relations into environment-specific category-level co-occurrence and transition patterns, while task outcomes calibrate the goal-conditioned reliability of each atom. For each new goal, 

Atom=Graph Evidence Propagation (AGEP) combines di-rect atom evidence with learned structural support. The fused evidence is then propagated over perceptual and tran-sition links to rank relevant atoms. Atom=object binding and traversability checks then ground the selected atoms into executable navigation targets. Our main contributions are as follows: • Sparse experience atom memory. We introduce sparse experience atoms that compactly encode navigation-relevant semantic, spatial, and feedback cues, without relying on a fixed object-room hierarchy or persistently storing visual snapshots and per-voxel high-dimensional semantic features. • Memory evolution across timescales. We coordinate online experience consolidation with slower experience-to-knowledge evolution, integrating repeated observa-tions into experience and learning environment-specific structural knowledge from co-occurrences, behavioral transitions, and task feedback for subsequent searches. • Experience-graph reasoning and grounded naviga-tion. We combine direct evidence with learned structural support, using atom-ObjectMap binding and feasibility checks on the current traversability map to turn historical experience into actionable navigation candidates across tasks and floors. • Comprehensive evaluation and real-world deploy-ment. Experiments on single-goal, sequential multi-floor, and multimodal navigation demonstrate strong path effi-ciency with a compact persistent-memory footprint. De-ployment on a real mobile robot further validates the fea-sibility of the system. 

## 2. Related Work 

2.1. Goal Search and Cross-Floor Execution 

Map-based ObjectNav combines goal-directed exploration with global-local planning, semantic maps, and learned ob-jectives [7, 32]. End-to-end policies and topological mem-ories instead learn search behavior or reason over previ-ously visited observations [8]. CoW, VLFM, ZSON, and GAMap use open-vocabulary evidence, multimodal goal embeddings, or geometric affordances [14, 28, 44, 46], whereas ESC and L3MVN score unexplored regions with pretrained object-scene priors [45, 51]. For multi-floor nav-igation, TravExplorer and LifelongCrossNav further model 3-D traversability, stairs, and cross-floor paths [26, 50]. 

2.2. Open-Vocabulary Maps and Scene Graphs 

Open-vocabulary maps attach language-aligned visual fea-tures to spatial locations, enabling post-hoc scene queries through dense, point-level, or implicit field representa-tions [19, 21, 31]. Scene graphs instead organize environ-ments around entities and relations. Early 3-D scene graphs unify objects, rooms, cameras, and spatial relations, while dynamic scene graphs add actionable topology and mov-ing agents [3, 34]. Hydra constructs hierarchical 3-D scene graphs online, and ConceptGraphs, OVSG, and Open3DSG support open-vocabulary objects, contextual entities, and relations [5, 17, 24]. Such structures can also support plan-ning through task-relevant subgraph retrieval, online scene-graph prompting, floor-room-object hierarchies, or multi-modal graph selection [20, 33, 39, 42]. These represen-tations offer complementary trade-offs between semantic coverage, organization, and scene information. 

2.3. Sequential Navigation and Experience Memory 

MultiON, GOAT, GOAT-Bench, and LMEE-Bench evalu-ate accumulated memory across multi-object, multimodal sequential-navigation, exploration, and question-answering settings [6, 23, 36, 38]. Navigation memories encode observation-action histories and states or retain goal-relevant working memory over topological maps [11, 12, 25, 30]. Other methods learn reusable abstractions, retrieve 2episodic experience, compress snapshots or trajectories, or update memory from feedback [4, 10, 15, 18, 35, 37, 41, 52]. ReMEmbR builds retrieval-augmented spatio-temporal memory, while LifelongCrossNav maintains 3-D semantic voxels for sequential cross-floor goals [2, 26]. 

## 3. Method 

We propose EvoNav, which organizes navigation observa-tions and interaction feedback into sparse atomic experi-ence. As shown in Figure 2, EvoNav has two core mod-ules. The first, illustrated in Fig. 2(a)-(c), performs two-timescale Experience-to-Knowledge evolution by retain-ing stable observations through online consolidation and extracting environment-specific structural knowledge at a slower timescale. The second, shown in Fig. 2(d), uses 

Atom-Graph Evidence Propagation (AGEP) to combine direct experience evidence with learned structural knowl-edge and retrieve goal-relevant atoms. These atoms are then grounded into executable navigation targets through instance binding and traversability checks. At time t, the atomic experience memory is: 

Mt = ( At, Et, Kt), (1) where At is the set of atoms, Et contains experience links, and Kt represents reusable scene knowledge derived within the current episode. Atoms and experience links are up-dated online, while category-level summaries and feed-back calibration are refreshed at subtask boundaries. The system separately maintains a compact ObjectMap and a goal-independent traversability map. The former provides instance-level geometry for grounding, whereas the latter uses obstacle information to validate candidate positions and support path planning. 

3.0 Problem Definition 

Given a goal sequence (g1, . . . , g K ) in the same unknown environment, the agent receives gk only when the current subtask starts and cannot access future goals during earlier subtasks. At time t, it selects an action using RGB-D ob-servations, its current pose, and accumulated memory. It searches for the current goal, declares that the goal has been found, and moves to the next subtask according to the task protocol. Maps and memory are retained across subtasks and reset at the start of a new episode. 

3.1. Sparse Experience Representation 

Our system builds a compact geometric ObjectMap and an atomic experience memory in parallel from the same RGB-D stream. Object masks are projected into 3D, clustered, and associated across frames to update ObjectMap instances with compact spatial extent, multi-class confidence, and ob-servation support. A separate goal-independent traversabil-ity map supports candidate validation and path planning. In-spired by Zettelkasten [1, 22, 40], a note-taking method that organizes experience as interconnected atomic notes, we consolidate object observations and their interaction con-text into sparse experience atoms. Each atom retains com-pact spatial summaries, semantic evidence, past observa-tion positions, and task feedback. The ObjectMap provides compact instance-level geometry for grounding, whereas atom memory accumulates reusable historical experience for knowledge evolution and later search. ## 4. Experiments 

4.1. Experimental Setup 

Benchmarks. We evaluate on three complementary benchmarks. HM3D ObjectNav isolates single-goal naviga-tion on 2,000 validation episodes from 20 scenes; HM3D-MFMON contains 927 ordered three-goal episodes from 36 multi-floor scenes, including 288 Cross-Floor-Required episodes, and is our primary test of long-horizon and cross-floor experience reuse [26]; GOAT-Bench evaluates 5-10 category, language-description, and instance-image goals on the Val-Unseen split [23]. 

Setup. The agent receives observations from a 640 × 480 

RGB-D camera with a depth range of [0 , 5.0] m and has a radius of 0.18 m. Its discrete action space contains M OVE -FORWARD (0.25 m), 30 ◦ left/right turns, 45 ◦ camera look-up/look-down actions, and S TOP . Each subtask has a bud-get of 500 actions, and a S TOP is considered successful when the agent is within 1.0 m of the target. The main text reports the prefix protocol, where an episode terminates at its first failed subtask. 

Evaluation metrics. For single-goal navigation, we re-port success rate (SR) and success weighted by path length (SPL). SR measures the fraction of successful episodes, while SPL additionally evaluates path efficiency using the ratio between geodesic shortest and executed path lengths.

4.3. Ablation Studies 

As shown in Table 6, removing atom-ObjectMap binding causes the largest degradation, suggesting that memory re-trieval alone is insufficient unless it is regrounded in current geometry. Disabling online consolidation or E2K evolution reduces SeqSR by approximately 2.6 and 2.1 points, re-spectively, supporting the complementary roles of suppress-ing redundant observations and extracting reusable struc-tural knowledge. Graph propagation contributes indirect relational evidence, whereas feedback calibration provides a smaller but consistent gain by suppressing repeated false guidance. 

4.4. Memory Efficiency and Scaling 

We define core persistent memory as the state used to store and retrieve experience across subtasks, excluding auxil-iary geometric and planning maps. Table 7 summarizes core memory and access cost under a common per-episode convention. 3D-Mem and MemoryExplorer use snapshots as persistent units, LifelongCrossNav uses feature-bearing voxels, and EvoNav stores atoms, experience links, and evolved class knowledge. EvoNav retains only 16 .16 atoms on average, corresponding to approximately 0.1 MiB of core memory, with slow-update and query times of ap-proximately 7 ms and 2 ms, respectively. By compari-son, the core memory of 3D-Mem, MemoryExplorer, and LifelongCrossNav is approximately 10 .2, 87 .4, and at least 

156 .2 MiB per episode, respectively. 

4.5. Real-World Experiments 

We deploy EvoNav on a Wheeltec R550 mobile robot equipped with an Astra Pro Plus RGB-D camera and an NVIDIA Jetson Orin NX Super 16GB module. The ef-fective sensing range is 0.6-5 m. Open-vocabulary percep-tion runs on a remote workstation with an NVIDIA RTX A6000 GPU, while localization, mapping, and motion con-trol execute on board. The robot and workstation commu-nicate over a local network using ROS 2. On this plat-form, EvoNav successfully completes long-horizon naviga-tion trials in real environments. ## 5. Conclusion 

We presented Experience-Evolving Memory, a lightweight memory framework for lifelong embodied navigation. The method consolidates semantic observations, spatial rela-tions, and behavioral transitions into sparse experience atoms, and progressively evolves repeated interactions into environment-specific structural knowledge. During naviga-tion, learned experience provides goal-relevant search guid-ance, while the ObjectMap and traversability map ground memory in geometrically feasible actions. This design supports experience reuse across consecutive subtasks, re-gions, and floors without manually injected layout pri-ors.
