## Abstract 

Semantic retrieval struggles with multi-hop question answering on both recall and preci-sion: gold passages are buried among distrac-tors that degrade the reader’s answer, and re-trieving them requires reasoning over the query rather than matching it. GraphRAG techniques expand semantic retrieval by connecting pas-sages to graph nodes and performing one or more hops of expansion after initial retrieval. This improves recall, but multi-hop expansion becomes expensive and brings additional noise into the retrieved set. We break this tradeoff by interleaving dense retrieval with knowledge-graph traversal for complex multi-hop ques-tions. Dense retrieval locates the first relevant regions of the search space at good recall, while traversal of a pre-existing external KG (Wiki-data) restores precision and improves recall by confirming retrieved passages against a reason-ing path through the KG. To keep traversal both question-adaptive and reliable, we have an LLM plan it as code generation task for graph traversal in SPARQL format but parse rather than execute the query. On full 1,000-question splits under a shared corpus and reader, and with a single configuration applied identically to every dataset, we reach 62.5 / 75.1 EM/F1 on HotpotQA, 73.5 / 81.0 on 2WikiMultiHopQA, and 37.1 / 50.0 on MuSiQue, ahead of repro-duced HippoRAG2, T 2RAG, and IRCoT on all three benchmarks. A second, token-matched setting stays ahead of T 2RAG on HotpotQA and 2Wiki while spending roughly its per-query budget. Executing the generated SPARQL in-stead of parsing it costs up to 11.4 EM, mostly because raw queries return empty results, not because of syntax errors. 

## 1 Introduction 

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2021) grounds large language models (LLMs) in factual, up-to-date knowledge by sup-plementing parametric memory with retrieved doc-uments (Karpukhin et al., 2020) (Gao et al., 2023). Standard RAG treats retrieval as a function of the query alone: the system encodes the question, searches a passage index, and returns the top-k

results by vector similarity. For simple factoid questions this suffices, but for multi-hop reasoning, vector similarity over the original query misses doc-uments that are essential yet semantically distant from the surface question. These bridging docu-ments can be discovered by reasoning about entity relationships. A natural fix is to widen the net, but dense re-trieval buys recall at a steep precision cost: on HotpotQA (Yang et al., 2018) the top-10 dense passages reach 80% recall at only 32% precision (Section 5), forcing the reader to reason over a con-text that is mostly distractors. Raising k dilutes precision further; lowering it drops gold. Graph-augmented retrieval (GraphRAG) (Peng et al., 2024) pursues this structure, but the dominant paradigm builds a graph from the target corpus : an LLM extracts entity–relation triplets from every passage (Edge et al., 2024; Guo et al., 2024). These triplets are fragmented and noisy, limiting the multi-hop reasoning they are meant to support (Kim et al., 2026), and the extraction is expensive construction cost (Li and Qin, 2026), which make the approach more suitable for closed-domain,i.e., finance, sport. We instead traverse a pre-existing external KG, Wikidata, whose structure is already dense and extraction-free. Prior external-KG methods, how-ever, traverse it with fixed, query-independent heuristics (Wu et al., 2025; Zhu et al., 2025) rather than adapting the traversal to each question. Our key idea is to interleave the two retriev-ers in a query-refinement loop so each supplies what the other lacks. Dense retrieval seeds entities and keeps recall high, and graph traversal confirms which seeds lie on the reasoning path and fetches the bridging passages dense retrieval missed, restor-ing precision (on HotpotQA, from 32% to 81% pre-cision at better recall). This interleaving, rather arXiv:2611.31771v1 [cs.CL] 24 Nov 2026 than either component alone, breaks the tradeoff. On the full 1,000-question evaluation splits of three multi-hop benchmarks, with a shared corpus, retriever, and reader across all systems, our ap-proach outperforms strong reproduced baselines on all three, with the largest margins where Wikidata coverage is strongest. Our contributions are: • Interleaving dense retrieval with KG traversal through a batch resolution and query-refinement loop, so dense recall and KG precision reinforce each other. • Parse-don’t-execute traversal , the enabling mechanism: the LLM plans traversal by writ-ing SPARQL, but rather than run the query, which mostly returns empty results on mis-linked identifiers, label mismatches, or over-constrained patterns (syntax errors and time-outs are rare), we parse it into tuples and ex-ecute anchored single-hop lookups. A con-trolled ablation shows that executing the raw query instead costs up to 11.4 EM (§6.2). • A controlled n=1000 study against repro-duced HippoRAG2, T 2RAG, and IRCoT un-der an identical corpus/retriever/reader/LLM, with component ablations and a cost–accuracy frontier: matched to T 2RAG’s per-query to-ken budget we remain more accurate on Hot-potQA and 2Wiki. • Two operating points along a cost–accuracy frontier , both applied identically to every dataset: an unbounded setting that pairs a lower resolver bar with a full, commit-oriented reader context for maximum accu-racy, and a bounded setting whose token-reduction levers match T 2RAG’s per-query budget while remaining more accurate than T2RAG on HotpotQA and 2Wiki. We use LLM-generated SPARQL as the plan-ning medium because LLMs reason and plan more reliably through structured outputs such as code. SPARQL and Wikidata schemas are well repre-sented in typical pretraining corpora, so the model already knows how to express a textual query as a well-formed graph pattern. Building on this in-tuition, our method improves both answer qual-ity and retrieval quality across all three datasets relative to the baselines, reaching 62.5, 73.5, and 37.1 EM on HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue—ahead of HippoRAG2, T 2RAG, and IRCoT on every split. 

## 2 Related Work 

Corpus-constructed graphs. Microsoft’s GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2024) builds a graph from the target corpus by extracting entity–relation triplets with an LLM, then clusters them into communities and generates hierarchical summaries. LightRAG (Guo et al., 2024) similarly constructs an entity graph and performs dual-level retrieval, while RAPTOR (Sarthi et al., 2024) recursively clusters and summarizes passages into a retrieval tree and KGP (Wang et al., 2024) builds adocument graph an LLM agent then traverses. Because LLM-generated triplets are fragmented and sparsely connected, TH-RAG (Kim et al., 2026) organizes them into a topic hierarchy to improve connectivity, and DualGraphRAG (Li and Qin, 2026) retrieves both local triples and shortest paths while cutting construction time by an order of magnitude. All of these require an expensive per-corpus build and apply uniform traversal regardless of the query. 

Iterative retrieval for multi-hop QA. A sepa-rate line interleaves retrieval with reasoning with-out any graph. MDR (Xiong et al., 2021) performs iterative dense retrieval, re-encoding the query with each retrieved passage to fetch the next hop; IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2023) alternates chain-of-thought steps with retrieval, Self-Ask (Press et al., 2023) de-composes a question into follow-up sub-questions, and FLARE (Jiang et al., 2023b) triggers retrieval whenever the generator is uncertain. Our query-refinement loop is in this spirit, but the rewritten query is grounded in structured facts recovered from the KG rather than generated free-form, and it drives graph traversal as well as dense retrieval. On the KG side, GNN-RAG (Mavromatis and Karypis, 2024) instead trains a graph neural network to re-trieve reasoning subgraphs, whereas our traversal is training-free. 

Agentic and reasoning-augmented GraphRAG. 

A parallel line integrates LLM reasoning more tightly with structure. Youtu-GraphRAG (Dong et al., 2025) adds hierarchical community detection in an agentic framework; Zarrinkia et al. (2026) use SPARQL-based chain-of-thought prompting with graph-walk compression. T2RAG (Gong et al., 2025) avoids explicit traversal, decomposing ques-tions into search triples resolved iteratively against a proposition index. These improve accuracy but pay multiple LLM calls per query for traversal or triple resolution. 

External-KG augmentation and traversal rea-soning. KG-Infused RAG (Wu et al., 2025) and KG 2RAG (Zhu et al., 2025) expand retrieval us-ing a pre-existing KG such as Wikidata, avoiding extraction cost and noise, but apply fixed-radius or fixed-parameter spreading activation, and KAP-ING (Baek et al., 2023) simply prepends retrieved triples to the prompt without traversal. Think-on-Graph (Sun et al., 2023) and its text-augmented successor ToG-2 (Ma et al., 2024) treat the KG as an environment for LLM-guided beam search; RoG (Luo et al., 2024) first plans relation paths on the KG and then retrieves along them; StructGPT (Jiang et al., 2023a) gives the LLM tool calls to read structured data iteratively; GraphFlow (Yu et al., 2026) trains a GFlowNet for trajectory sampling; ARK (Polonuer et al., 2026) is a tool-using agent balancing breadth and depth. These either apply uniform budgets, require graph-specific training, or incur full LLM inference at every traversal step. We share their use of a pre-existing KG but differ in how we plan the traversal. ToG and RoG al-ready achieve question-specific, execution-failure-free traversal, but at a cost: ToG invokes the LLM at every hop to pick the next relation, and RoG fine-tunes a dedicated relation-path planner. Methods that instead have an LLM emit executable SPARQL inherit its failure modes (hallucinated identifiers, syntax errors, timeouts, empty results); the closest, the concurrent MARS (Srivastava et al., 2026), like-wise performs adaptive multi-hop KGQA without fine-tuning but generates and executes SPARQL and so remains exposed to them. Our distinction is to treat generated SPARQL as a plan rather than an executable artifact : a single planning call emits SPARQL that we parse into anchored single-hop lookups, interleaved with dense retrieval, with no per-step LLM inference, no execution risk, and no traversal-specific training. We are thus not the first to reach failure-free question-specific traversal, but the first to reach it by parsing rather than executing generated SPARQL over a live external KG. 

## 3 Method 

3.1 Task Formulation 

Given a multi-hop question q, a corpus of pas-sages P = {p1, . . . , p n} each with a title ti, and a knowledge graph K (Wikidata) whose entities cor-respond to passage titles, the goal is to retrieve the gold supporting passages Gq ⊂ P and generate an answer from their text. Benchmarks annotate each question with 2–4 gold passages whose titles are en-tities on the reasoning chain. The reader generates the final answer conditioned only on passage text. The KG is used purely for retrieval guidance, so that answers remain grounded in natural-language evidence rather than raw triples. 

3.2 Overview 

Figure 1 shows the pipeline as a three-agent loop. Dense retrieval seeds entities from the question and keeps recall high. A batch resolution agent reads the seed passages, extracts the facts on the reason-ing path, and rewrites the question with resolved entities filled in. A plan agent expresses the next traversal as SPARQL, which we parse (rather than execute) into single-hop lookups against Wikidata; discovered bridge entities are title-matched to cor-pus passages and routed back through the resolver, which either confirms passages or emits a sharper refined query that re-seeds dense retrieval. The loop repeats until the resolver judges the question answerable, no progress is made, or a maximum-iteration budget is reached, after which a reader answers from the confirmed passages. Dense re-trieval and KG traversal thus feed each other: re-trieved text supplies the named entities the plan agent anchors on, and KG facts sharpen the query that dense retrieval acts on.

3.4 Batch Resolution and Query Refinement 

The resolution agent is the bridge between text and graph. Given the question, the facts resolved so far, and a set of candidate passages, it decides in a single LLM call which passages lie on the reasoning path, extracts a fact from each, states what is still needed, and rewrites the question with all resolved entities filled in. The refined query is the engine of the feedback loop. When KG traversal discovers a bridge entity (e.g., that a painter died in Venice), the resolver folds it into a sharper query that dense retrieval can act on, surfacing the specific passage that the orig-inal query could not distinguish from same-titled distractors. Symmetrically, retrieved passages sup-ply the named entities the plan agent anchors its next SPARQL on. 

Unbounded vs. bounded setting . We expose a 

relevance bar on the resolver as a knob along a cost– accuracy frontier, giving two operating points that we apply identically to every dataset. The bounded 

setting confirms only passages on the direct reason-ing path, maximizing precision at low token cost. The unbounded setting makes two changes: (i) the resolver confirms any passage that helps with the final answer or any intermediate hop , trading precision for recall, and (ii) similar to HippoRAG2 (Gutiérrez et al., 2025), the reader is always given a full set of reader-top-k passages (confirmed first, backfilled by reranking the seen pool) under a com-mit prompt that requires a best-guess answer rather than abstaining. Deep multi-hop questions leave the bounded resolver with too few confirmed pas-sages and lead the reader to refuse when a hop is only partially supported; the unbounded setting sup-plies more complete evidence and converts those abstentions into answers, at higher per-query cost (Section 6). 

3.5 Seeding, KG Title-Matching, and n-gram Matching 

Three deterministic mechanisms feed the resolver. (i) Dense seeding : Contriever-msmarco retrieves the top-k passages for the (refined) query. (ii) KG title-matching : when SPARQL traverses an en-tity X, X and any discovered bridge entity are matched by normalized title against the corpus, re-covering gold passages that dense retrieval never surfaced. (iii) Question n-gram matching : con-tiguous 5 →2-word n-grams of the question are matched against corpus titles, catching exact entity names that dense retrieval misses (“Billy Elliot”, “B Boy”) at zero LLM cost. ## 4 Experimental Setup 

Datasets. We evaluate on three multi-hop QA benchmarks: HotpotQA (Yang et al., 2018) (9,811-passage corpus), 2WikiMultiHopQA (Ho et al., 2020) (6,119 passages), and MuSiQue (Trivedi et al., 2022) (11,656 passages). For each we use the 1,000-question evaluation split and its retrieval corpus released by HippoRAG (Gutiérrez et al., 2024) and subsequently adopted by HippoRAG2 (Gutiérrez et al., 2025) and T 2RAG (Gong et al., 2025); using the same splits and corpora keeps our numbers directly comparable to that line of work. HotpotQA is predominantly 2-hop; 2Wiki mixes comparison and compositional 2-hop ques-tions; MuSiQue contains 2–4-hop compositional questions and is the hardest. 

Baselines. Along with LightRAG, we repro-duce three other strong GraphRAG systems, Hip-poRAG2 (Gutiérrez et al., 2025) and T 2RAG (Gong et al., 2025), and the iterative retrieve-and-reason baseline IRCoT (Trivedi et al., 2023), under iden-tical conditions (same corpus, retriever, reader LLM, and question set), so that comparisons are controlled rather than cross-paper. IRCoT is the canonical graph-free multi-hop baseline in this pro-tocol: it interleaves chain-of-thought steps with dense retrieval but applies no confirmation filter, which lets us isolate the contribution of KG-guided confirmation. We stress that these are matched-harness reproductions, not the baselines’ published configurations: HippoRAG2’s leading numbers use NV-Embed-v2 embeddings with a GPT-4o-mini reader, whereas here every system shares Contriever-msmarco and Qwen3-32B to enable a fair comparison between systems using the same backbone. This design isolates the traversal method from retriever and reader strength; a portion of the 2Wiki gap in particular is an abstention effect rather than retrieval quality, which we quantify in Section 6. 

Implementation. All systems use Qwen3-32B (Yang et al., 2025) as the LLM and Contriever-msmarco (Izacard et al., 2022) with a FAISS flat index as the dense retriever. We report our method at two operating points, each applied iden-tically to all three datasets: Ours (unbounded)

## 5 Results 

5.1 Main Results 

Our system is the best on all three splits. Under the unbounded setting, applied identically to all three datasets, our method reaches 62.5 EM on HotpotQA (exceeding HippoRAG2 by +9 .0,T2RAG by +14 .1, and IRCoT by +4 .8), 73.5 EM on 2Wiki, and 37.1 EM / 50.0 F1 on MuSiQue, ahead of HippoRAG2 (28.6 / 40.4), T 2RAG (26.6 / 37.7), and IRCoT (26.4 / 38.8). IRCoT, the strongest baseline on HotpotQA at 57.7 EM, il-lustrates the ceiling of graph-free iterative retrieval, which we exceed on every split. Feeding the same reader from dense retrieval alone (DPR-only) trails our full system by 14–42 EM per dataset, confirm-ing that the KG-guided retrieval set, not the reader, drives the gains. 

Ours (bounded) tightens the token-reduction levers of §6.3 until per-query cost matches T2RAG’s budget. Even so it stays ahead of T 2RAG on HotpotQA (57.2 vs. 48.4) and 2Wiki (67.7 vs. 55.2), i.e. both cheaper and more accurate. MuSiQue is the exception: its deep-hop ques-tions are the least compressible, so EM under the bounded budget falls to 27.3. 

5.2 Retrieval Quality 

Table 2 contrasts our supporting-passage retrieval with dense retrieval alone. Dense retrieval attains decent recall but very low precision: some gold passages are present but drowned in distractors. Adding KG traversal converts this into a high-precision retrieval set at higher recall on HotpotQA and 2Wiki, a roughly 2.5–3× precision gain that di-rectly explains the answer-quality gains in Table 1. On MuSiQue, recall reaches 68.0 at 55.6 preci-sion, the strongest retrieval numbers of any system, though a large share of questions still land in partial recall, reflecting the difficulty of its deeper com-positional chains (Section 6). LightRAG’s corpus-constructed graph retrieves least effectively of all systems (43.2/67.9/34.8 recall), with the HotpotQA and MuSiQue shortfalls traced to its hybrid re-triever returning empty results on roughly 40% of short factoid questions (keyword extraction yields no low-level keywords, so no local context is re-trieved). 

## 6 Analysis 

6.1 Component Ablations 

Table 3 isolates the two retrieval mechanisms. Dense retrieval alone (DPR-only) gives high recall but low precision and weak answers; SPARQL-only traversal is precise but recall-limited to what KG title/ n-gram matching anchors; interleaving the two (our full system) is strongest on every dataset. The two are complementary rather than redundant: on entity-named 2Wiki, SPARQL-only alone already reaches 69.6 EM, above every base-line, whereas HotpotQA and MuSiQue—whose entities are less often exact title matches—depend heavily on dense seeding ( +22 and +20 EM from adding it). 

Unbounded vs. bounded. The unbounded set-ting pairs a lower-bar resolver with a full commit-oriented reader context; the bounded setting adds token-reduction levers to reach T 2RAG’s per-query budget. The gap between them concentrates on questions with longer or partially-supported chains, where the tighter-budget resolver confirms too few passages and the reader is left to abstain. MuSiQue is the clearest case: the unbounded setting reaches 37.1 EM / 50.0 F1 (Table 1), against 27.3 / 36.1 for bounded. HotpotQA and 2Wiki, whose reasoning is shallower and whose retrieval is easier, are less sensitive, and the bounded setting stays within a few EM of unbounded on both. This is a knob along a cost–accuracy frontier, not a per-dataset choice: both settings use the same configuration across every benchmark.

6.3 Computational Efficiency: Tokens and LLM Calls

Construction cost. Part of our 2Wiki margin is a refusal gap, which we quantify rather than leave implicit. Scoring ev-ery system’s answers with a refusal detector (“not mentioned”, “cannot be determined”, and similar), HippoRAG2 abstains on 34.3% of 2Wiki questions at n=1000 , against 0.2% for our unbounded and 

7.2% for our bounded setting. ## 7 Conclusion 

We showed that a live external knowledge graph can be traversed effectively for multi-hop retrieval without building a corpus graph, by having an LLM plan traversal as SPARQL and then pars-ing rather than executing it into reliable single-hop lookups. Interleaved with dense seeding and a batch query-refinement loop, this outperforms strong reproduced graph-based baselines on all three benchmarks under matched conditions, at lower LLM cost and, when a curated KG already covers the domain, without the per-corpus graph-construction pass those baselines require. A single configuration, applied identically to every dataset, wins throughout; on the hardest split, MuSiQue, it reaches 37.1 EM, ahead of all three baselines, and a token-matched bounded setting stays ahead of T2RAG on HotpotQA and 2Wiki while spending roughly its per-query budget. 

## 8 Limitations 

Our approach depends on the availability and cov-erage of an external knowledge graph. We avoid a per-corpus construction pass by reusing Wikidata, but this shifts rather than eliminates the cost: Wiki-data itself is the product of large-scale curation, and in a domain not covered by an existing KG a graph would first have to be built or extracted, at which point the construction cost of the corpus-graph baselines would apply to us as well. Even where a KG exists, entities absent from it cannot be traversed, which bounds recall on niche-entity questions. The method targets benchmarks whose passage titles align with KG entity labels; corpora without such alignment would require an entity-linking step we do not evaluate. The approach also needs a named entity to an-chor traversal on. A query with no such entity (e.g., “who has won the most Grand Slams?”) reduces the contribution of the KG. Most information-seeking queries are entity-bearing, however: over 70% of web-search queries contain a named entity (Kas-turia et al., 2022), and Wikidata covers such entities broadly, so the only added cost is entity linking, with no per-corpus graph to build. On our bench-marks, roughly 7–11% of questions per dataset contain no named entity (by NER); for these, dense retrieval provides robustness. For a specialized cor-pus that Wikidata does not cover (e.g., QA over financial reports), a graph would have to be con-structed at a cost similar to T 2RAG’s, removing our offline efficiency advantage, although we would ex-pect the accuracy benefits of interleaving to carry over. MuSiQue remains our hardest split even at the unbounded operating point (37.1 EM): its deepest questions require composing three or four hops, and the plan agent cannot always anchor a first hop whose start entity is described relative to a prior hop rather than named. All experiments use a single reader LLM (Qwen3-32B); we did not study sensitivity to reader choice. 

## 9 Ethical Considerations 

We used a smaller model on these academic bench-marks to show the validity of our approach and to reduce the impact on the environment. Through-out the development of this work, generative AI systems were employed for language refinement and code test case generation. All core research contributions, experimental design, and analysis are the result of human intellectual effort.

## A Prompts

A.1 Plan Agent 

The plan agent is prompted with a system mes-sage (task framing + a retrieved Wikidata property schema + one worked example) followed by a per-iteration user message carrying the running state. The system message opens:                                     

> You are an expert in Wikidata and SPARQL. Your task: generate SPARQL queries that express what graph traversal paths to explore. . . The SPARQL you generate will NOT be executed directly. Instead, it will be parsed to extract (entity, property, direction) traversal tuples. So: focus on expressing the RIGHT entities and properties; use rdfs:label "Name"@en to anchor to named entities; use wdt:PXX for properties; keep queries simple and clear — they’re acommunication format, not executable code.

An agentic suffix casts it as a fact-discovery loop:                       

> You receive: the question; facts already resolved; what information is still needed; available properties on known entities (from probing); dead ends (properties that returned nothing —DO NOT repeat these). CRITICAL RULES: ALWAYS anchor queries to SPECIFIC named entities. NEVER query abstract classes. Focus on finding the MISSING information. Use CONFIRMED properties from the probed list. DO NOT repeat dead ends.

A.3 Reader 

The default reader reasons step-by-step and emits a short span (“ Thought: . . . Answer: ”), with three few-shot examples (a bridge, a comparison, and a yes/no question). The unbounded setting swaps in a commit variant that forbids abstention:                        

> ALWAYS commit to a concrete answer. Even if the passages are incomplete or the answer requires combining facts across passages and your own knowledge, give your single best guess. NEVER answer "unknown", "not stated", "insufficient information", or "cannot be determined". These are always wrong.

## B SPARQL Parser 

The parser converts a generated SPARQL query into an ordered list of traversal steps (ei, r i, d i)

without ever executing it. It applies the following rules to each query: 1. Strip non-traversal blocks. SERVICE wikibase:label , FILTER , and FILTER NOT EXISTS blocks are removed — they affect for-matting/execution but carry no traversal edge. 2. Label anchors. ?x rdfs:label "Name"@en 

binds variable ?x to a named entity; the name is later resolved to a QID via the entity-search API. 3. Direct QID anchors. wd:Q42 wdt:P69 ?u 

yields an outgoing step from Q42 along P69 .4. Incoming patterns. ?w wdt:P57 wd:Q25191 

yields an incoming step into Q25191 along 

P57 .5. Discard type constraints. Triples with 

P31 (instance of) or P279 (subclass of) are dropped; they constrain types rather than tra-verse. 6. Drop invented placeholders. Round-number QIDs (e.g. wd:Q12345 ) the model emits when it does not know a real ID carry no label hint and are skipped. 7. Chain dependent hops. When one hop’s out-put variable feeds the next ( ?v1 wdt:P1 ?v2 . ?v2 wdt:P2 ?v3 ), the second step is an-chored to a $RESULT reference so it executes against the first hop’s resolved entity. ## C MuSiQue Corpus De-duplication 

The MuSiQue retrieval corpus released by Hip-poRAG contains 11,656 passages stored as {title, text} pairs with no passage identifier. 647 titles are duplicated across 2,465 passages (homonyms, e.g. 28 distinct passages titled “New York City”). A naive corpus map keyed by title collapses these duplicates and silently drops 1,818 passages, so only 9,838 ever enter the index or reach the reader; supporting-passage recall then becomes title-level and optimistic. We key the corpus, in-dex, and gold labels by a synthetic passage id (title::hash(text) ) so that all 11,656 passages are indexed and recall is measured at the passage level. This matches how the graph-based baselines (HippoRAG2, T 2RAG) already ingest the corpus, which chunk by content rather than title; the change corrects our own ingestion rather than altering the shared benchmark. Beyond honest measurement, it materially helps answering: feeding the reader the correct homonym passage rather than a collapsed survivor raises MuSiQue EM from 29.4 to 37.1 (Appendix E gives a concrete case). HotpotQA and 2Wiki corpora have unique titles and are unaf-fected. 

## D Error Decomposition 

To locate where each dataset’s remaining errors come from, we bucket every wrong answer (EM =0) by supporting-passage recall: reader failures 

(recall = 100% — the gold passages were retrieved but the reader did not produce the exact answer), 

partial retrieval (0 < recall < 100% ), and zero retrieval . Table 6 shows a sharp split by dataset. On HotpotQA and 2Wiki, 75–84% of errors oc-cur despite perfect retrieval: the coupled retrieval set already contains the gold passages, and the residual loss is the reader emitting a near-miss span rather than the exact gold string. Retrieval is effectively solved on these two, and answer gen-eration is the ceiling. MuSiQue inverts this: only 31% of its errors are reader-side, while 69% are retrieval-limited (partial or zero recall). Of the 629 MuSiQue errors, 247 produced no SPARQL facts at all—the plan agent never anchored a traversal, the described-entity failure mode of Appendix E— confirming that MuSiQue’s bottleneck is assem-bling the reasoning chain, not reading it. 

## E Qualitative Examples 

A full trace (and why de-duplication matters). 

Consider the MuSiQue question “ How many times did the plague occur in the city where the painter of The Bacchanal of the Andrians died? ” (gold answer: 22). Dense seeding retrieves the paint-ing’s passage, from which the resolver extracts that Titian ( Q47551 ) painted it. The plan agent writes SPARQL anchoring Titian and traversing 

P20 (place of death); the parser turns this into the single-hop lookup Q47551 → P20 → Venice .The resolver folds “died in Venice” into a refined query, which re-seeds dense retrieval and surfaces the plague-in-Venice passage. On the collapsed corpus the reader receives the wrong “Black Death” homonym text and answers “Once” (EM = 0 ); on the de-duplicated corpus (Appendix C) it receives the passage stating “plague occurred in Venice 22 times between 1361 and 1528” and answers “22” (EM = 1 ). The same traversal succeeds either way — what changes is whether the reader is handed the correct passage text. 

Property probing corrects relation choice. Acommon plan-agent error is selecting a plausible but wrong property, e.g. P570 (date of death) when the question needs P20 (place of death). After each traversal we probe the discovered entity for its avail-able properties and feed the list back to the plan agent, which then re-issues the hop with the correct relation — turning a dead end into a resolved fact without a wasted iteration. 

Failure 1: an entity described, not named. 

For “ What is the birthplace of the person after whom São José dos Campos was named? ” (gold: Nazareth), the first hop’s start entity is described 

(“the person after whom X was named”) rather than named. The plan agent has no label to an-chor on, so it emits no traversable SPARQL: the run ends at iteration 0 with zero SPARQL facts and no confirmed passages, and the reader guesses “Bethlehem.” This is the dominant MuSiQue failure mode — the plan agent cannot anchor a hop whose start entity is defined only relative to a later hop. 

Failure 2: dense seeding anchors the wrong en-tity. For “ Who is the spouse of the actor that plays Jarvis in the Avengers: Age of Ultron? ” (gold: Jennifer Connelly, spouse of Paul Bettany), dense retrieval surfaces a Samuel L. Jackson / Nick Fury passage instead of Paul Bettany / Jarvis. The re-solver confirms the wrong actor, the traversal chain is anchored on the wrong entity, and the answer col-lapses to “Unknown.” Here the failure is upstream of traversal: the seed itself is wrong, so no amount of correct KG traversal recovers the gold path.
