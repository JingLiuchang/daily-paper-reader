## ABSTRACT 

With the growing demand for large language model (LLM) services, optimizing LLM serving systems is becoming increasingly complex, and the bottleneck is no longer predicting how a given configuration will perform. The harder problem is deciding what to investigate: which mechanisms matter for a workload, which configurations are worth testing, and what evidence a decision still needs. In this work, we present N OE VERA , an agentic 

Second Brain that goes beyond just one-shot retrieval by turning papers, code, benchmarks, and experimental results into a persistent, provenance-preserving knowledge base with three planes: source evidence, reusable synthesis, and agent memory. Decision-aware retrieval assembles compact evidence packs, role-specific agents reason over them and invoke a simulator (Vidur or GAUSS) whenever quantitative support is needed, and every finding is written back as a scoped evidence record, so knowledge guides experiments and experiments refine knowledge. In configuration search over four serving scenarios, NoeVera comes within 5% of the optimum in a median of 6–7 simulator calls, versus 9 for Bayesian optimization and 15 for a knowledge-free agent with the same tools; on A100 GPUs, its representative configurations deliver ∼ 1.30 × vLLM-default throughput and meet every scenario’s latency objective. When reused, provenance-preserving evidence records over-generalize on only 8% of applicability traps, versus 28% for a general-purpose agent memory. On the held-out 48-task split of a 72-task benchmark, NoeVera scores 29% higher than an equally equipped agent and 36% higher than its single-call retrieval counterpart (0.80 vs. 0.62 and 0.59), and even when baselines receive NoeVera’s own prompts, a statistically significant part of its evidence-evolution advantage remains.

## 2 NOE VERA DESIGN 

Figure 1 illustrates NoeVera’s four stages: knowledge distil-lation into a persistent Second Brain, decision-aware navi-gation, agentic reasoning, and experimentation with results NoeVera: Can Knowledge Guide Experiments and Experiments Refine Knowledge? 

written back to the knowledge base. The design follows four goals: G1 , preserve original evidence while enabling traceable synthesis (§2.1); G2 , retrieve evidence for deci-sions rather than similarity alone (§2.2); G3 , use knowledge to decide when and what to simulate (§2.3); and G4 , make findings persistent and reusable while preserving conflicts and limiting conclusions to tested conditions (§2.4). 

2.1 An Evidence-Preserving Second Brain 

We represent the persistent knowledge state as Bt =(Et, St, Mt): source evidence, synthesized knowledge, and agent memory, which are logical planes rather than physi-cally separate stores. Distillation preserves source coverage, promotes knowledge selectively, and keeps every derived conclusion traceable to its evidence. Our knowledge base represents 2,627 source files (papers, documentation, repos-itories, benchmark reports, and experimental results), and organizes the derived knowledge into 2,912 wiki pages, and indexes it as 87,357 typed retrieval chunks. 

Evidence Plane. Each source has a source card, a source brief, and pointers back to its raw content, allowing reason-ing to recover conditions that summaries may omit, such as the workload, batch size, or memory limit under which a throughput gain was measured. The plane also carries authority signals: evidence strength, source role, corrobo-ration, freshness, and applicability. These signals are not interchangeable: a foundational paper may remain authorita-tive as it ages, while a recent measurement may be relevant but uncorroborated. Thus, freshness provides context rather than a rule that newer evidence takes precedence. Sources not yet suitable for synthesis remain available as anchor or deferred evidence than being forced into general claims. 

Synthesis Plane. Source observations are distilled into claims, concepts, techniques, systems, clusters, compar-isons, and decision matrices that connect otherwise separate sources, for instance observations about memory pressure and latency spread across prefix-caching, KV-cache, and scheduling papers. Synthesis is selective: only evidence that yields reusable understanding is promoted. Every derived object s retains a support set Support( s) ⊆ E t, non-empty for an evidence-backed claim, so decision matrices remain traceable syntheses rather than independently verified facts. 

Agent Memory Plane. Routing traces, ingestion deci-sions, review outcomes, benchmark results, and knowledge-utilization records capture how the system uses its knowl-edge. They show why an answer is unsupported (a missing source, failed retrieval, a dropped condition, or faulty rea-soning over adequate evidence) and thus where a fix belongs. This diagnostic memory is distinct from experimental find-ings, which enter as domain evidence (§2.4), and in every reported experiment it is excluded from retrieval at inference time: it is used for analysis, not for answering. 

2.2 Decision-Aware Navigation 

Textual relevance alone does not guarantee sufficient ev-idence: a passage may use the right terminology while describing a different workload or a superseded implemen-tation. NoeVera addresses this by combining complemen-tary retrieval signals with authority- and applicability-aware ranking, then assembling a compact evidence pack tailored to the decision at hand. 

Query profiling. The profile records query terms, exact technical phrases, configuration identifiers, and the intent: concept explanation, troubleshooting, method comparison, optimization decision, or quantitative what-if analysis. It also records the persona u, because an engineer diagnosing a regression and a product manager evaluating a rollout need different evidence; the persona shapes retrieval priorities, not only presentation. 

Hybrid retrieval across complementary signals. The can-didate set K(q, u ) is the union of results from a deliberately chosen set of complementary retrieval mechanisms: lexi-cal BM25 and exact technical matching, dense semantic retrieval, knowledge-graph expansion and navigation-hub retrieval, code-oriented retrieval, and fine-grained evidence-atom retrieval. The design principle is coverage, not count: exact configuration names and API identifiers require pre-cise lexical matching, conceptual questions require semantic similarity, cross-technique comparisons require graph and navigation access, code-level questions require repository-aware retrieval, and evidence-grounded reasoning requires atomic passages that survive packing. Combining these sig-nals lets K(q, u ) cover all of these query classes rather than biasing toward any one. 

Authority-aware ranking and evidence packs. Candi-dates are ranked by query relevance, metadata fit, persona compatibility, evidence strength, applicability, freshness, and lifecycle authority, combined as a normalized weighted sum whose weights are tuned on the development split and then fixed; material under review stays discoverable with reduced recommendation authority, and superseded material provides history rather than guidance. 2.3 Agentic Reasoning and Knowledge-Guided Experimentation 

Building on the retrieved evidence pack, NoeVera uses a controller to coordinate three roles: a scout that discovers evidence, an investigator that examines technical details and open questions, and a synthesizer that consolidates findings for the persona. Engineering responses emphasize mecha-nisms, workload assumptions, interventions, and validation steps; product (PM) responses emphasize benefits, trade-offs, risks, success metrics, and conditions for revisiting a decision. In both modes, the agents distinguish supported observations from inferred recommendations and preserve uncertainty rather than turning a plausible mechanism into an unsupported number. 

Deciding when and what to simulate. The controller de-cides at = SelectTool( q, u, P, Θ) : whether existing evi-dence suffices or a quantitative tool is needed, where Θ

is a set of candidate experimental settings. Crucially, Θ

need not come from the user: the investigator extracts, from the pack’s mechanisms and benchmark reports, parameter combinations reported as viable under the target workload’s prompt-length and concurrency profile, and filters them against the hardware’s memory budget. The controller in-vokes a simulator when the query profile, built from the query text alone before retrieval, marks the decision as need-ing a quantitative estimate and the pack contains no directly applicable estimate for the target workload and hardware. Two pathways supply quantitative evidence. Before any query, a systematic offline grid Dsim = {(wi, h i, c i, ˆyi)}

of simulator runs over relevant serving parameters enriches the knowledge base with estimates tied to their conditions. At query time, the agent runs a simulator on selected can-didates ct for the target (wt, h t), in parallel with its ongo-ing investigation, and interprets the estimates ˆyt alongside the retrieved mechanisms. Simulated estimates and hard-ware measurements are never conflated: an estimate inherits the simulator’s assumptions, whereas a measurement holds only for its runtime, workload, and hardware. Rather than solving a global optimization problem, NoeVera connects evidence-grounded reasoning to the selection of informative experiments. Vidur (Agrawal et al., 2024) is our primary on-demand backend for this work, but the simulator is swap-pable: §4.1 repeats the search with GAUSS (Bharadwaj et al., 2026), an analytical + Monte-Carlo simulator. 

2.4 Closing the Loop: Scoped, Reusable Evidence 

Every experimental finding is stored as an evidence record 

et = ( wt, h t, c t, tool t, yt, ϕ t), holding the workload, hard-ware, configuration, tool or method (e.g., a pinned Vidur commit), quantitative observations, and provenance, and is written back: Bt+1 = Update( Bt, e t). A record’s scope is explicit: one simulation supports a statement about that configuration, not a general claim, and it is promoted into synthesis only after its scope and evidential role are as-sessed. Findings that reinforce, qualify, or contradict earlier evidence are kept side by side with their conditions, so later reasoning sees a conflict instead of silently replacing one result with another. When a later query retrieves a record, an applicability check compares its workload and hardware (model family and size, precision, GPU SKU, and prompt/output length distribution, within fixed tolerances) and recorded assumptions with the query: a full match re-turns the record as applicable, a partial match returns it as historical context with an explicit caveat, and a mismatch rejects it. The loop Bt → Θt → et → B t+1 thus lets knowl-edge select experiments whose results become evidence for later decisions. 

Example. Consider the Llama-3-70B optimization sce-nario introduced in §1: BF16 with TP=4 on a 4 ×A100-80GB node, 512/128-token requests at 8 requests/s, and TTFT p95 ≤ 400 ms. NoeVera profiles the query as a quanti-tative what-if with Mh = 76 GB and assembles a 14-item, 2,480-token evidence pack (including two measured bench-marks). Based on the retrieved evidence, it proposes three configurations (chunk size, prefix caching, maximum se-quences): (512, on, 128), (1024, on, 96), and (256, off, 192). Three Vidur calls estimate capacities of 1,180, 1,290, and 1,050 generated tokens/s, respectively, with TTFT p95 of 388, 402, and 362 ms at the offered load of 1,024 tokens/s. NoeV-era recommends the first configuration, the highest-capacity candidate satisfying the SLO. It states the assumptions (the 512/128 workload holds and the prefix-cache hit rate is at least 0.3), proposes a vLLM A/B test, and writes back the finding. The value of this write-back becomes apparent in a later query. At query 17 of the §4.3 stream, a user reports de-livered throughput of 920 tokens/s at the same offered load and asks whether the stored 1,180-token/s estimate indicates unrealized headroom or an underperforming deployment. NoeVera finds that the model, GPU, and workload match, but the assumed cache hit rate remains unverified. It there-fore treats the finding as partially applicable, surfaces the estimate with this caveat, and requests the current hit rate, which would save three Vidur calls once confirmed (full trace in Appendix E.7).

Systems. We compare against an LLM-only refer-ence; Dense RAG (dense top-k retrieval over the raw files); Hybrid RAG; GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2025), Hip-poRAG 2 (Guti´ errez et al., 2025b), and LightRAG (Guo et al., 2025) with their authors’ defaults; PaperQA2 (Skar-linski et al., 2024), an agentic literature-synthesis system evaluated on the navigation track; and ReAct, a generic ReAct-style agent (Yao et al., 2023) with hybrid search and a document reader. Hybrid RAG uses NoeVera’s hybrid retrieval and authority- and lifecycle-aware ranking over the raw files and the Evidence Plane’s source representa-tions and authority metadata, but excludes the Synthesis and Agent Memory planes and operates in a single call. It therefore shares NoeVera’s retrieval substrate, but neither its synthesized knowledge nor its orchestration. The suffix +V adds Vidur through one shared tool interface and its doc-umentation: ReAct+V is the baseline most comparable to NoeVera+V, OpenHands+V gives OpenHands (Wang et al., 2025) the raw file tree, grep , and the Vidur command-line tool, and LLM-only+V and Hybrid RAG+V separate the simulator’s value from that of knowledge (configurations in Appendix A.2). NoeVera+G uses GAUSS (Bharadwaj et al., 2026) as the on-demand backend instead (§4.1 only). We include methods that operate over a user-supplied corpus, are open or faithfully re-implementable, answer research questions, and run on our backbone; Glia, the AI Scien-tist (Lu et al., 2024; Yamada et al., 2025), and AlphaE-volve (Novikov et al., 2025) produce algorithms or papers judged by other criteria, and Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023) requires its own fine-tuned generator (Appendix A.1). 

Shared configuration. All systems use GPT-5.5 (temper-ature 0.2, at most 4,096 output tokens per call), the same 2,627-file corpus snapshot, task instructions, and persona, at most 8 tool steps per turn, an 8,000-token evidence budget where context is assembled, and a 3-minute limit per NoeV-eraBench task; C1 search runs instead allow up to k = 30 

simulator calls and 20 minutes, without the step or 3-minute limits. Structured-RAG indices use GPT-5.5-mini for extrac-tion and text-embedding-3-large for embeddings. All systems were frozen before the held-out split was unsealed. NoeVeraBench. The benchmark has 72 tasks in three tracks.

Decision-and-Experiment

Prior knowledge differs. Only the NoeVera variants can consult the offline Vidur grid, so this is a full-system com-parison in which accumulated evidence (including grid es-timates that may already cover these scenarios) and the search policy contribute jointly; removing the grid raises NoeVera+V’s calls per benchmark query from 1.6 to 3.4 (Appendix E.5), and a matched-prior experiment is future work. 

Results. NoeVera+V comes within 5% of the optimum in a median of 6 calls (IQR 4–9), versus 9 for BO, 11 for the Vidur-Search-style procedure, 12 for random search, 15 for ReAct+V, and 20 for grid search (Table 1a). It has the low-est median in every scenario (consistency, not significance, with four scenarios), finds a feasible configuration within 10 calls in 19 of 20 runs, and has the lowest conditional regret at k = 10 . On hardware (Table 1b), its configurations are the fastest in every scenario (1.29–1.34 × the vLLM default; mean 1.31 ×, versus 1.34 × for the exhaustive optimum) with the lowest TTFT. The TTFT constraint binds only in Sc1 ( τ = 300 ms), where NoeVera+V, NoeVera+G, and BO meet it and the default, random, grid, Vidur-Search-style, and ReAct+V configurations do not; elsewhere TTFTs clear the SLOs by 98–858 ms, so both NoeVera variants meet the SLO on hardware in all four scenarios. Within each sce-nario, Vidur’s predictions for the six Vidur-based methods’ configurations rank-correlate with measured throughput at a median Spearman ρ of 0.83 (range 0.77–0.94; pooled 0.87, inflated by the 8B–70B gap). Vidur understates absolute gaps, and unevenly: it places the default 8.7% below the optimum versus 25% measured (plausibly operator-profiler calibration), and the baselines’ measured gaps (9–16%) are three to four times their simulated regrets at k = 10 (2.8– 4.2%), whereas NoeVera+V’s are close (2.2% vs. 1.9%). We therefore use the simulator only to rank configurations and base all hardware claims on vLLM measurements. 

Swapping the on-demand simulator. NoeVera+G replaces the on-demand Vidur with GAUSS (Bharadwaj et al., 2026) but keeps the offline grid, and its configurations are scored by Vidur against the same optimum, so only the on-demand estimator changes. It needs one more median call than No-eVera+V in every scenario (7 [5, 11] vs. 6 [4, 9]) but fewer than every baseline, all of which search with Vidur. GAUSS rank-correlates slightly less well with measured throughput (median ρ 0.80, range 0.71–0.89), yet NoeVera+G’s con-figurations reach 1.30 × the vLLM default (vs. 1.31 ×) and meet all four SLOs with TTFT 4 ms higher (Appendix B). 

4.2 C1 Diagnostics: When to Simulate and What It Adds 

A reliable agent should also know when experimentation is unnecessary. On a separate 48-task invocation set dis-joint from NoeVeraBench (12 experiment-opportunity tasks and 36 no-call controls whose answers need no simulation), we compare never-call, always-call, and selective policies (Table 2). Per seed, NoeVera+V’s selective policy called Vidur on 13 tasks on average, 10 of which needed a call, taking 10 of the 12 opportunities, with 0.08 unnecessary calls per control (3 controls received one each); it retains 97% of always-call quality on opportunity tasks at 27% of the simulator time. Always calling degrades both systems’ no-call-control quality (0.68 →0.62), whereas selective invo-cation stays near the no-call baseline (0.67 for NoeVera+V, 0.68 for ReAct+V): unnecessary calls hurt, and selectivity prevents them. NoeVera’s controller also decides better than ReAct+V’s: higher recall (0.83 vs. 0.72), precision (0.77 vs. 0.63), and Qopp (0.72 vs. 0.63), with fewer unnecessary calls (0.08 vs. 0.14). 

What the simulator adds. Without a simulator, no system exceeds 0.48 on the diagnostic D track (Table 4; per-intent scores in Appendix E.2), and method comparison, a compar-ative rather than quantitative intent, is the only one on which retrieval-based systems exceed 0.6. Adding the same Vidur interface lifts every system, but the lift tracks agentic orches-tration more than retrieval: +0 .15 for LLM-only, +0 .13 for Hybrid RAG, +0 .19 for ReAct, and +0 .25 for NoeVera, whose advantage over ReAct+V is largest on experiment design ( +0 .12 ) and optimization ( +0 .08 ). 4.3 C2: Safe Reuse of Persistent Experimental Feedback Setup. From a fixed snapshot Bt, each of 8 seed questions triggers a Vidur investigation whose finding is written back (§2.4). whose GPU, model size, or workload invalidates it ( Ftrap ), 24 on which it conflicts with an existing source obtained under different conditions ( Fconf ), and 24 unrelated controls (Fctrl ). To isolate storage format, reasoning is fixed to No-eVera’s orchestration while the finding is stored as a plain chunk in the Hybrid RAG index, a Mem0 (Chhikara et al., 2025) memory, or an evidence record; the “ReAct+chunk” column of Table 3 is an end-to-end reference in which Re-Act reads a plain-chunk finding through its own loop (its no-finding score is 0.68, so +0 .02 is the gain from the find-ing). 4.7 Cost and Robustness 

NoeVera+V uses 78k tokens and costs $0.35 per query (3.5 × Hybrid RAG’s cost, 1.1 × ReAct+V’s), with a median latency of 71 s (p95 118 s), 31% of it in Vidur running in parallel with the investigation. A compute-matched Hybrid RAG with the same 78k tokens (best-of-3 self-consistency and a larger retrieval budget) reaches only 0.63, so the gain is not explained by extra LLM computation, although match-ing tokens does not equalize simulator time or offline com-pute. ## 5 RELATED WORK 

Retrieval, knowledge graphs, and agent memory. Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023) and Corrective RAG (Yan et al., 2024) make retrieval adaptive and self-correcting; RAP-TOR (Sarthi et al., 2024) retrieves over hierarchical sum-maries; GraphRAG (Edge et al., 2025), LightRAG (Guo et al., 2025), and HippoRAG (Guti´ errez et al., 2025a;b) add entity graphs, dual-level retrieval, and associative recall; and AgenticRAG (Suresh et al., 2026) gives a reasoning model iterative search and evidence-analysis tools. Generative Agents (Park et al., 2023), MemGPT (Packer et al., 2024), Mem0 (Chhikara et al., 2025), and A-MEM (Xu et al., 2025) let agents accumulate memories beyond a context window. NoeVera builds on these mechanisms but targets decision-ready technical evidence: it separates sources from synthesis and usage records, ranks by authority, applicability, and life-cycle, and stores experimental findings as scoped evidence rather than conversational memory, the distinction behind its over-generalization gap against Mem0 (§4.3). Unlike retrieval systems focused primarily on finding relevant in-formation, NoeVera also tracks the conditions under which experimental evidence remains applicable to subsequent decisions. 

Agents for research and systems optimization. Pa-perQA2 (Skarlinski et al., 2024) and OpenScholar (Asai et al., 2026) synthesize scientific literature with citation-grounded generation; the AI Scientist (Lu et al., 2024; Ya-mada et al., 2025) automates the path from idea genera-tion to manuscript; AstaBench (Bragg et al., 2026) bench-marks research agents; AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025) evolves programs against automated evaluators; and Kernel-Bench (Ouyang et al., 2025) tests GPU-kernel generation. Closest to our work, Glia (Hamadanian et al., 2026) coordi-nates Researcher and Supervisor agents that form hypothe-ses, run experiments, and refine system designs, showing that reasoning-guided experimentation can outperform less structured search. Glia asks whether agents can discover bet-ter mechanisms; NoeVera asks how agents can accumulate, navigate, and reuse evidence across questions. The two are complementary, as a Second Brain could supply reusable knowledge to an autonomous design agent, and we make no claim to discover better algorithms. NoeVera further closes the knowledge–experiment loop by using retrieved evidence to guide simulator calls and writing back their results for subsequent reasoning. 

LLM inference simulation. Vidur (Agrawal et al., 2024) combines operator profiling with predictive modeling and, through Vidur-Search, automates deployment-configuration search; LLMServingSim (Cho et al., 2024; 2026) co-simulates hardware and software for homogeneous, hetero-geneous, and disaggregated serving; and GAUSS (Bharad-waj et al., 2026) trades Vidur’s operator profiling for an analytical + Monte-Carlo approach with lower per-call cost. NoeVera introduces no new simulation method and treats the simulator as a swappable component. Its contribution lies in selecting which configurations warrant simulation us-ing accumulated technical evidence, rather than improving the simulator’s predictive model. 

## 6 DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS 

Lessons. Our evaluation yields three broader lessons for knowledge-guided research systems. First, knowledge orga-nization is a first-class design choice: separating evidence from synthesis lets agents navigate compact representations without losing the conditions behind a conclusion, while scoped evidence records preserve applicability conditions to prevent written-back findings from being over-generalized (§4.3). Second, relevant evidence alone is insufficient for decision-making: orchestration helps most when evidence must be weighed and revised, but structure can’t substitute for quantitative measurement, as no system exceeded 0.48 on track D without a simulator. Knowing when to exper-iment is therefore distinct from knowing what is already known. Unnecessary experiments also carries a cost: the +V baselines perform slightly worse on N and E (§4.4), and always invoking the simulator reduces no-call-control qual-ity from 0.68 to 0.62 (Table 2). Third, experiments should extend knowledge rather than end as isolated tool calls: per-sisting findings with their applicability conditions reduces subsequent simulation without degrading answer quality. 

Where NoeVera does not help, and limitations. A dedi-cated literature-synthesis agent matches NoeVera on Knowl-edge Navigation (PaperQA2: 0.87 versus 0.86); without a simulator, NoeVera is no better than plain retrieval or a generic agent on the D track (0.48); and it costs 3.5 ×

as much per query as single-call RAG and needs an of-fline build, a trade-off most favorable when related ques-tions recur (§4.3). In configuration search, only the NoeV-era variants have the offline grid, so their gains combine prior evidence and search policy; separating them requires a matched-prior variant. Four scenarios support consistency but not significance for C1, and the C2 test rests on 8 parent investigations. The backend swap tests sensitivity to the on-demand estimator, not independence from Vidur, since NoeVera: Can Knowledge Guide Experiments and Experiments Refine Knowledge? 

NoeVera+G keeps the Vidur-built grid and Vidur’s optimum as its target. Simulator estimates inherit their modeling assumptions (Vidur’s understate absolute gaps, unevenly), and provenance is not correctness: a traced source can still be wrong, and persistence alone does not guarantee that the right evidence is retrieved later. Also, NoeVeraBench’s 72 tasks are a bounded sample of LLM-systems research, and performance remains sensitive to the backbone model. Finally, our implementation targets LLM serving, where structured evidence and performance models are readily available; other domains may need different representations, authority criteria, and tools, with benefits weighed against offline and online costs. 

## 7 CONCLUSION 

NoeVera treats knowledge in LLM-serving research as a persistent, provenance-preserving system rather than a col-lection of documents retrieved anew for each question. By separating evidence, synthesis, and agent memory, navigat-ing for decisions rather than similarity alone, and writing scoped experimental findings back into the knowledge base, NoeVera reaches strong configurations with fewer simulator calls (with Vidur or GAUSS), reuses past findings without over-generalizing them, and revises decisions when evi-dence changes. We see this evidence-to-decision loop as a foundation for research agents that understand what is known, what remains uncertain, and what is worth investi-gating next.

## A SYSTEMS AND EXPERIMENTAL CONFIGURATION 

A.1 Inclusion Criteria 

We benchmark a method if it (i) operates over a user-supplied corpus, (ii) is open source or faithfully re-implementable, (iii) produces the same kind of output as NoeVera (answers to research questions and decisions), and (iv) can run on our backbone. Glia (Hamadanian et al., 2026), the AI Scientist (Lu et al., 2024; Yamada et al., 2025), and AlphaEvolve (Novikov et al., 2025) produce algorithms or papers judged by different criteria and so fail (iii); the configuration-search experiment (§4.1) instead compares NoeVera with optimization-style search. Vidur (Agrawal et al., 2024), GAUSS (Bharadwaj et al., 2026), and LLMServingSim (Cho et al., 2024) are quantitative tools rather than competing systems, and a Vidur-Search-style procedure appears as a search baseline. Self-RAG (Asai et al., 2023) requires its own fine-tuned generator and fails (iv). Commercial deep-research products search the open web rather than our corpus, cannot call a simulator, and change over time, so they fail (i) and (ii); we do not compare against them.

C.2 Provenance and Evaluation Splits 

Of the 72 tasks, 38 were written by domain experts, 12 were adapted from anonymized NoeVera usage patterns, and 22 are controlled evidence-update scenarios. Four systems researchers reviewed every task for technical validity, ambiguity, realism, and information leakage, and annotated each with expected conclusions, acceptable alternatives, critical errors, required evidence, and scoring anchors. The 24-task development split (8 per track) was used to develop and tune every system, baselines included; it comprises 14 validation tasks and 10 worked examples, with associated task templates, rubric schemas, and evaluation procedures. The 48-task held-out split (17 N, 17 D, 14 E) approximately preserves each track’s share and has two-stage provenance: 33 tasks were written and sealed after review but before any system was frozen, and 15 were written by three researchers outside the team after all systems were frozen. All 48 tasks were evaluated in a single post-freeze campaign with three seeds. In two-turn tasks, turn-2 evidence is withheld until turn 2, and every system receives the same turn-1 input and its own turn-1 response as history. On the N track’s 6 calibration tasks (§3), confident but unsupported answers score below appropriately qualified ones; per-task results for these and the 8 literature-synthesis tasks are in the evaluation records. 

C.3 Grading, Length Control, and Reliability 

Two blinded LLM judges from different model families score every response against its task’s anchors; disagreements above a fixed threshold are adjudicated by a third grader. Scores are normalized to [0 , 1] , and a track score is the mean over that NoeVera: Can Knowledge Guide Experiments and Experiments Refine Knowledge? 

track’s tasks. Rubrics credit correct, appropriately qualified decisions rather than the use of particular tools; an audit removed six rubric items that rewarded tool invocation rather than correct evidence use. The primary scores are length-controlled: for each system, a linear mixed-effects model of rubric score on log response length, with a random intercept per task, yields an adjusted mean evaluated at a common reference length of 1,240 words. The adjustment is below 0.01, raises NoeVera’s scores by less than 0.01, and changes no ranking; unadjusted scores were retained in the evaluation records. Two systems researchers independently graded a stratified 12.5% sample of held-out split responses (36 responses, 18 from each of the two systems compared: NoeVera+V and ReAct+V).
