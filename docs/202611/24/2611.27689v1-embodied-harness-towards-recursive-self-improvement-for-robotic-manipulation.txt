Abstract
Vision-language model (VLM)-based agents offer a promising approach to robotic manipulation, yet remain
limited by inadequate memory organization, uncertain failure attribution, and limited reuse of execution
experience. We propose Embodied-Harness, a dual-agent framework that improves manipulation by refining
the execution harness without updating model parameters.
The Actor interacts with the environment
through a robot-control interface, while the Reflector converts execution evidence into reusable operational
guidance. Hierarchical Memory Architecture maintains short-term execution context and long-term experiential
knowledge to support execution and reflection. Evidence-based Experience Reflection grounds failure diagnosis
in observed interactions, while Recursive Harness Self-Improvement refines operational guidance across
successive attempts. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboSPA, and RMBench demonstrate the effectiveness of
Embodied-Harness in generalization under environmental variations, spatial reasoning, long-horizon planning,
and memory-dependent manipulation. Our method achieves success rates of 82.4%, 76.7%, and 94.4% on the
three benchmarks, respectively. Real-world evaluation further yields an average success rate of 96.7% across
three manipulation tasks. These results highlight the potential of Embodied-Harness to improve robotic
manipulation through experience-driven harness self-improvement.

Introduction
Robotic manipulation is a fundamental capability of embodied intelligence [1–3], enabling robots to interact with
the physical world and perform diverse tasks across household assistance [4, 5], industrial manufacturing [6, 7],
and healthcare [8, 9]. Developing general manipulation capabilities is essential for enabling versatile and
autonomous robotic operation in real-world environments, representing a critical step toward general embodied
intelligence (GEI) [10–12].
Towards this goal, different paradigms have emerged for robotic manipulation. End-to-end frameworks,
including vision-language-action (VLA) models [13–19] and world-action models (WAMs) [20–25], have
demonstrated remarkable progress in learning robotic policies from multimodal inputs. However, these
approaches often rely on large-scale robotic datasets, face challenges in generalizing to unseen environments [26–
28], and struggle with reliable long-horizon manipulation [29–31]. In contrast, agentic frameworks leverage
the reasoning and planning capabilities of vision-language models (VLMs) to decompose complex tasks and
coordinate robot actions [32–35]. Such frameworks support flexible action planning and feedback-driven
execution, offering a promising alternative without requiring task-specific updates to the underlying model
parameters [36–38].
Despite these advantages, agentic frameworks still face several challenges in achieving reliable robotic
manipulation. (i) Inadequate Memory Organization. Many real-world manipulation tasks are inherently long-
horizon, requiring agents to track task progress and detailed execution histories while retaining experience
across attempts and tasks [39–42]. Managing such information across different temporal scales and levels
of granularity remains challenging. (ii) Uncertain Failure Attribution. Manipulation failures may stem from
inaccurate action grounding, unsatisfied preconditions, or violated state constraints [43–45].
However,
execution feedback often reveals only observable outcomes rather than underlying causes, making it difficult
to identify critical errors and derive reliable operational guidance. (iii) Limited Self-Improvement Capability.
Even when agents identify execution errors and extract useful insights, these reflections may remain isolated
from subsequent executions. Without mechanisms to accumulate, validate, and refine operational guidance
across attempts, agents may repeatedly encounter similar failures [46–48].
To address these challenges, we propose Embodied-Harness, a dual-agent framework for robotic manipulation
that enables recursive harness self-improvement through accumulated execution experience (Fig. 1). The
framework comprises two complementary roles, an Actor and a Reflector. The Actor interacts with the
environment through control interfaces, while the Reflector analyzes execution trajectories and environmental
feedback to improve subsequent task execution. Specifically, Embodied-Harness incorporates three com-
plementary mechanisms: (i) Hierarchical Memory Architecture organizes short-term execution context and
long-term experiential knowledge across different temporal scales; (ii) Evidence-based Experience Reflection
identifies potential failure causes from execution evidence and derives actionable operational guidance; and (iii)
Recursive Harness Self-Improvement refines the execution harness using accumulated guidance, with subsequent
execution feedback informing further reflection and refinement. Together, these mechanisms enable continual
improvement in robotic manipulation without updating the underlying model parameters.
We evaluate Embodied-Harness on three robotic manipulation benchmarks, RoboTwin 2.0, RoboSPA, and
RMBench, covering generalization under environmental variations, fine-grained spatial reasoning, long-horizon
procedural planning, and memory-dependent manipulation. Embodied-Harness outperforms baselines spanning
VLA models, WAMs, and agentic frameworks, achieving average success rates of 82.4%, 76.7%, and 94.4%
on the three benchmarks, respectively. Real-world evaluation further yields an average success rate of 96.7%
across three manipulation tasks. Ablation studies and further analyses support the effectiveness of the
proposed components and illustrate how accumulated experience enables more reliable manipulation.
Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• We propose Embodied-Harness, an experience-driven dual-agent framework that improves robotic
manipulation through complementary execution and reflection without updating model parameters.
• We design a Hierarchical Memory Architecture that integrates short-term working memory and long-term
experiential memory to support coherent task execution and effective experience reuse.

• We introduce Evidence-based Experience Reflection and Recursive Harness Self-Improvement to derive
actionable guidance from execution feedback and refine the harness across successive attempts.
• We conduct extensive experiments on three robotic manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks,
demonstrating the effectiveness of Embodied-Harness across diverse manipulation settings.

Related Work
2.1
End-to-End Models for Robotic Manipulation
End-to-end policies map visual observations and language instructions directly to robot actions. Vision-
language-action (VLA) models build this mapping on large-scale robot data and pretrained vision-language
backbones [13, 14, 49, 50], with later works adopting diffusion or flow-matching action generators [15, 16, 51–
54] and extending them with cross-embodiment training, efficiency, or visual reasoning [17–19, 55–58].
World action models (WAMs) instead exploit video generative priors, either guiding actions with predicted
futures [20, 59, 60] or jointly modeling video and actions [21–25, 61]. Despite their progress, these models
typically require large-scale robotic training data and remain limited in generalizing to unseen environments
and task variations.
2.2
Agentic Frameworks for Robotic Manipulation
Agentic frameworks connect foundation models to robots through perception, planning, and control in-
terfaces. Language models sequence skills, generate plans or programs, and incorporate environmental
feedback [32, 33, 36, 62, 63], while embodied VLMs predict plans, affordances, and spatial targets [12, 64–67].
Other methods translate VLM reasoning into value maps, keypoints, or geometric constraints for motion
generation [34, 37, 68–71], or pair high-level reasoners with low-level policies [15, 35, 72–75]. Closest to our
setting, RoboDawn [76] controls robots through VLM-issued commands, while Harness VLA [38] combines
analytic primitives with a frozen VLA through memory-guided planning. Embodied-Harness advances these
frameworks through coordinated execution and reflection, enabling evidence-based failure diagnosis, experience
reuse, and progressive refinement of execution strategies across attempts.
2.3
Reflection and Self-Improvement in Agents
Reflection lets agents improve from feedback without updating model parameters. Language agents reason
while acting [77], revise their own outputs [78], or store verbal reflections across trials [46], while others distill
experience into insights, memories, skills, or rules [39, 40, 47, 48, 79]. Prompt and agent-design optimization
further improves systems built on fixed models [80–84]. In robotics, prior work explains, detects, or corrects
manipulation failures [43–45], and SenseNova-RoboRSI [74] revises the agent harness and retains changes
validated by re-evaluation. Embodied-Harness emphasizes the link between failure diagnosis and corrective
guidance, using observed execution evidence to identify potential failure causes and specify actionable execution
conditions for subsequent robotic manipulation attempts.

Self-Improvement to enable progressive improvement without updating the underlying model parameters.

3.2
Hierarchical Memory Architecture
Robotic manipulation requires maintaining execution context within individual episodes while preserving
reusable knowledge across attempts and tasks. To support these different temporal scales and information
granularities, we introduce a Hierarchical Memory Architecture comprising Short-Term Working Memory and
Long-Term Experiential Memory.
Short-Term Working Memory. Short-Term Working Memory supports the Actor’s decision-making within
a single execution episode by continuously maintaining task progress and execution context. It comprises
Condensed Progress Memory and Detailed Execution Memory, which capture high-level task states and
fine-grained interaction histories, respectively.
Condensed Progress Memory. Long-horizon manipulation involves interdependent subgoals whose progress is
often scattered across interaction histories, making it difficult to maintain coherent task states. To address this,
Condensed Progress Memory consolidates completed subgoals, pending operations, environmental changes,
and execution constraints into a compact representation:

3.3
Evidence-based Experience Reflection
Robotic manipulation failures may arise from different underlying causes, while similar task outcomes often
provide limited evidence for identifying execution errors. Moreover, unconstrained reflection may produce
explanations unsupported by observed interactions. To address these challenges, we introduce Evidence-based
Experience Reflection, which grounds failure diagnosis in concrete execution evidence and accumulated
experience.
After each execution attempt, the Reflector analyzes the task instruction g, trajectory τk, task outcome rk,
current harness Hk, and experiential memory Ek:
dk = Rϕ(g, τk, rk, Hk, Ek),
(9)
where Rϕ denotes the frozen Reflector model and dk represents the execution diagnosis.
Each reflection outcome is assigned to exactly one of the following five operational categories:
• Contact & Pose Control: Examines errors in physical interactions, including grasp configurations, contact
positions, gripper operations, and object orientations.
• Motion & Coordination: Focuses on improper action sequencing, arm coordination, and motion planning
that may lead to unsuccessful or unsafe manipulation.
• Task Protocol & Completion: Captures failures to satisfy task requirements, execution restrictions, success
conditions, or termination criteria.
• Spatial Grounding: Addresses incorrect target identification, spatial relationship interpretation, and
coordinate grounding that result in actions at unintended locations.
• State Tracking & Recovery: Identifies deficiencies in tracking task progress, verifying action outcomes,
interpreting feedback, and recovering from execution failures.
The Reflector identifies discrepancies between intended and observed outcomes to produce evidence-grounded
diagnoses for subsequent harness self-improvement.
3.4
Recursive Harness Self-Improvement
Following evidence-based reflection, the framework converts execution diagnosis into reusable corrective
guidance and refines the execution harness.
Given the diagnosis dk produced by the Reflector, the framework identifies operations requiring adjustment
and specifies their execution conditions, conditioned on the current harness and accumulated experience:
∆Gk = Generate(dk, Hk, Ek),
(10)
where ∆Gk denotes candidate guidance that translates identified execution issues into actionable instructions
for subsequent attempts.
The candidate guidance is integrated with existing operational knowledge to update the harness:
Hk+1 = Update(Hk, ∆Gk, Ek).
(11)
The update uses accumulated experience to retain effective guidance, resolve inconsistencies, and refine
instructions based on execution evidence. The refined harness Hk+1 guides subsequent Actor attempts, whose
trajectories and feedback support further reflection. This cycle of execution, diagnosis, guidance generation,
and harness refinement enables progressive improvement without updating model parameters.

5
Conclusion
We presented Embodied-Harness, a dual-agent framework that converts execution experience into reusable
operational guidance for robotic manipulation without updating model parameters. Hierarchical memory
supports context-aware execution by the Actor and evidence-based reflection by the Reflector, enabling
iterative harness improvement. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, RoboSPA, RMBench, and real-world tasks
demonstrate its effectiveness, while ablations and iterative analyses substantiate the roles of memory, reflection,
and evidence grounding. These findings establish harness refinement as a promising direction for improving
embodied agents. Future work will investigate experience transfer across tasks and embodiments and more
efficient execution and reflection.
