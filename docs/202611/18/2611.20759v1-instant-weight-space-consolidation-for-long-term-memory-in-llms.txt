## Instant Weight-Space Consolidation for Long-Term Memory in LLMs 

Manan Suri, Dinesh Manocha 

University of Maryland, College Park 

{manans, dmanocha}@umd.edu 

Abstract 

Agentic memory systems give LLMs persistent memory by extracting experience into text and retrieving it at query time, but the model itself doesn’t evolve: however many interactions it has had, it starts each query the same. Extrac-tion errors compound, implicit cross-session patterns that no single fragment states cannot be surfaced, and the construction cost grows with every conversation. Fine-tuning on past in-teractions does change the weights, but requires backpropagation at serving time and degrades on long histories. We introduce CLAM (Car-ried Latent Adapter Memory), which combines the strengths of both: a recurrent hypernetwork performs parametric consolidation , compiling a stream of session activations into a low-rank adapter in a single forward pass with no gra-dient steps and no language-model generation calls, while a complementary episodic buffer 

supplies verbatim detail at inference. A gated latent state carries knowledge across sessions in fixed size, so the parametric memory accu-mulates without growing. On LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S across three open models (2– 7 B parameters), CLAM leads all agentic base-lines by 1–45 points and all gradient-based parametric methods by 10–45 points, while re-quiring zero generative LLM calls, zero gra-dient steps, and 3–151 × less wall-clock time than extraction systems to build its memory. The two channels are complementary: the con-solidated adapter adds 12–15 accuracy points when the episodic buffer misses the gold evi-dence, and still adds 4–5 points even with an oracle buffer. 

1 Introduction 

Biological memory is not a database that an organ-ism queries; it is a multi-level process in which two complementary systems cooperate. A fast episodic store, seated in the hippocampus, captures specific experiences rapidly and without interfer-ence. A slow semantic store, residing in neocortical synapses, integrates those episodes over time into structured, persistent knowledge (Tulving, 1972; Squire, 1992). The bridge between them is con-solidation : transient experience becomes a durable change in synaptic weights, and after consolidation the organism is physically different. Complemen-tary Learning Systems (CLS) theory formalizes why both stores are necessary: the fast store gives exact recall of specific episodes without overwrit-ing prior knowledge, the slow store gives general-ization and cross-episode integration, and neither alone suffices (McClelland et al., 1995; Kumaran et al., 2016). They are complementary precisely because they trade off different failure modes: the fast store is lossless but unintegrated, the slow store is integrated but lossy. A system that has only one operates with a fundamental blind spot. Contemporary LLM memory systems are so-phisticated episodic stores. Agentic frameworks extract facts and summaries from conversations, organize them as profiles or knowledge graphs, and retrieve relevant fragments into the context window at query time (Chhikara et al., 2025; Rasmussen et al., 2025; Packer et al., 2023; Xu et al., 2025; Park et al., 2023; Zhong et al., 2024; Liu et al., 2026a). This is effective when the needed infor-mation was explicitly stated, correctly extracted, and successfully retrieved, but each step introduces error that compounds across the pipeline: extrac-tion misses implicit facts, indexing loses context, and retrieval surfaces the wrong fragment for the query at hand. More fundamentally, the model’s weights never change. Implicit patterns that no single fragment states, cross-session structure that requires integration, and preferences that accumu-late gradually over many interactions have no path-way into the model. In CLS terms, these systems have a sophisticated hippocampus and no neocor-tex: no consolidation pathway turns experience into weight-resident knowledge. The natural remedy is to write experience into 1arXiv:2611.20759v1 [cs.CL] 16 Nov 2026 the weights directly. A nascent line of work does so by fine-tuning at test time: generating QA pairs from the conversation and training on them with SGD (Ren et al., 2026), or adapting on unlabelled context with an entropy gate (Hu et al., 2025). This does produce a parametric change, but it requires backpropagation at serving time, which is opera-tionally costly and slow. More fundamentally, as our experiments show, gradient based consolida-tion degrades on long histories: the gradient signal becomes noisy, the model overfits early sessions, and performance drops as the history grows. It is not the stable, accumulating consolidation that long-term memory requires. We take a different approach that combines the strengths of both paradigms. A hypernetwork, trained once offline, can map session activations to adapter weights in a single forward pass: the persistence of a weight change at the speed of infer-ence, with no gradient step at test time. We build CLAM (Carried Latent Adapter Memory) on this observation. A recurrent hypernetwork performs 

parametric consolidation : it ingests each session’s activations from the frozen base LLM and folds them into a compact latent state through a learned, gated recurrence; a single forward pass over the accumulated state emits the low-rank adapter the model wears while answering. A complementary 

episodic buffer supplies verbatim detail at infer-ence for what a bounded parametric state cannot retain with perfect fidelity. The parametric consol-idation channel plays the role of slow, integrated knowledge; the episodic buffer plays the role of fast, lossless recall; and we find they are comple-mentary. 

Main results. On LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S across three open base models (Gemma-2-2B (Gemma Team et al., 2024), Qwen3-4B (Yang et al., 2025), Mistral-7B (Jiang et al., 2023)), CLAM leads all agentic baselines by 1–45 points and all gradient-based parametric methods by 10– 45 points, with 95% bootstrap confidence inter-vals that exclude zero in 28 of 30 paired compar-isons. It does this with zero generative language-model calls, zero gradient steps, and zero gener-ated tokens to build its memory: one hypernet-work forward pass per conversation, 3–151 × faster than extraction systems that spend 12–464 gen-erative LLM calls and 3×10 4 to 6.2×10 5 gener-ated tokens on the same conversation, and 1.3–16 ×

faster than gradient-based methods that take 18–63 backward passes. The two channels validate the complementary-systems design: adding parametric consolidation on top of the same episodic buffer is worth +7 to +11 points overall; the adapter con-tributes +12 to +15 points on the 36% of questions where the buffer fails to surface the gold evidence; and it still adds +4 to +5 points even when the buffer is given oracle access to the gold spans, con-firming that the consolidated weights carry infor-mation the episodic channel cannot supply rather than a better-indexed copy of it. Contributions. 

• CLAM : a recurrent hypernetwork that per-forms parametric consolidation of interaction sessions into a low-rank adapter in a single forward pass with a carried latent state, paired with an episodic buffer in a dual-channel archi-tecture inspired by complementary learning systems. • A scalable multi-session data pipeline ,used to generate up to ∼900K high-quality, memory-dependent training trajectories. • Rigorous evaluation across two benchmarks, three model scales, and twelve baselines span-ning both agentic and parametric paradigms. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Memory Systems for LLMs 

The dominant paradigm stores interaction history externally and retrieves it at query time. Flat retrieval indexes raw conversation chunks and prepends the top matches (Lewis et al., 2020; Guu et al., 2020). Profile-plus-episodic sys-tems additionally extract structured user facts (Chhikara et al., 2025). Graph-based systems or-ganize memory as a (temporal) knowledge graph for multi-hop traversal (Rasmussen et al., 2025; Jiménez Gutiérrez et al., 2024). Agentic archi-tectures manage memory with explicit read/write tools: MemGPT/Letta treats context like paged virtual memory (Packer et al., 2023), and Gener-ative Agents periodically reflect episodic obser-vations into higher-level summaries (Park et al., 2023). Others add forgetting dynamics (Zhong et al., 2024), compression (Fang et al., 2025), self-organizing links (Xu et al., 2025), or intent-aware retrieval over compressed memory units (Liu et al., 2026a). Despite their diversity, these systems share one property: the base model’s parameters never

They are thus non-parametric; most require per-item LLM extraction, so memory is not built instantly; none accumulate in a bounded parametric state that improves with more sessions (their stores grow without bound); and knowledge is not persistent across queries without re-reading history. CLAM is parametric, instant, accumulating, and persistent at once, which motivates our design. A nascent line does modify weights to consolidate memory over time (Behrouz et al., 2026); CLAM shares this goal but writes memory through a single hyper-network forward pass rather than gradient-based self-modification, and pairs it with episodic recall. 

2.2 Hypernetworks and Context Distillation 

CLAM belongs to a growing line of work that 

generates adapter weights with a hypernetwork (Ha et al., 2017), paired with context distillation, which trains a model to behave as if a context were present without reading it (Snell et al., 2022; Askell et al., 2021); recent work distills context into a la-tent memory rather than into weights (Zheng et al., 2026). Adapter generators map an input to a low-rank adapter in one forward pass: from a document (Charakorn et al., 2026), from a task description (Charakorn et al., 2025), an evolving code reposi-tory through a recurrent state (Hotsko et al., 2026), a reusable skill (Zhao et al., 2026), or video frames (Suri et al., 2026); from an LLM used directly as a weight generator (Liu et al., 2026b). CLAM reuses this document-to-adapter foundation but dif-fers on three axes. Input: these methods condition on a single static artifact (one document, skill, or clip); CLAM consumes a stream of interaction sessions. Accumulation: to handle many inputs, most prior work composes one adapter per chunk by rank-concatenation or merging, so the repre-sented set grows with the number of chunks and the merged signal dilutes (roughly 1/√N ), which does not scale. Code2LoRA-Evo (Hotsko et al., 2026) instead carries a recurrent state across code diffs. CLAM instead keeps a fixed rank and ac-cumulates through a recurrent neural update of a fixed-size latent state, so adapter cost and capacity stay constant as sessions grow. Setting: none target long-term LLM memory or pair adapter generation with a complementary episodic-retrieval channel. We also relate to test-time training (Sun et al., 2020; Hardt and Sun, 2024), which adapts weights per input via gradient steps; our adaptation is a single forward pass with no test-time optimization.

3.1 Consolidation Channel 

The consolidation channel maps a session stream to an adapter through three stages: read each session into a fixed-size latent workspace, carry 

the workspace across sessions via a gated recur-rence, and decode the final workspace into adapter weights (Hu et al., 2022). 

Read. To encode session St, we pass it through the frozen base model and collect per-layer hid-den states Zt ∈ RL×| St|× d, where L is the number of transformer blocks and d the model dimension. A stack of Perceiver cross-attention blocks (Jae-gle et al., 2021) compresses Zt into a fixed-size workspace: m latent queries cross-attend into Zt,with each slot pooling the session independently of the others. 

Carry. Memory requires the workspace to per-sist and accumulate. We maintain a carried state 

Qt ∈ Rm×d that threads through the stream via a gated recurrence (Figure 1b). At each step: (1) the Perceiver encoder reads St using the carried state 

Qt−1 as its latent queries, conditioning the read on all prior sessions—each slot pools independently, attending only to the session tokens; (2) a single self-attention layer over the m slots lets them ex-change information and reorganise—this is the only point at which slots communicate; (3) a gated write folds the result into the carried state:

3.2 Episodic Buffer 

A fixed-size parametric state cannot retain every verbatim detail with perfect fidelity—exact dates, proper names, precise figures require lossless sur-face form. The episodic buffer provides this com-plement: it supplies local evidence to a model whose weights have already been shaped by the full interaction history. The adapter encodes cross-session structure—what the user cares about, how entities relate, which topics recur—while the buffer provides the specific tokens the adapted model needs to ground its answer. We implement the buffer with minimal machin-ery: each session is embedded with a sentence en-coder (Reimers and Gurevych, 2019), and at query time we return the top-k pieces by cosine similarity. Our ablations show the gains are robust to retriever sophistication, and stronger indexing is orthogonal and only helps further.

The parametric channel is O(1) in the number of past sessions: a constant-size state, a constant-cost update per session, and a constant-size adapter.

4 Training Data Generation 

No public dataset provides multi-session, memory-dependent training data at scale (LoCoMo has ∼10 conversations, LongMemEval a few hundred ques-tions), so we build one with a fully automated six-stage pipeline (Figure 2), driven throughout by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (Dubey et al., 2024). It follows the LongMemEval (Wu et al., 2025) and LoCoMo (Maharana et al., 2024) recipes (planted evidence, distractor sessions, memory taxonomies) but is automated end-to-end. The pipeline runs in six stages (Figure 2). 

(1) Personas: thousands of personas are sampled from a balanced, decorrelated attribute grid (in-dustry, style, expertise, region, coherent age/life-stage, interests, plus a “seed twist” and cliche bans).

5 Experimental Setup 

5.1 Benchmarks 

We evaluate on LoCoMo (Maharana et al., 2024) and LongMemEval-S (Wu et al., 2025), which are standard LLM memory evaluation benchmarks. On LoCoMo we use the 1,540 questions of the four answerable categories (the adversarial cate-gory is excluded). For LongMemEval-S we sam-ple 30 questions per type (seed 0) and evaluate on the 173 answerable ones; its 7 abstention items are excluded, as LoCoMo’s adversarial category is. Because the parametric baselines train at test time, they are compared on a 180-question Lo-CoMo subset and on the same LongMemEval-S questions. Every system is run on the same three open base models: Gemma-2-2B (Gemma Team et al., 2024), Qwen3-4B (Yang et al., 2025) and 

Mistral-7B (Jiang et al., 2023). On both bench-marks the episodic buffer retrieves the same unit: a session split on turn boundaries into pieces of at most 1,800 characters.

Parametric (weight-space). Systems that write the history into parameters, our own setting. We reimplement TMEM (Ren et al., 2026) in both of its modes, built once per conversation ( TMEM-once ) (where the model is prompted to generate training data based on conversational history, ag-nostic of the current query) and rebuilt per ques-tion ( TMEM-per-question ), and TLM (Hu et al., 2025) (where the model is trained for each new query). We construct four strong baselines: Test-time LoRA , the same rank-8 adapter on the same projection fitted by SGD; FT-Ceiling , a full fine-tune of every weight under one protocol for all bases (AdamW (Loshchilov and Hutter, 2019), batch 4, 3% warmup, clipping 1.0, lr 5 × 10 −5 and 8 epochs on LoCoMo, 2 on LongMemEval-S;); and 

D2L-Merge and D2L-Retrieve , the same released hypernetwork (Charakorn et al., 2026), with the first based on their proposed chunk concatenation mechanism, and the second involving retrieving first to reduce the tokens to be internalized.

6.3 Parametric Consolidation Scales Where Test-Time Training Does Not Claim. The full CLAM system leads every weight-space memory; its consolidation channel alone leads every rank-8 method that adapts once per conversation, without a gradient step, and holds its accuracy on long histories. Table 3 evaluates each baseline as designed, without retrieval. The full system leads every method by 10.6 to 44.6 points on LoCoMo and 28.7 to 44.3 on LongMemEval-S. Like for like, the consolidation channel leads test-time LoRA, TLM, TMEM-once and both D2L variants on both bench-marks; only FT-Ceiling and TMEM-per-question, which rebuilds its memory for every question (315 gradient steps and 107.5 LLM calls per rebuild), lead it, and only on LoCoMo. In a controlled sweep on our synthetic validation set from 16 to 192 chunks (Fig-ure 6(b)), the consolidation channel stays level, the adapter adds 6 to 11 points over the buffer at every length, and test-time training stays below 5%. 

7 Conclusion 

Existing LLM memory systems consolidate experi-ence only in token space. We introduced CLAM, which adds the missing half: a recurrent hypernet-work compiles a stream of sessions into a low-rank adapter in a single forward pass, with no gradient 8steps and no generation calls, while a gated latent state carries knowledge forward in fixed size and a lightweight retrieval channel supplies verbatim detail at inference. Across two benchmarks and three open base models the two channels prove complementary, and the memory is built at a cost no comparable system approaches. Weight-space consolidation is a practical second memory chan-nel for language agents, and we hope it encourages work on memory that is internalised rather than re-read. 

Limitations 

CLAM is evaluated on two English conversational-memory benchmarks with base models of 2–7 B parameters; behavior at larger scale, in other lan-guages, and on non-conversational histories such as documents or code remains to be established. The consolidated adapter is a fixed-size store, though better results can be expected from scaling the adapter rank and the hypernetwork’s parameter count. Finally, the hypernetwork is trained once per base model on synthetic multi-session data; trans-ferring a trained hypernetwork to an unseen base model without retraining is outside the scope of this work. 

Ethics Statement 

This work uses two public benchmarks, LoCoMo and LongMemEval-S, together with a synthetic corpus generated from fictional personas. Both benchmarks, the open base models and the baseline implementations are used in accordance with their respective licences and intended research use; no human subjects were involved and no personal data was collected.

A Further Details 

A.1 Implementation Details 

The context encoder is a Perceiver-style resam-pler of 9 cross-attention blocks with 8 latent slots per layer, a 4× feed-forward expansion, SiLU ac-tivations and RMSNorm. Keys and values are the session activations alone, so slots pool a ses-sion independently and exchange no information during a read; the only slot-to-slot mixing is the between-session consolidation block, an 8-head self-attention with pre- and post-LayerNorm and a 

4× expansion, applied once per session boundary. A single-block decoder carries one output query per (layer, rank index) pair — L × r queries, 208 for a 

26 -layer base at r=8 — and each decodes into one rank-1 component. The resulting adapter is rank-8 on down_proj in every block, α=r, dropout 0:

2.40 M / 3.54 M / 4.72 M parameters on Gemma / Qwen / Mistral, about 7 MB in half precision, plus a fixed learned bias that does not vary with the conversation. The head is zero-initialised, so an un-trained hypernetwork is an exact no-op on the base model. The episodic buffer uses all-MiniLM-L6-v2 over session pieces of at most 1,800 characters, cosine top-k with k=5 and a 7K-token budget, re-sorted chronologically. At inference the adapter is merged into the base weights and the answer is a single greedy pass capped at 48 new tokens. 

A.2 Training 

Only the hypernetwork is trained, by context distil-lation against a teacher that answers with the full history in context. Stage 1 initialises from the re-leased document-to-adapter checkpoint with the recurrence inserted as a no-op. Stage 2 trains the carried state on multi-session examples of 3–30 

sessions with truncated backpropagation through time: cross-entropy on the gold answer plus a small penalty on adapter magnitude, AdamW at 1×10 −5,gradient accumulation 8 for an effective batch of 

64 , packed context and input lengths of 6144 to-kens, and a checkpoint every 1,000 steps. Reported checkpoints are 25 K Stage-2 steps for Gemma and 

8K for Qwen and Mistral. Training runs on a single A100-80GB and is paid once per base model, never per conversation.

B.4 Episodic-Buffer Granularity 

The episodic buffer retrieves session-level units: each session, split on turn boundaries into pieces of at most 1,800 characters and carrying its session’s date header.
