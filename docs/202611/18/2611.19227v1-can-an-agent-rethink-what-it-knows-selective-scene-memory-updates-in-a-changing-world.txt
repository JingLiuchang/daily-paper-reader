Abstract —Task configurations in factories, warehouses, and everyday physical environments change over time. Physical AI systems therefore need to incorporate new facts into scene memory while preserving valid information about equipment, objects, and operating constraints. We study updates to this parametric semantic-memory layer and present a joint evaluation of correction and preservation under finite update budgets. At fixed current fact count and memory parameter count, the protocol combines independent single-fact updates, budget tra-jectories, old-to-new answer diagnostics, and first stable success. Using a frozen 4B language model with late-layer LoRA, we evaluate seven preparation conditions and six update budgets on four-field synthetic semantic configurations. In two exposure-matched history conditions, increasing the budget from eight to 32 updates raises joint success from 6.25% to 94.79% and 91.15%, respectively, while exact obsolete-value outputs fall from 174/192 and 166/192 to zero. Constant32, which matches preparation steps but repeatedly trains on unchanged content during the first 32 events, achieves higher early joint success at eight steps (53.65%); at 32 steps, it reaches 85.42%, below both changing-history conditions. These results distinguish rapid correction under a small budget from simultaneous correctness of the new fact and the other three facts after further training. For both changing histories, most cases remain jointly correct at all subsequent measured points from step 16 onward. Output analysis shows that short-budget failures primarily retain the old value; repeated training on the new fact together with the other current facts progressively replaces these obsolete answers. 

Index Terms —Physical AI, scene memory, parametric memory, memory updates, correction and preservation, update budget 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Production changeovers, warehouse reorganization, and on-site collaboration alter the knowledge on which tasks depend. During a production changeover, for example, a workpiece-handling configuration may change while equipment condi-tions and other operating rules remain valid. In a warehouse, an object’s assigned location may change while other ob-jects’ attributes and handling requirements must be retained. Physical AI systems that perceive, reason, or act in physical environments need to maintain current knowledge relevant to these on-site tasks. We define scene memory as a set of semantic facts as-sociated with a particular site, device, or task configuration and revisable as that setting changes. It supplies currently

valid information for queries and high-level task decisions. Perception, event reports, or human corrections provide update content; parametric memory writes that content into persistent state, from which subsequent queries retrieve the latest facts. Updating scene memory requires a local change to take effect while the remaining current facts stay correct. We study finite-budget updates at fixed current fact count and memory parameter count . Each evaluation independently starts from a prepared checkpoint, changes one fact, and preserves the others. Correction, preservation, joint success, and obsolete-value outputs are measured together at six update budgets. Our experiments use four-field synthetic semantic configurations and independent language queries to test the memory layer’s outputs. Figure 1 shows how the update histories are constructed. At recall time, the model receives its checkpoint and the current query; the facts are supplied by the parameterized state. This paper examines the criteria for a successful memory update and its stability during subsequent training. An effective update must incorporate the new fact, preserve the remaining facts, and maintain joint correctness in later evaluations. At step 16, the two histories yield 153 and 144 cases with all four facts correct, respectively; in each condition, three of these cases become incorrect again at a later checkpoint. We there-fore evaluate correctness at individual training checkpoints and identify the first checkpoint from which four-fact joint correctness persists at all subsequent measured points. This distinguishes checkpoint-level update success from sustained correctness. We present a joint evaluation protocol at fixed memory size, measuring single-fact correction and preservation of the remaining facts within the same probe. We track the transition from old-value to new-value outputs, the model’s relative preference between the two answers, and the onset of four-fact joint correctness that persists at subsequent measured checkpoints. We also report complete results across seven conditions, paired comparisons, and checkpoint-reload veri-fication. The evaluation thus addresses three questions: how many training updates a correction requires, whether other facts remain correct, and whether joint correctness persists with continued training. Here, stability means correctness at every subsequent measured checkpoint through step 32 within arXiv:2611.19227v1 [cs.AI] 14 Nov 2026 the same correction run. II. R ELATED WORK 

LoRA represents adaptation through low-rank weight up-dates [1]. We instantiate our evaluation with late-layer LoRA, freezing its learned input factor during rewriting. This fac-torization represents scene-level semantic state and fixes the writable parameter set for comparing update trajectories. Model editing addresses targeted knowledge changes to-gether with preservation of unrelated information. WISE uses side memory and routing [2]; MEMOIR restricts updates through sparse, activation-dependent masks [3]. Editable Neu-ral Networks and MEND train models or update rules for editability [4], [5]. Our evaluation focuses on the budget axis: it measures how correction and preservation develop together as an update proceeds, complementing the architectural and optimization approaches in these studies. Continual-learning research distinguishes acquisition of new information from retention of old information [6]. We in-stantiate this distinction as updates to a semantic state with a fixed number of fields: one field receives a new value while the others retain their currently valid values. Joint correctness captures consistency of the complete state; output decomposition and stable-success budgets further characterize how that state is established. Semantic knowledge in Physical AI must be connected to a concrete environment. SayCan combines language-model task knowledge with value functions for executable skills [7]; DynaMem maintains changing environment information us-ing dynamic three-dimensional spatio-semantic memory [8]. These studies demonstrate the role of scene information in physical tasks. We focus on the update interface of parametric semantic memory, evaluating whether a locally changed fact and the remaining state stay jointly correct. This perspective complements environment perception, spatial representation, and task execution. III. J OINT EVALUATION OF SCENE -M EMORY UPDATES 

A. Scene State and Local Updates 

We represent each scene configuration as a set of currently valid semantic facts. An update replaces one field’s old value with a new value while retaining the other fields. After the update, queries with different wording test each field, record-ing correctness of the new value, correctness of the remaining fields, and joint correctness of the complete configuration. This interface corresponds to local configuration revisions in factories, changes to object information in warehouses, and task-rule updates in everyday environments. We implement this interface using four-field synthetic con-figurations. Teaching statements write field values into mem-ory, checkpoints store the parameterized state, and evaluation questions test retrieval after writing. All configurations use the same number of fields and parameters, allowing comparisons of update budgets, preparation histories, and post-update cor-rectness at fixed state capacity.

C. A Memory Interface for Physical Settings 

This update interface operates on scene facts. In facto-ries, these may describe equipment configurations, process conditions, and operating rules; in warehouses and facility management, object attributes, zone conventions, and task conditions; and in everyday physical settings, environment configurations and collaboration requirements. Robots, pro-duction systems, and other Physical AI platforms can organize semantic-memory evaluation around the same requirement: revise a local fact while preserving the rest of the state. Our experimental unit is a synthetic semantic configuration, and the measured outcome is its language-based readout after an update. Treating this memory layer as a separate component gives state writing, knowledge retrieval, and downstream task execution distinct interfaces. Joint correctness then character-izes changes between memory versions. VI. C ONCLUSION 

Motivated by changing knowledge in factories, warehouses, and everyday physical environments, we study parametric up-dates to the semantic-memory layer of Physical AI and present a joint evaluation of correction and preservation under finite update budgets. At fixed fact count and memory parameter count, the method combines budget trajectories, old/new an-swer diagnostics, and stable success at measured checkpoints to characterize how a complete state emerges after a local revision. In four-field synthetic configurations with fixed-A LoRA, increasing the budget from eight to 32 steps raises joint success from 6.25% to 94.79% and 91.15% in the two primary histories, and removes exact obsolete-value outputs. The ranking reversal against the constant-content reference further illustrates why budget and preservation should be considered together. The complete protocol and measurements provide a reproducible component-level basis for evaluating memory updates for physical settings. AUTHOR CONTRIBUTIONS
