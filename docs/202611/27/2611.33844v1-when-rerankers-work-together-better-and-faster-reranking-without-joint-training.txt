ABSTRACT 

Standalone reranker quality does not determine its value in a multistage RAG pipeline. We systematically study heterogeneous rerankers composed without joint training, spanning lexical, single-vector, late-interaction, and joint query–context scoring. Two lightweight semantic coarse scorers, Cache-Pointwise and Cache-Shapley, learn from passage relevance labels and share cacheable representations and linear-time singleton inference. Under matched candidates and budgets, we compare cascades with stage-removal references to measure evidence retention, repair and breakage, reader accuracy, and online pruning cost. On NoLiMa 32K with Gemma, inserting these scorers before CrossEncoder or Provence improves answer accuracy by 4.02–9.43 percentage points while accelerating online pruning by 1.57–1.88 × relative to the corresponding reranker alone. Cache-Pointwise composed with ColBERTv2 through reciprocal rank fusion improves accuracy by 4.60 points at 1.36 × speedup over direct ColBERTv2. The stage-removal comparisons reveal an asymmetry: a cascade can outperform its final reranker alone while underperforming its coarse selector alone. Two readers, a second multi-hop dataset, and three-stage extensions show that the gains depend on the reader, reranker pairing, and stage configuration. These findings demonstrate a practical route to better and faster reranking without joint training and motivate studying stage order, candidate budgets, and rank fusion as determinants of composition quality. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) (Lewis et al., 2020) depends on which retrieved items reach the reader. Large candidate pools improve coverage. They also increase irrelevant text and reranker computation, motivating intermediate selection that preserves required evidence. RAG commonly combines BM25 or dual-encoder retrieval (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009; Karpukhin et al., 2020; Reimers & Gurevych, 2019) with a CrossEncoder (Nogueira & Cho, 2019). Multi-stage systems already establish the coarse-to-fine topology and its quality–cost trade-off: Feature-based learning-to-rank reduces BERT load (Zhang et al., 2021), CMC reranks cached embeddings before a CrossEncoder (Song et al., 2024), and FunnelRAG varies granularity, scale, and capacity (Zhao et al., 2025). Matched-token evaluations show that DOS RAG can match or outperform more complex RAG pipelines (Laitenberger et al., 2025). We study how rerankers with different scoring mechanisms behave when composed without joint training. On NoLiMa (Modarressi et al., 2025) 32K with Gemma, Cache-Pointwise before CrossEn-coder raises answer accuracy from 21.49% to 28.51% and accelerates online pruning by 1.58 ×. Yet Cache-Pointwise alone reaches 31.49% under the same 30% token cap (Tables 1–2). This contrast motivates two questions: when does coarse selection improve an existing reranker, and when does subsequent reranking improve the coarse selector? We test lexical, single-vector, late-interaction, and joint query–context scoring under a shared evaluation protocol. To instantiate lightweight semantic coarse selection, our weakly supervised scorer encodes corpus units independently and each query once, then applies a compact set model to reusable pair features. MS MARCO (Bajaj et al., 2016) provides passage-level labels, so each passage remains one training player. No downstream scores, reader feedback, or gradients across stages enter coarse training. This setup tests whether separately developed scoring mechanisms can work effectively together through candidate selection alone. The matched models differ only in credit training. Cache-Pointwise uses singleton scores in the pairwise loss. Cache-Shapley is a Shapley-trained singleton scorer: it compares sampled-coalition marginal credits during training, then uses the same O(n) singleton readout. Together they test whether composition gains hold across two training objectives under a shared deployment interface. Their matched comparison also isolates how coalition-derived supervision affects coarse-scorer quality and stability. We cross no filtering, BM25, frozen MiniLM, Cache-Pointwise, and Cache-Shapley with fixed CrossEncoder and Provence stages, and extend the study to ColBERTv2 endpoints and three-stage cascades. Two open-weight readers, knowledge slices, a second multi-hop dataset, Monte-Carlo sweeps, and three seeds establish where these effects persist and where they change. Our contributions are: 1. A systematic study of heterogeneous rerankers composed without joint training, spanning lexical, single-vector, late-interaction, and joint scoring in two- and three-stage pipelines. 2. Two cacheable semantic coarse rerankers with matched architectures and O(n) singleton deployment, demonstrating that lightweight coarse selection can improve precise-only evidence retention, answer quality, and speed simultaneously. 3. Controlled stage-removal and evidence-transition analyses that identify improvement, preser-vation, and degradation across compositions, and establish stage interactions as a design consideration for multistage pruning. The central finding is that independently trained rerankers can improve quality and speed together, but each stage’s value depends on its specific composition. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Multi-stage retrieval and evaluation. BM25, dual encoders, ColBERTv2 late interaction, and CrossEncoders provide lexical, single-vector, token-level, and joint scoring (Robertson & Zaragoza, 2Preprint. 2009; Karpukhin et al., 2020; Reimers & Gurevych, 2019; Santhanam et al., 2022; Nogueira & Cho, 2019). Feature-based learning-to-rank filters candidates before BERT (Zhang et al., 2021); CMC, CODER, and EBCAR rerank cached embeddings (Song et al., 2024; Zerveas et al., 2022; Yuan et al., 2026); and FunnelRAG varies granularity and capacity (Zhao et al., 2025). Evaluations with matched token budgets find strong performance from source-order-preserving RAG relative to summarization-based pipelines (Laitenberger et al., 2025). Our protocol compares the same cascade with both stage-removal references at fixed budget and reader. 

RAG reranking and context selection. RECOMP selects or summarizes evidence (Xu et al., 2024); Selective Context prunes redundant lexical units (Li et al., 2023), while LLMLingua and LLMLingua-2 compress prompts (Jiang et al., 2023; Pan et al., 2024); and Provence and EXIT prune sentences (Chirkova et al., 2025; Hwang et al., 2025). LooComp learns sentence-removal scores from evidence annotations (Do et al., 2026). Other methods optimize masks or budgets (Huang et al., 2025; Taguchi et al., 2025; Meng et al., 2025). Unlike direct selection, our coarse output feeds a fixed precise stage, exposing both admitted work and downstream compatibility. 

Context value and generator feedback. Leave-one-out attribution and influence-based selection quantify context utility relative to a target generator (Liu et al., 2025; Deng et al., 2025); attention-based attribution learns to approximate ablation effects (Cohen-Wang et al., 2025). DynamicRAG and BAR-RAG optimize evidence selection using generator feedback (Sun et al., 2025; 2026). Our training uses passage relevance for reader-independent reuse, and fixed LLMs supply downstream evaluation. 

RAG evaluation frameworks. RAGAS and RAGVUE decompose RAG outputs into retrieval, answer, and grounding dimensions (Es et al., 2024; Murugaraj et al., 2026). RAG Playground compares retrieval strategies and prompting choices (Papadimitriou et al., 2024); Simon et al. (2024) propose a systematic evaluation methodology with a configuration-dependency case study. SCARF evaluates deployed RAG configurations through a black-box interface (Rengo et al., 2025). These frameworks assess pipeline outputs or compare complete configurations. Our protocol attributes changes inside a reranking cascade through matched stage-removal references, conditional evidence transitions, and explicit online cost coordinates. 

3 COMPOSING HETEROGENEOUS RERANKERS 

We compose stages with different scoring mechanisms while keeping their parameters fixed during cascade execution. Learned coarse scorers are trained separately from the downstream rerankers and reader; the cascade has no joint training objective. We estimate ϕi from M uniformly sampled permutations (Castro et al., 2009). Each candidate unit receives the average marginal score observed when it enters a permutation prefix. The matched objectives therefore isolate training-credit choice while holding the representation, scorer architecture, and passage labels fixed. 3.4 SINGLETON DEPLOYMENT AND ONLINE COST 

Both cacheable models deploy through singleton scores ri = vθ ({ci}). Each query requires one query encoding, n feature constructions, and n compact scorer evaluations; corpus-unit encoding occurs once when the cache is populated. The precise reranker then processes the admitted candidates together with the query. Cache-Shapley uses Shapley credits exclusively as its training supervision rule; deployment uses the learned singleton value vθ ({ci}) as a relevance score. 3.5 FIXED -B UDGET SELECTION 

The experiments sort candidates by singleton score, apply the dataset’s count or token budget, and restore source order. Count selection retains round( rn ) units, clipped to [1 , n ] for nonempty inputs, with ties rounded to even. NoLiMa greedily scans descending scores, skips units that do not fit the remaining token budget, and continues to lower-ranked units. It then counts the joined context exactly and removes the lowest-scored selected units if needed to meet the cap. Appendix A.2 gives complete pseudocode for matched training and semantic cascade inference. 

4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Cache-Pointwise and Cache-Shapley share a frozen MiniLM-L6-v2 encoder, scorer architecture, MS MARCO passage supervision, optimization, and singleton deployment; only the training credit differs. We evaluate passage ranking, zero-shot sentence and paragraph ranking, NoLiMa 32K selection with Gemma 3 (Gemma Team et al., 2025) and Llama 3.1 (Grattafiori et al., 2024) readers, and cascades ending in CrossEncoder, ColBERTv2, or Provence. Every cascade uses matched examples, fixed stage loads, and a common final budget. We report ranking, exact evidence retention, reader quality, synchronized online latency, and memory. The experiments test coarse-scorer transfer (Section 4.2), coarse-only reader outcomes (Section 4.3), and heterogeneous cascades (Section 4.4). Sections 4.5–4.6 examine variation across knowledge 5Preprint. types and datasets. Appendix A gives the full evaluation and execution setup. Appendix B.2 reports training-credit, readout, and multi-seed diagnostics. 4.2 PRE -C OMPOSITION TRANSFER ACROSS CANDIDATE GRANULARITIES 

Passage supervision transfers across candidate granularities. This experiment evaluates the coarse scorer before any precise reranker or reader is introduced. Frozen cosine and the learned heads share the same candidate embeddings, so their difference isolates the learned query–candidate feature fusion and its weak supervision. Both learned scorers improve frozen MiniLM cosine on MS MARCO, HotpotQA, and 2WikiMultiHopQA across NPRS and standard ranking metrics. Frozen cosine leads on MuSiQue. A matched BGE control reproduces the same three-positive/one-negative transfer pattern. Appendix Tables 9–11 contain the complete NPRS, MAP, nDCG, evidence-recall, and paired results. Passage labels thus support transfer to sentence candidates, while MuSiQue shows that this advantage varies across target tasks. 4.3 COARSE -O NLY REFERENCES SEPARATE EVIDENCE FROM READER RESPONSE 

Retained evidence and surrounding text jointly shape reader input. NoLiMa removes literal question–evidence overlap and evaluates semantic selection under identical 32K contexts and token budgets. A selector determines whether the annotated evidence survives and which surrounding sentences reach the reader; every method restores source order before generation. Table 1 presents evidence-preserving reader anchors alongside key direct and cascade results. Appendix Table 12 supplies the complete direct reference panel. Both anchors retain all gold evidence, yet removing the surrounding book raises QA from 22.18% to 95.06%. Evidence retention therefore leaves substantial variation in reader accuracy unexplained. An evidence-fixed distractor control in Appendix A.9 further examines this effect. Among direct selectors, Cache-Pointwise achieves the strongest aggregate retention and QA for both readers; Cache-Shapley improves the shared frozen representation with Gemma. The Cache-PW–Provence cascade reaches 31.72% QA with Gemma but 21.84% with Llama, below direct Cache-PW’s 26.55% for that reader. 4.5 COMPATIBILITY VARIES ACROSS KNOWLEDGE REGIMES 

Commonsense and world knowledge reward different candidate distributions. An aggregate score can hide a gain on one reasoning regime and a loss on another. Table 4 therefore separates NoLiMa outcomes by knowledge type under identical inputs and budgets. Accuracy measures reader response and retention measures evidence survival. Repair/breakage (R/B) compares each cascade with its precise-only reference: repair is the fraction of reference misses recovered by the cascade, and breakage is the fraction of reference hits lost by the cascade. These conditional transitions explain how the net retention difference is formed. Appendix Tables 16 and 17 provide complete intervals and denominators. 

Repair and breakage localize evidence complementarity. The learned cascades repair precise-reranker misses in both knowledge regimes. Provence after Cache-Pointwise repairs 237 of 362 prior misses on world knowledge and 62 of 70 on commonsense: world knowledge has more repaired misses in absolute terms but a lower repair rate and a higher breakage rate. Commonsense QA improves over direct Cache-Pointwise; world-knowledge QA decreases slightly. Both cascade slices exceed direct Provence. 6 CONCLUSION 

We systematically studied heterogeneous RAG rerankers composed without joint training across stages. Lightweight semantic coarse selection improves the evidence retention and answer quality of fixed precise rerankers while accelerating online pruning. The gains demonstrate that separately developed scoring mechanisms can form effective cascades through candidate selection and rank fusion. The stage-removal comparisons also show why these gains depend on composition: a cascade can improve its precise-only endpoint while a later stage reduces coarse-only quality. Reader identity, knowledge type, and reranker pairing change this balance. These findings motivate multistage pruning as a research direction, with stage order, candidate budgets, rank fusion, and interactions among scoring mechanisms as central design choices. C DESIGN JUSTIFICATION AND RESEARCH OUTLOOK 

This appendix states the computational distinction behind cacheable coarse ranking and records the system and objective questions opened by the experiments. C.1 WHY CACHEABLE COARSE RANKING REDUCES PRECISE -S TAGE COST 

An independently encoded corpus vector is computed once and reused across queries. Online query encoding and feature fusion scale linearly with the number of candidates. CrossEncoder and Provence process query–candidate token interactions for every candidate they receive. A cacheable coarse stage therefore reduces the number of expensive joint evaluations. Table 13 measures the complete online pruning boundary and includes the additional first-stage work. A complete computational account tracks indexing cost alongside the measured query encoding, feature scoring, precise-stage candidates, synchronized latency, memory, and reader input. In the measured cascades, the reduction in precise-stage work exceeds the added coarse-stage cost, producing a net online pruning speedup. 26 Preprint. C.2 IMPLICATIONS FOR MULTISTAGE PRUNING 

Stage-removal comparisons support insertion and removal under an application’s quality and cost constraints. Declare (h, α, B, R) and compare h, g, and g→h on matched examples. For Cache-PW →CrossEncoder (Table 2), the contrast with direct CrossEncoder gives +21.84 retention points, +7.01 QA points, and 1.58 × speed at 0.56 GiB additional memory, favoring the cascade when that memory is feasible. The reverse contrast shows that CrossEncoder reduces direct Cache-PW retention by 13.56 and QA by 2.99 points, favoring coarse-only deployment when the endpoint is optional. The three-stage panel in Appendix B.1 extends this choice to pipeline depth: adding coarse selection has different reader effects before ColBERTv2–CrossEncoder and ColBERTv2–Provence. Stage type, order, candidate load, and rank fusion therefore provide concrete variables for choosing which stages to combine and how much work to assign to each. C.3 RESEARCH OUTLOOK 

Training credit and deployment readout. The matched objectives show distinct peak and stability behavior, with singleton deployment as the strongest tested readout. Controlled evidence relations, multi-seed QA, and matched stronger encoders can connect pointwise or coalition-derived training credit to reader outcomes. Sweeps over Mtrain , Mreadout , evidence density, and context length can identify when a learned set score transfers to singleton ranking. 

Objective–representation interaction. The BGE pointwise control confirms that representation choice changes transfer and the relation between retention and QA. A matched BGE Shapley model and multi-seed cascade evaluation can separate the contribution of the frozen representation from the training-credit objective. 

Relation-specific interaction analysis. Controlled examples with duplicated, complementary, conflicting, and individually insufficient evidence can connect conditional marginal patterns to reader behavior. Interaction indices can then analyze a specified learned game with relation labels and paired reader outcomes. 

Multistage pruning and interactions among rerankers. The quality and speed gains from separately trained stages motivate a broader study of heterogeneous pipelines. Varying stage order, candidate loads, and rank fusion can reveal which scoring mechanisms complement one another and when further pruning removes useful evidence. Our two- and three-stage panels provide starting points for adaptive routing, early exit, and joint quality–latency optimization. Joint training can then be compared with independently trained compositions under the same stage-removal references. Large-corpus experiments should report indexing, cache memory, throughput, synchronized latency, and answer quality together. 

Broader long-context evaluation. FictionLiveBench and other non-literal narrative benchmarks can test transfer beyond NoLiMa. A complete evaluation should cover multiple lengths, token budgets, stories, readers, and insertion positions with cluster-level uncertainty. These directions aim to identify which pruning stages preserve or improve reader quality at an acceptable computational cost across datasets and serving conditions. 27
