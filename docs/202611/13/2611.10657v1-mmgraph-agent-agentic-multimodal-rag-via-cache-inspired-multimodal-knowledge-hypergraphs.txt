Abstract
Driven by increasingly complex real-world applications, Retrieval-Augmented
Generation (RAG) has evolved from textual pipelines to multimodal settings, com-
prising two complementary dimensions: knowledge organization and retrieval
enhancement. However, existing methods still face challenges of high construction
cost, context window limitations, and the lack of unified training across offline and
online knowledge sources. To address these challenges, we propose MMGraph-
Agent, a unified multimodal RAG framework based on cache-inspired multimodal
knowledge hypergraphs. MMGraph-Agent enables extremely lightweight hyper-
graph construction with zero API cost and dynamic memory-based retrieval that
alleviates long-context issues. Experiments across offline, online, and joint re-
trieval settings demonstrate consistent improvements in performance, efficiency,
and architectural unification. Our software and data are publicly available1.

However, as shown in Figure 1, three main challenges remain. (i) High Cost in Knowledge
Organization. Constructing multimodal knowledge graphs often incurs substantial costs [35, 14],
relying on manual annotation or expensive API calls, which limits scalability. (ii) Long Context in
Retrieval Enhancement. Multi-round retrieval is constrained by model context windows [2]; as
retrieved evidence accumulates, context overflow becomes increasingly likely [34, 66], degrading
reasoning quality. (iii) Lack of a Unified Framework. Unifying local private knowledge [14] and
online web knowledge [60] within a single framework is challenging due to structural differences.
To address these challenges, we propose MMGraph-Agent, a unified multimodal RAG framework
based on cache-inspired multimodal knowledge hypergraphs. As shown in Figure 2, we first propose
a lightweight multimodal hypergraph construction method with zero API cost, substantially
reducing both construction cost and build time while still preserving knowledge completeness. We
further design a cache-inspired multimodal hypergraph memory that dynamically stores, updates,
and serves retrieved multimodal evidence during iterative agentic reasoning, thereby alleviating
context window limitations and improving multi-round retrieval efficiency. Furthermore, MMGraph-
Agent also integrates multimodal reinforcement learning (RL) to enable coordinated retrieval from
offline knowledge corpora and online web environments within a single unified RAG framework.
To validate its effectiveness, we conduct experiments in three settings: Knowledge-Only, WebSearch-
Only, and Knowledge+WebSearch. Results demonstrate that MMGraph-Agent consistently outper-
forms a range of baselines spanning generation, RAG-based, and training-based methods in answer
accuracy and retrieval efficiency, while substantially reducing construction cost and context token
usage, thereby demonstrating its strong potential for real-world multimodal question answering.

Related Work
Multimodal RAG. RAG grounds LLMs with external knowledge [27] and has evolved from text-only
pipelines to multimodal settings [65, 50]. For knowledge organization, prior work moves from flat or
hierarchical chunk retrieval [46, 1] to entity-relation graphs and higher-order hyperedges [9, 61, 29,
15, 16, 32, 37, 35, 54, 56, 30, 38, 20], further extended to multimodal knowledge graphs and hybrid
vector-graph designs [53, 14, 42, 19, 7, 67, 31]. In this work, MMGraph-Agent builds a multimodal
hypergraph that preserves cross-modal relational structure at zero API cost and high build speed.
Agentic Retrieval. For retrieval enhancement, LLM-based agents reframe retrieval as sequential
decision-making, letting the model decide when and what to retrieve [64, 55]. Recent methods drive
iterative, reasoning-grounded retrieval over textual and graph corpora [24, 48, 34] and extend it to
multimodal and open-web search [60, 18, 8, 49, 59, 28, 33, 62, 21, 51]. Building on this line of work,
MMGraph-Agent introduces a cache-inspired hypergraph memory to keep context bounded across
long retrieval trajectories, and a unified policy that jointly retrieves from offline and online sources.

Experiments
We answer six RQs: RQ1: Does MMGraph-Agent outperform existing baselines across the three
regimes? RQ2: Does each module of MMGraph-Agent work? RQ3: How cheap is the zero-API-cost
multimodal hypergraph construction? RQ4: Does the cache-inspired memory bound the prompt
under long-chain reasoning? RQ5: Does the offline-trained policy transfer to online retrieval without
parameter updates? RQ6: How does MMGraph-Agent solve a hard representative multimodal case?
4.1
Experimental Setup
Datasets and Evaluation Metrics. We evaluate on 23 multimodal QA datasets grouped by regime
(§D.1): (i) Knowledge-Only (K-only): DocBench [72], MMLongBench-Doc [39], ViDoSeek [58],
2Wiki [17], HotpotQA [63], Musique [52], NQ [26], PopQA [40], TriviaQA [25]; (ii) WebSearch-
Only (Web-only): FVQA [57], InfoSeek [5], MMSearch [23], SimpleVQA [6], LiveVQA [11];
(iii) Knowledge+WebSearch (K+Web): MedXpertQA [73], FinMME [36], We-Math2.0 [43], R-
Bench [13], MicroVQA [4], EEmo-Bench [12], ArtQuest [3], AstroLLaVA [68], GeoGrid-Bench [22];
ViDoSeek, PopQA, TriviaQA and all Web-only datasets are held out as OOD to probe generalization.
The only headline metric is token-level F1 (%) against the gold answer [34, 35], which doubles as the
per-rollout reward RF1; per-regime, per-dataset and efficiency breakdowns are in Appendix D.2.
Baselines and Ablations. All methods share one corpus and prompt (§E): (a) generation-only
NaiveGeneration (NaiveGen) [69]; (b) offline RAG-based StandardMMRAG (StdMMRAG) [65] and
RAG-Anything (RAG-Any) [14]; (c) training-based SFT [70], GRPO [47], and MMSearch-R1 [60]
learning a web-search policy; plus w/o Hypergraph, w/o Memory, w/o Policy, and w/o Retrieval each
isolating one module of §3. NaiveGen, StdMMRAG and RAG-Any have GPT-4.1-mini [41] variants.
Implementation Details. Backbone: Qwen2.5-VL-3B [44] with a frozen CLIP [45] encoder f
(D=768). Memory: per-bank retrieval budget C=5; evidence truncated to 1,200 words, condensed
to an ≈300-word digest per round, context cap 5,000 tokens. GRPO: G=8, ϵ=0.2, κ=10−3 over
75 steps on 600 examples (233 from six K-only and 367 multimodal with 425 images from eight
in-domain datasets; none OOD), on 8×A100 (80GB); F1 is mean±std over three sampled evaluation
runs on the same 640-item pool (temperature 1.0); full hyperparameters in Appendix E.

4.2
Main Results (RQ1)
As shown in Table 3, MMGraph-Agent-3B improves over the second-best baseline per dataset by
+11.6 F1 on average across all 23 datasets, with the largest gains on FinMME (+44.93), GeoGrid-
Bench (+29.47), and ViDoSeek (+23.06); RAG-based and training-based baselines trail it on every
regime, and the gap widens on long-document corpora and on the Knowledge+WebSearch regime
where evidence must be combined across offline and online sources within one trajectory. On the
eight held-out OOD datasets, it still leads the strongest published agent MMSearch-R1 by +13.0
F1, indicating that the offline hypergraph and the shared retrieval schema generalize to distributions
unseen during training, and it ranks first on all eight † (OOD) rows of Table 3.

5
Conclusion
In this work, we introduce MMGraph-Agent, a unified multimodal RAG framework that addresses
three problems of current systems: costly knowledge organization, long-context retrieval, and the
structural split between offline and online sources. It pairs a zero-API-cost multimodal hypergraph, a
cache-inspired L1/L2 memory, and a GRPO-trained policy emitting retrieval and answer tokens over
one shared action space. On 23 multimodal QA datasets MMGraph-Agent outperforms generation-
only, RAG-based, and training-based baselines, and ablations show that the three modules play
complementary roles. Multilingual encoders and learned memory constants are left for future work.

A.1

Operator O1: Sentence-Level Multimodal Extractor (πext, §3.1). spaCy splits each parsed chunk
into sentences and turns every sentence into an n-ary hyperedge over its named entities; no LLM is
invoked, so extraction incurs zero API cost and runs entirely on local CPU hardware.
# operate.py: zero-API-cost extractor running under ProcessPoolExecutor
def _mmextractor_worker(text: str):
global _NLP
doc = _NLP(text)
# spaCy en_core_web_lg, frozen
results = []
for sent in doc.sents:
ents = [ent.text for ent in sent.ents]
if ents:
results.append({
"sentence": sent.text.strip(),
"entities": list(dict.fromkeys(ents))
})
return results

Benchmark Detail
This appendix expands §4 into a self-contained reference for the evaluation suite. We do not propose
a new benchmark: every dataset is publicly released by its authors and reused as is, re-grouped by
retrieval regime to match MMGraph-Agent’s joint offline-online setting. Throughout, K is the static
multimodal corpus, W the dynamic web reachable via S, and (q, vq) a query with gold answer y∗.
D.1
Dataset Details
The 23 datasets split into three retrieval regimes; the eight †-marked rows in Table 3 are OOD datasets
held out from training so that §4.2 can probe out-of-distribution generalization. We sample 16–32
items per dataset for a 640-item evaluation pool, following MMSearch-R1 [60]; the reported std.
reflects three repeated runs on this same fixed pool, as decoding samples at temperature 1.0. The 600
training examples comprise 233 questions from six Knowledge-Only datasets and 367 multimodal
questions (425 images) from eight in-domain datasets; none is from the eight held-out OOD datasets,
and with Ron disabled in training every retrieval targets the offline hypergraph.
(i) Knowledge-Only (9 datasets). The gold answer is recoverable from K, so MMGraph-Agent
serves them through Gm
H and rarely escalates to the web. DocBench [72], MMLongBench-Doc [39]
require evidence from long PDFs with figures and tables; ViDoSeek† [58] adds visually rich pages.
2Wiki [17], HotpotQA [63], Musique [52] test multi-hop composition over Wikipedia, while NQ [26],
PopQA† [40], TriviaQA† [25] probe single-hop factoid recall over open-domain Wikipedia.
(ii) WebSearch-Only (5 datasets). The gold answer concerns entities absent from K, so MMGraph-
Agent must escalate to W via SerpAPI. FVQA† [57] requires commonsense facts about visual entities;
InfoSeek† [5], MMSearch† [23], SimpleVQA† [6] ask encyclopedic questions whose answers depend
on visual subject identification; LiveVQA† [11] adds time-sensitive after-cutoff news queries. We
build no offline KG for them, so the WebSearch-Only column isolates the online-retrieval skill of πθ.
(iii) Knowledge+WebSearch (9 datasets). The gold answer requires combining offline grounding
with up-to-date or domain-specific web evidence within one trajectory. MedXpertQA [73] and
MicroVQA [4] pose clinical and microscopy questions; FinMME [36] and R-Bench [13] pair financial
or scientific reports with current snippets; We-Math2.0 [43] and GeoGrid-Bench [22] couple math/geo
evidence with web explanations; and EEmo-Bench [12], ArtQuest [3], AstroLLaVA [68] require both
visual grounding and external cultural or scientific commentary retrieved from the web.

tokens; the cache statistics in §4.5 and the cost–latency numbers in §4.4 are derived from these logs
without extra inference, with API calls priced at the public gpt-4o, gpt-4o-mini, and SerpAPI

turn, 4,096 per trajectory) under a MAX_GEN_ROUND = 8 budget; the same decoding configuration
is shared across all baselines and ablations of Table 3 so that retrieval-side gains cannot be confounded

the macro-F1 standard deviation is reported after the ± sign in every table cell, while † in Table 3

Future Work
Several promising directions extend the framework. First, multilingual and broader-modality encoders
in place of CLIP+spaCy would let Gm
H absorb a wider range of corpora and modalities (e.g., audio,
video and point clouds) while keeping the zero-API-cost guarantee of §3.1. Second, learning the
memory constants (C and the truncation, digest and context-cap lengths) inside the GRPO loop would
adapt the cache to each regime’s trajectory length without the manual sweep of §4.5. Third, adding
process-supervision terms (citation faithfulness, retrieval cost, latency) to the reward of Eq. (6) would
let πθ trade F1 against deployment constraints while keeping the same offline-to-online interface.
Finally, scaling πθ to larger backbones with more training and building Gm
H over more domains
and languages are natural extensions, as both reuse the zero-API-cost construction of §3.1 and the
offline-to-online GRPO recipe of §3.3, with the unified L1/L2 cache keeping the prompt bounded.
