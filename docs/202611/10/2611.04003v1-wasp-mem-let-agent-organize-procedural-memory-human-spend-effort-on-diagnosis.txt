ABSTRACT 

LLM agents struggle to automate complex enterprise procedures because static, pre-built harness cannot capture proprietary policies, undocumented behaviors, or state-dependent recovery paths that surface only during execution. Procedural memory accrued from agent experience offers a training-free path to closing this gap—yet unlike episodic records or semantic facts, it must consolidate repeated successes and diagnosed failures into reusable actions and recovery strategies. We introduce WASP-Mem (Wiki-based Agent-Synthesized Procedural Memory), a nimble but effective method that distills execution traces and feedback into per-sistent, interlinked wiki pages, enabling continual agent learning. On TheAgent-Company benchmark, WASP-Mem raises the checkpoint pass rate by 4.6–6.1 %over the strongest baseline we compared across three models while requiring 

57–61 % less storage. We further present a systematic controlled study span-ning consolidation method, feedback type, trace ingestion scope, and refinement rounds. Our results reveal that prescriptive knowledge organization such as hu-man defined schemas and explicit deduplication rules hurts performance rather than closing the procedural-knowledge gap. In contrast, human diagnosis con-verts failed traces into corrective knowledge and drives sustained improvement: a single round of human diagnosis outperforms four rounds of self-judgment by 

7.0–10.9 %. These findings point to a simple division of labor: let agents organize procedural memory, and invest human effort where it matters most—diagnosis. 

1 INTRODUCTION 

LLM agents are becoming practical interfaces for complex enterprise procedure automation: they plan, call tools, and modify shared services to complete multi-step workplace tasks (Xu et al., 2024; ang Gao et al., 2025). Their behavior is governed by a static agent harness, including the soft-ware implementation, system prompt, tools, environment interfaces, and fixed contexts supplied before deployment (Sumers et al., 2023). Yet the operational knowledge needed in a proprietary environment is intrinsically unforeseeable: under-documented APIs, organization-specific policies, strict output conventions, abnormal service states, and recovery paths often surface only during ex-ecution. In production, designers cannot know in advance which hidden condition will cause the next failure. Compounding this, unlike many coding or mathematical benchmarks where broadly available knowledge combines with compilers, tests, exact answers, or resettable sandboxes to pro-vide cheap verification (Jimenez et al., 2023; Hendrycks et al., 2021), enterprise feedback depends on dedicated proprietary environments and organization-specific outcomes, making evaluation and diagnosis substantially more expensive (Xu et al., 2024). Long-term memory offers a training-free way to close this residual knowledge gap without changing model parameters (Sumers et al., 2023; Du et al., 2025). Episodic memory preserves what happened in a particular rollout; semantic memory stores general facts or concepts. Procedural memory, in contrast, captures how to act under particular conditions (Tulving, 1972; Cohen & Squire, 1980;

Its unit is not a transcript or isolated statement but reusable guidance specifying when a procedure applies, which actions and implementation details carry it out, what can fail, and how to recover. Consequently, procedural memory requires consolidation rather than naive accumulation: repeated successes should be merged, contradictions resolved, and diagnosed failures incorporated as missing preconditions or troubleshooting paths (Chen et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2024; Fang et al., 2025). Without such consolidation, raw sessions accumulate redundancy and noise without becoming accurate, robust knowledge that generalizes across recurring task instances. Formally, this process is a multi-round execution–consolidation cycle (Figure 1a). During the ex-ecution phase, given a task, the agent retrieves zero or more relevant memory pages, acts on the environment through its tools, and records its actions and observations as a raw rollout trace. An evaluator or human then provides feedback based on the resulting environment state. During the consolidation phase, a method reads the current store alongside eligible traces and feedback, and abstracts them into reusable procedural knowledge: when a procedure applies, which actions to per-form, what failures may arise, and how to diagnose and resolve them. The method then writes or merges this knowledge according to the memory system’s representation and interaction interface. One execution pass followed by consolidation forms a round; only the updated memory store carries into the next round, while the model parameters and harness remain fixed. Agent memory is a rapidly expanding design space, with new representations, extraction rules, re-trieval mechanisms, and update procedures appearing continuously (Du et al., 2025; ang Gao et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Ouyang et al., 2025; Hu et al., 2026; Wang et al., 2026). Choosing among overwhelming candidates resembles a classic Secretary Problem (Ferguson, 1989) with no known stopping rule, and exhaustive evaluation is infeasible. Practitioners instead need higher-level prin-ciples explaining which design dimensions matter, where human and system effort produces the greatest return, and which interventions merely add constraints or volume. We organize our study around three questions: (1) How should execution experience be consolidated into reusable proce-dural memory? (2) Which feedback, trace ingestion scope, and refinement choices most improve future execution? (3) Where should human effort be spent: prescribing knowledge organization upfront or diagnosing failures reactively? 

Through controlled experiments and qualitative analysis, we derive actionable answers to these ques-tions. Our contributions are threefold: • Nimble but Effective Consolidation Method. We introduce Wiki-based Agent-Synthesized 

Procedural Memory ( WASP-Mem ), an agentic procedural-memory organizer for training-free continual learning. It consolidates agent execution traces and human feedback into persistent, interlinked wiki pages, achieving both the most compact knowledge store and the strongest down-stream task performance among all compared methods (Figure 1b). • Controlled Study. To determine what makes procedural-memory consolidation effective, we systematically vary consolidation method, feedback type, trace-ingestion scope, and refinement rounds across three models, identifying which interventions most improve future execution. 2Preprint • Learnings From a Bitter Lesson. We distill our findings into a design principle: build a general-purpose memory system, but let the agent self-organize its procedural knowledge. Imposing domain-specific structure on procedural knowledge at design time harms performance. Instead, human intervention is most effective when applied reactively—through failure diagnosis, targeted feedback, and iterative refinement. 

2 RELATED WORK 

Procedural memory for continual learning. Many long-term agent-memory systems focus on episodic or semantic memories and are evaluated through conversational or question-answering re-call (Packer et al., 2023; Chhikara et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2025; Sumers et al., 2023; Du et al., 2025), rather than reusable procedural knowledge. Procedural approaches broadly follow three patterns. Case-based methods retain and retrieve trajectories or task-local reflections (Shinn et al., 2023; Zhou et al., 2025); these preserve exact evidence but are case-specific, sensitive data leakage risky and ac-cumulate redundancy without cross-session consolidation. Abstractive methods distill traces into reusable insights (Zhao et al., 2023; Fu et al., 2024; Hu et al., 2026) but processes rollouts indepen-dently, without cross-session deduplication or verification. Skill-based methods induce workflows, scripts, or hierarchical routines (Wang et al., 2023; 2024; Fang et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026), yet reusable components can be duplicated across skills and conflicts may go unresolved. In contrast, for WASP-Mem , agent synthesizes interlinked procedural pages at different granularities, and con-textually deduplicates and reconciles knowledge. The resulting memory is compact navigable, and readable by both agents and humans. 

Benchmarks for enterprise procedural learning. Suitable benchmarks are scarce because they must expose proprietary-knowledge gaps, target enterprise automation rather than coding or math-ematics, provide stateful tools, include enough related tasks for procedural transfer, and offer exe-cution annotations finer than task-level success. TheAgentCompany provides multi-service profes-sional workflows and checkpoint-level grading (Xu et al., 2024), while τ 2-Bench provides domain policies, tool APIs, verifiable state transitions, and customer-support tasks (Barres et al., 2025). SaaS-Bench broadens the setting to larger scale but its strongest reported agent completes fewer than 4% of tasks end-to-end (Shi et al., 2026) and package was also unavailable during our experi-ments. We adopt TheAgentCompany and τ 2-Bench: agents generate rollouts and feedback on train-ing tasks, each method consolidates them into memory, and the agent with frozen store is evaluated on held-out test tasks. We judge memory methods by downstream checkpoint and task completion, rather than recall of stored experience. 

3 METHOD 

We target recurring enterprise workflows in which a task attempt can modify a shared environment and relevant organization-specific procedures may be absent from the initial agent harness. Let Mr

denote the persistent memory store at round r, Br a batch of execution traces, Fr the corresponding environment or human feedback, and S the ingestion scope governing which traces and feedback are eligible for consolidation. A consolidation method C updates the store as 

Mr+1 = C(Mr , B r , F r ; S). (1) The goal is to capture residual procedural knowledge not already encoded in the fixed harness. Con-solidation transforms many rollout-specific observations into fewer reusable procedures by merging repeated evidence, correcting inaccurate knowledge, and retaining distinct lessons when their con-ditions or remedies differ. The model parameters, harness, and executor loop remain fixed; methods differ only in their memory representation and consolidation operation. After consolidation on train-ing tasks, the store is frozen and evaluated by the checkpoint and task completion rates the agent achieves on test tasks. 3.1 WASP-Mem CONSOLIDATION METHOD 

WASP-Mem is a memory-consolidation and retrieval method for training-free continual learning that organizes execution traces and feedback into persistent, interlinked procedural wiki pages at 3Preprint different granularities and retrieves them to guide future execution, drawing on the idea of an LLM-maintained wiki as a personal knowledge base (Karpathy, 2026). WASP-Mem initializes a wiki filesystem with no initial procedural content and mandatory control files such as index.md , which serves as a high-level router over the wiki pages. A WASP-Mem consolidator agent creates, orga-nizes, revises, or removes pages and their links during consolidation. Three interface operations are provided: • Ingestion. Given a raw agent-session .jsonl file and optional asynchronously produced natural-language feedback, the WASP-Mem agent spawns a subagent that reviews the session trace to identify actionable observations retrospectively, then uses them to add, update, or remove pages and links. • Query. During both consolidation and execution, agents begin from index.md and combine semantic search with shell navigation to locate relevant pages and follow inter-page relationships, such as related conditions, actions, and outcomes; the semantic-search index is rebuilt after each consolidation phase. • Lint. Prompt-based rules and scripts direct the agent to scrutinize generalizability, reusability, ambiguity, excessive page length, link integrity, and conflicts, and to determine whether pages should be split, merged, or relinked. 

WASP-Mem variants. Free Form is the basic WASP-Mem instantiation described above. Each page is markdown with a minimal metadata header, such as its title, brief description, and last-modified time; the consolidator decides the remaining page structure and content. Schema Form 

adds prescriptive, human-designed organization: pages must be Procedures, Actions, or Lessons, each with fixed fields and enumerated options. Both Free Form and Schema Form read the existing wiki before writing and therefore perform implicit, agent-managed deduplication. Schema Form-ED 

further adds explicit deduplication rules: each session is first processed independently into candidate pages, after which a separate normalization, matching, and merge stage consolidates candidates before committing them to the persistent store. Schema Form and Schema Form-ED thus represent increasing levels of upfront prescriptive knowledge organization. 3.2 CONTROLLED CONSOLIDATION DESIGN SPACE 

To identify which consolidation choices improve downstream execution and where human and sys-tem effort is most rewarding, we vary four dimensions systematically while holding the remaining factors fixed. 

Consolidation method. We compare the three WASP-Mem variants above with four baselines. No Memory executes without assistance from any long-term memory, while No Consolidation stores raw execution traces and retrieves the most similar trace without abstraction or integration. Insights 

distills each session into abstract procedural insights (Hu et al., 2026) and stores them in a flat collection, with no cross-session deduplication, verification, or reconciliation. SkillX constructs a knowledge base in the form of agent skills organized at three levels: planning, functional, and atomic (Wang et al., 2026). This hierarchy is prescriptive, analogous to imposing a page schema: each planning skill forms an independent procedural hierarchy, and reusable functional or atomic skills are not deduplicated into a shared pool across those hierarchies. 

Feedback type. Feedback determines what evidence the consolidator has for deciding whether an execution was correct and why it succeeded or failed. We construct a ladder from Self-Judge , which uses only the agent’s own assessment, to external Checkpoint-binary feedback that localizes success and failure to individual checkpoints within the task, and Checkpoint-binary + Human feedback that additionally provides natural-language diagnosis. This separates the value of an external outcome signal from the value of diagnostic explanation. 

Ingestion scope. Ingestion scope determines which experiences are eligible for consolidation. 

Success-only admits evidence from successful executions, whereas Both also admits failed traces and their feedback. Failure traces become useful when outcome or diagnostic feedback distinguishes a reusable procedural lesson from incidental unsuccessful behavior. 4Preprint 

Refinement rounds. Refinement rounds control whether memory is consolidated once or repeat-edly revised as further execution traces become available. R0 serves as the no-memory baseline, R1 performs the first consolidation, and R2–R4 repeatedly execute against and update the same persis-tent store, allowing knowledge to be corroborated, corrected, or extended over time. Appendix B specifies the complete controlled matrix and protocols. 

4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

TheAgentCompany. Our primary controlled study uses TheAgentCompany (Xu et al., 2024), which contains 127 workplace tasks and 542 execution-based checkpoints across GitLab, Rock-etChat, OwnCloud, and Plane. Its six professional domains share tools, APIs, and workflows, pro-viding recurring procedural structure across tasks. The isolated services form a resettable testbed for state-changing workplace actions. To model recurring enterprise automation in which agents repeatedly encounter the same workflows, training and test sets contain the same 127 tasks. We enforced strict post-consolidation guardrails to prevent case-specific data leakage. Each condition scores one fresh attempt per task under the current memory store. 

τ 2-Bench Retail. We additionally use the retail domain of τ 2-Bench (Barres et al., 2025) to ex-amine in-domain generalization across disjoint train and test sets. We define a custom operating procedure that is absent from the agent harness and encode its requirements in the ground-truth ac-tion checkpoints. The disjoint train and test tasks share substantial procedural knowledge through common policy rules and tool workflows. The resulting benchmark contains 74 training tasks with 391 checkpoints and 40 test tasks with 192 checkpoints. Agents first execute the training tasks with-out access to the procedure. The resulting traces and checkpoint feedback are then used to construct each memory condition’s store, which is frozen and evaluated on the test tasks while the original procedure remains withheld. 

Models, harnesses, and environments. For both benchmarks, we report results with Claude Opus 4.6 (Anthropic, 2026), GPT-5.4 (OpenAI, 2026), and GLM-5 (GLM-5 Team, 2026). On TheAgent-Company, we use the pi-agent harness, which exposes bash as its only action tool for issuing API calls against self-hosted services in isolated Docker environments. Cohere Embed v4 provides 1024-dimensional embeddings for semantic search. For τ 2-Bench, we use its native function-calling agent and simulated customer environment. All memory methods receive the same training rollout batch, and LLM-based methods use the same consolidator model (Appendix E). 

Metrics and controls. Our primary metric is checkpoint pass rate : the fraction of binary, execution-grounded checkpoints passed. For τ 2-Bench, checkpoints are ground-truth action se-quences verified against the environment state. Fully-passed rate is the fraction of tasks satisfying all benchmark evaluation criteria. We also report memory-store size and consolidation API cost. The full four-dimensional controlled study is conducted on TheAgentCompany, while τ 2-Bench repeats the consolidation-method comparison under held-out in-domain generalization. Appendix C gives the complete matrix and exact results, and Appendix E reports implementation details and costs. 4.2 CONSOLIDATION METHOD

4.3 FEEDBACK TYPE AND INGESTION SCOPE 

This experiment asks how feedback richness and trace-ingestion scope jointly affect consolidation quality. It uses Free Form WASP-Mem consolidated in a single round (R1). Feedback varies from 

Self-Judge , to external Checkpoint-binary outcomes, to Checkpoint-binary + Human feedback with natural-language diagnosis. Within each feedback condition, we compare Success-only ingestion with Both , which also admits failed traces. The two scopes consolidate the same R0 rollout batch and receive the same subsequent execution budget, so their difference isolates the value of learning from failure. Feedback richness first determines the overall quality of the consolidated memory. When both successful and failed traces are ingested, checkpoint pass rate rises from 64.4–64.9% with Self-Judge to 72.3–72.7% with Checkpoint-binary and 75.8–77.1% with Checkpoint-binary + Human. External checkpoint outcomes improve performance by +7.4–8.1 pp over Self-Judge by correcting mistaken self-assessments and localizing unsuccessful actions. Human diagnosis adds a further +3.5–4.4 pp by explaining failure causes and corrective procedures that cannot be recovered reliably from a binary outcome alone. This benefit is concentrated in failed traces. 4.4 MULTI -R OUND REFINEMENT 

This experiment asks whether repeated execution and consolidation continue to close the residual procedural-knowledge gap. It uses Free Form WASP-Mem with Both ingestion scope, varies rounds from R0 through R4, and compares Self-Judge, Checkpoint-binary, and Checkpoint-binary + Hu-man feedback. R0 executes without memory; each subsequent round executes against the current frozen store and then consolidates the resulting traces and feedback. All reported results reflect fresh executions from that round rather than cumulative or best-over-round scores. With Checkpoint-binary + Human feedback, checkpoint pass rate increases monotonically for every model and reaches 85.6–86.7% at R4. The first consolidation round contributes +17.1–17.5 pp, yet the residual failures continue to reveal useful procedural gaps: R3 to R4 still adds +3.1–3.9 pp, with gains remaining non-trivial through the final round. Checkpoint-binary feedback reaches only 78.2–79.7% at R4, with its final-round change shrinking to −0.6 to +0.9 pp. Self-Judge is lower and non-monotonic, ending at 65.5–68.8%. Human diagnosis therefore does more than improve the first memory store; it repeatedly turns the remaining failures into new corrective knowledge. Feedback quality also matters more than additional rounds alone. After one Checkpoint-binary + Human round, checkpoint pass rate is already 75.8–77.1%, exceeding the corresponding Self-8Preprint Judge R4 result by 7.0–10.9 pp for every model. Four rounds of self-assessment do not recover the information supplied by one round of external outcomes and human failure diagnosis. Figure 3b shows how local checkpoint gains translate into end-to-end task success. For Claude Opus 4.6, fully passed tasks increase from 46 at R0 to 71, 79, 86, and 92 through R4. As repeated refine-ment makes earlier steps reliable and resolves later failure points, tasks shift from partial completion into full passes. 

Feedback Quality over Rollout Volume. Richer feedback on fewer traces outperforms sparse feedback on more rollouts; local checkpoint gains compound into end-to-end success. 

5 DISCUSSION 

Our study answers the three questions posed in the introduction and turns them into a design princi-ple for agentic continual learning. 

How should experience be consolidated? Experience should be transformed into shared, revis-able procedural memory rather than accumulated as independent traces or abstractions. By organiz-ing interlinked pages, WASP-Mem can merge and correct knowledge across sessions while preserv-ing useful relationships at different granularities. Free Form WASP-Mem improves over No Memory by 10.9–14.5 pp across all six model–benchmark settings with 92–255 KB stores—two orders of magnitude smaller than raw-trace retrieval. The value of memory lies in selective consolidation, not volume. 

Which feedback, ingestion, and refinement choices matter? Failure traces are useful primarily when feedback makes them interpretable. Execution-grounded outcomes localize failures, diagnosis explains the required correction, and iterative refinement turns newly exposed gaps into durable knowledge. One diagnostically supervised round outperforms four Self-Judge rounds by 7.0–10.9 pp: feedback quality matters more than rollout volume. 

Where should human effort be spent? General system design remains necessary: memory needs persistence, retrieval, update operations, navigation, and quality checks. Domain-specific organiza-tion, however, should be left to the agent rather than prescribed before the knowledge gap is ob-served. Human effort is better spent reactively diagnosing failures. Additional constraints should address demonstrated memory failures, not anticipated ones. 

WASP-Mem therefore contributes both a training-free mechanism for acquiring proprietary proce-dural knowledge and a practical principle: let the agent organize memory, and spend human effort on diagnosis. 

Limitations. Our controlled study uses two benchmarks, three models, and single-run configura-tions; variance across seeds remains unquantified (Appendix F). More broadly, enterprise procedu-ral automation remains less studied than code, mathematics, or personal assistants, and no available open benchmark combines stateful environments, proprietary procedures, and execution-grounded feedback at scale. Longer-term memory stability, broader enterprise environments, replication, and scalable automated diagnosis remain important directions for future work. 

6 CONCLUSION 

We introduced WASP-Mem , a training-free continual-learning method that consolidates execution traces and feedback into persistent, interlinked procedural wiki pages. Across two enterprise bench-marks and three model families, Free Form WASP-Mem leads or ties checkpoint pass rate in all six settings while maintaining compact stores. The controlled study further shows that prescribed schemas and explicit deduplication rules do not improve consolidation, that failed traces become useful as feedback becomes more diagnostic, and that one round with checkpoint outcomes and human diagnosis outperforms four rounds of self-judgment. Continued diagnosis produces larger 9Preprint and more sustainable improvements across refinement rounds, with local checkpoint corrections compounding into end-to-end task completion. The resulting design principle is simple: provide a general memory system, let the agent organize the procedural knowledge revealed by experience, and spend human effort reactively on diagnosing the failures that remain. 10 Preprint

LLM wiki variants. Free Form lets the consolidator inspect the current wiki, then decide what to record and whether to add a page or revise one. Schema Form retains this one-step, implicit dedu-plication process but requires three human-defined page types: Procedures, Actions, and Lessons. Schema Form-ED, short for Schema Form with explicit deduplication, uses the same page schema 16 Preprint but first extracts incremental pages without seeing existing content. A deterministic script then matches page identifiers and asks the LLM to execute the selected merges. 

Implicit and explicit deduplication. The two settings differ in who decides that a new page and an existing page describe the same thing. Under implicit deduplication, used by Free Form and Schema Form, consolidation is a single stage. The consolidator sees the current wiki together with the new evidence. Deciding whether that evidence extends an existing page or warrants a new one is part of the same judgment that writes the page. Under explicit deduplication, used by Schema Form-ED, consolidation is two stages. The consolidator first writes pages for the new evidence with no sight of the current wiki, producing an incremental wiki. A deterministic script then normalizes every page identifier and treats two pages as duplicates when their normalized identifiers agree, for example mapping any page describing a shell invocation of the Python interpreter to the same action identifier regardless of what that invocation was for. Only the selected pairs are passed back to the LLM, whose role is reduced to merging two pages it is told belong together. 

Page linking. Every wiki variant is reached from a single index.md entry point and connected by Obsidian-style [[wikilinks]] , so that retrieval can start at the index and walk to related content. The variants differ in what the links are allowed to express. Free Form places links wherever the consolidator judges two pages related, and the resulting graph is unconstrained. The two schema variants inherit a fixed link grammar from the page types: a Procedure links to every Action it invokes, and a Lesson attaches to the single Procedure or Action whose failure it describes. Actions do not link to one another, so a procedure’s steps are recoverable only through the Procedure page that lists them. E IMPLEMENTATION AND REPRODUCIBILITY 

Environment. Each task ran in a Docker environment connected to self-hosted GitLab, Rock-etChat, OwnCloud, and Plane services. We used pi-agent v0.75.3 as the fixed executor harness and exposed bash as its only action tool. All service interaction, including API calls, was therefore issued through shell commands. Every attempt was launched with --no-session , so the har-ness wrote no session state to disk and nothing it might otherwise retain could carry across tasks or rounds. Self-hosted services were confirmed running before each execution batch. 

Environment reset between attempts. Models and retrieval. Claude Opus 4.6, GPT-5.4, and GLM-5 are the executor-consolidator model variants. Claude Sonnet 4.6 performs evaluation, and Cohere Embed v4 provides 1024-dimensional semantic-search embeddings. F LIMITATIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

Section 6 lists the bounds on this study. Three of them constrain the conclusions most, and each is taken up here with the experiment that would lift it. 

Single runs. Agent execution is stochastic, but each configuration was executed once. Large gaps motivate the paper’s conclusions. Small differences should be treated as unresolved until replicated, among them the 0.2 to 1.7 pp between Task-binary and Checkpoint-binary (Table 8) and the 0.8 to 1.6 pp between the best fully-passed rates in Experiment 1 (Table 7). Stateful long-horizon execution makes exhaustive replication costly, but cost does not remove the need to replicate the central contrasts. 

Repeated-task distribution. R1 to R4 re-executed the same benchmark tasks. The experiment measures deepening competence over recurring procedural workloads, not unrestricted generaliza-tion to unseen distributions. Held-out task families and cross-domain transfer are necessary to mea-sure how broadly the learned procedures apply. 23 Preprint 

Human diagnosis. Checkpoint-binary + Human provides the strongest feedback but requires human effort after execution. Automating failure diagnosis without reverting to unreliable self-judgment is the most direct route to scaling the approach. 24
