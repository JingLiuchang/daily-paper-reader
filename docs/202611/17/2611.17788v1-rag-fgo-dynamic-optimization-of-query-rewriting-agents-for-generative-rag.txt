Abstract—In retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), query
rewriting serves as a core interface that aligns user intent with ex-
ternal knowledge bases, and its effectiveness directly determines
overall system performance. However, generative retrieval faces
three critical challenges in real-world deployment: noisy retrieval
feedback, the difficulty of jointly optimizing discrete prompt
templates and continuous decoding-control parameters, and the
limited generalization of static rewriting strategies under seman-
tically complex scenarios. To address these issues, we propose
RAG-FGO, a dynamic optimization framework for generative
retrieval strategies. RAG-FGO formulates query rewriting as a
non-differentiable black-box optimization problem in a mixed
discrete–continuous space, and represents prompt templates and
decoding parameters as adaptively optimizable query-rewriting
agents. The framework performs global search to enable semantic
jumps and structural reorganization that escape local optima,
while applying local perturbations for fine-grained alignment and
precise refinement. In addition, RAG-FGO introduces an elite-
preserving memory pool that accumulates high-quality strategies
across iterations, thereby mitigating the impact of stochasticity
and noisy feedback on optimization stability. Experimental results
on a diverse suite of benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness
and robustness of the proposed method, supporting its applica-
bility to complex semantic retrieval tasks.

I. INTRODUCTION
Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) integrates external
knowledge sources into the generation process by retrieving
relevant evidence from a knowledge corpus and injecting it
into the model context, thereby explicitly coupling language
models with external memory. As a result, RAG has become
a core paradigm for knowledge-intensive tasks such as open-
domain question answering, fact verification, and domain-
specific text generation [1]–[4]. Compared to generation meth-
ods that rely solely on parametric memory, RAG dynamically
incorporates traceable evidence at inference time, providing
factual grounding for generated outputs. This design reduces

tion, which makes strategy optimization non-trivial. Moreover,
retrieval quality is often assessed through indirect or proxy
feedback signals that are noisy and may drift across queries
and domains, rendering strategy selection and optimization
susceptible to noise and non-stationary distributions [13],
[14]. As a result, static rewriting strategies frequently fail to
generalize under semantically complex scenarios.
Motivated by these observations, we adopt a different mod-
eling perspective: Instead of treating generative retrieval as a
one-shot query rewriting process, we reformulate it as an opti-
mization problem over a retrieval strategy space. Specifically,
we represent a retrieval strategy as query-rewriting agents
jointly parameterized by discrete prompt templates and con-
tinuous decoding parameters. The prompt template defines the
structural form of query rewriting, while decoding parameters
(e.g., temperature, top-p, and repetition penalty) control the
generation distribution and output characteristics. Given a user
query, our query-rewriting agents generate rewritten queries
for retrieval. Their performance is evaluated based on the
semantic similarity between the retrieved results and the target
information need. Since strategy performance can only be
assessed through randomly generated queries and their corre-
sponding retrieval outcomes, and this evaluation involves non-
differentiable retrieval operators and sampling randomness, the
optimization problem exhibits a mixed discrete–continuous,
noisy, black-box nature that is difficult to solve using gradient-
based methods [15], [16]. Consequently, strategies based on
static or purely local adjustments often fail to sufficiently
explore the strategy space and struggle to achieve stable
improvements under noisy feedback.
To address these challenges, we propose RAG-FGO, a
dynamic optimization framework for query-rewriting agents.
Inspired by the Fungal Growth Optimizer (FGO) [17], RAG-
FGO combines global exploration with local refinement to
enable effective search in complex and non-stationary strategy
spaces. Figure 1 illustrates this exploration dynamic in the
retrieval strategy space. Global operations facilitate semantic
jumps and structural reorganization across distant regions of
the strategy space, while local operations perform fine-grained
perturbations and convergent optimization over prompt seman-
tics and decoding parameters. Furthermore, we introduce an
elite-preserving memory pool that accumulates high-quality
query-rewriting agents across iterations. This mechanism mit-
igates strategy degradation caused by generation stochasticity
and noisy retrieval feedback, thereby significantly improving
optimization stability. We conduct systematic evaluations on
multiple knowledge-intensive question answering and reason-
ing benchmarks. Experimental results demonstrate that, under
identical model size and retrieval budget constraints, RAG-
FGO consistently improves retrieval quality and downstream
task performance, and exhibits stronger robustness and trans-
ferability across semantically complex and cross-task scenar-
ios. Our main contributions are summarized as follows:
• A novel optimization perspective for generative retrieval
that unifies prompt templates and decoding parameters
into a single mixed discrete–continuous query-rewriting
agent.
• The RAG-FGO dynamic optimization framework, which
facilitates stable and efficient exploration of the strategy
space through integrated local and global search, and an
elite memory mechanism.
• Extensive empirical evaluations across multiple question
answering and reasoning benchmarks, demonstrating the
proposed method’s effectiveness and generalization capa-
bility in semantically complex scenarios.
II. RELATED WORK
A. RAG in Large Language Models
RAG [1] has become a widely adopted solution to the
knowledge limitations of LLMs by explicitly coupling gen-
eration with external evidence retrieval. This enables more
grounded and verifiable outputs in knowledge-intensive tasks
such as open-domain question answering and factual ver-
ification. Prior work has advanced RAG along two main
directions: enhancing retrieval quality through dense retriev-
ers [7], [18], late-interaction models like ColBERT [19], and
scalable infrastructures; and improving evidence fusion via
encoder–decoder designs and retrieval-aware generation [20],
[21]. Recent efforts further explore multi-stage pipelines [22],
reinforcement-learning-based adaptive retrieval [23], [24], and
long-context reasoning for larger evidence windows [25].
More recently, generative retrieval has emerged as a key
extension, where LLMs actively rewrite or generate queries
to better align user intent with knowledge-base representa-
tions [26]. Representative methods include hypothetical doc-
ument generation, such as HyDE [9], as well as LLM-
based query rewriting and expansion [27]. These approaches
introduce a controllable semantic transformation space and
demonstrate stronger end-to-end performance on ambiguous
or compositional queries.
However, existing generative retrieval methods typically rely
on static prompt templates and one-shot generation, without
modeling query formulation as an optimizable process. Fixed
configurations often result in redundant or mismatched re-
trieval [8], limiting adaptation to semantic variability across
tasks, query types, or domains. In contrast to prior work
that treats query generation as a fixed preprocessing step,
our approach reframes it as a learnable retrieval-agent search
problem (jointly optimizing discrete prompt structures and
continuous decoding controls under downstream feedback) to
enable dynamic generative retrieval.
B. Heuristic Search Algorithms
Heuristic and bio-inspired search algorithms are classical
yet effective tools for tackling high-dimensional and non-
convex optimization problems, especially when the objective
function is expensive, discontinuous, or lacks analytical gra-
dients [28]–[30]. With the increasing reliance on black-box
LLMs, similar optimization challenges naturally arise in LLM-
related settings, where gradients are unavailable, unreliable, or
practically inaccessible due to discrete prompt structures and
stochastic generation. Consequently, gradient-free search has
become a practical paradigm for improving LLM behaviors
under indirect supervision signals.


3
Existing studies have explored a variety of heuristic strate-
gies for prompt-related optimization. A representative line
of work focuses on prompt evolution, leveraging genetic
algorithms, evolutionary strategies, or population-based search
to refine prompt candidates through iterative mutation and
selection [13], [31], [32]. Another line investigates gradient-
free prompt discovery or selection mechanisms, including
discrete trigger search and black-box prompt construction [33],
[34]. Reinforcement-learning-based approaches further model
prompting as sequential decision making, where prompts or
actions are optimized according to task rewards [35]. Beyond
prompt structures, heuristic optimizers have also been applied
to generation-time control, where swarm-intelligence methods
such as PSO or GA tune decoding parameters or steer gener-
ation behavior without backpropagation [36], [37].
Among these families, bio-inspired methods are partic-
ularly attractive because they tend to exhibit a favorable
balance between broad exploration and fine-grained local
refinement, making them robust to multi-modality and noisy
feedback [38]. The FGO [17], for instance, mimics hyphal
expansion and branching to coordinate local and global search,
enabling efficient navigation of complex landscapes. This
search characteristic is well aligned with retrieval-agent op-
timization in RAG, where effective strategies often require
both non-trivial semantic shifts (to escape suboptimal query
formulations) and controlled refinements (to preserve intent
and stability). Motivated by this alignment, we adapt FGO
to the RAG setting and develop a dynamic retrieval-agent
optimization framework that iteratively improves query refor-
mulation strategies via structured exploration–exploitation in
the strategy space.
III. METHOD
In this section, we present the proposed RAG-FGO frame-
work in detail. We begin by formalizing the problem of gen-
erative retrieval optimization and defining the query-rewriting
agents that jointly model prompt templates and decoding
parameters (Section III-1). We then provide an overview of
the overall framework and its optimization pipeline. (Sec-
tion III-2). Next, we describe the agent initialization strat-
egy and the scoring-and-selection mechanism used to eval-
uate and retain high-quality query-rewriting agents (Sec-
tion III-3 and Section III-4). We further elaborate on the
exploration–exploitation process, including local and global
search operations for agent evolution (Section III-5). Finally,
we introduce the elite-preserving memory pool mechanism,
describe agent accumulation and termination conditions, and
explain how the optimized query-rewriting agents are selected
and deployed for inference (Sections III-6 and III-7).

2) Overview of RAG-FGO Framework: Figure 2 illustrates
the key definitions and operational concepts of the proposed
RAG-FGO framework. The framework is designed for the dy-
namic optimization of query-rewriting agents, each comprising
a discrete prompt template and a set of continuous decoding
parameters (e.g., temperature, top-p, and maximum generation
length). Starting from a natural language query, the template
initialization module generates an initial population of diverse
agents. Each agent is used to generate a rewritten query, which
is then utilized to retrieve contextual information from an
external knowledge base. These retrieved results are compared
against reference contexts to evaluate the performance of each
agent based on semantic similarity. Following this evaluation,
the query-rewriting agents are ranked, and the top-performing
individuals are selected as the basis for subsequent iterative
refinement.
To navigate the complex retrieval strategy space, the frame-
work employs local search and global search in parallel, each
serving a distinct optimization objective. Global search utilizes
LLMs to restructure prompts entirely, facilitating large-scale
semantic shifts that allow the system to escape local optima
and discover new, high-efficiency zones. Conversely, local
search focuses on fine-grained exploitation within promis-
ing regions by introducing subtle variations—such as syn-
onym replacement or tone adjustment—to achieve precise
alignment. The high-quality query-rewriting agents identified
through these dual operations are then integrated into an elite-
preserving memory pool.
This elite-preserving memory pool serves to mitigate the
impact of retrieval noise and stochastic fluctuations by accu-
mulating historically effective strategies over successive itera-
tions. As the optimization progresses, the pool is continuously
updated to maintain a diverse yet high-performing population
of agents, providing a robust foundation for the next round
of search. This cycle of exploration and memory-enhanced
refinement continues until the system converges on a stable,
optimal configuration. Ultimately, the agent demonstrating the
highest retrieval performance is selected from the pool as the
final output, ensuring significant improvements in semantic
similarity and factual grounding. The detailed training work-
flow of RAG-FGO is illustrated in Figure 3.

IV. EXPERIMENTS
A. Experimental Setup
1) Datasets: We evaluate our methods on two groups of
benchmarks. QA benchmarks: LongBench [25] targets long-
context understanding with inputs up to 100K tokens. Hot-
potQA [39] is a multi-hop QA dataset over multiple supporting
documents. 2WikiMultihopQA [40] focuses on compositional
multi-hop questions in Wikipedia. MuSiQue [41] decomposes
multi-hop reasoning into single-hop questions. Natural Ques-
tions (NQ) [42] contains real queries with Wikipedia passages.
SQuAD [43] is a reading comprehension dataset with span-
based answers. StrategyQA [44] requires implicit reasoning
steps to answer strategy-based questions. AmbigQA [45] tar-
gets ambiguous queries that involve multiple potential inter-
pretations. PopQA [46] focuses on long-tail factual knowl-
edge with low-popularity entities. Reasoning benchmarks:
MATH500 [47] is a subset of the MATH dataset containing
500 challenging competition-level problems. MMLU-pro [48]
extends the standard MMLU benchmark with more profes-
sional and domain-specific tasks. GPQA [49] is a challenging
benchmark that evaluates graduate-level problem-solving in
science and engineering domains. DROP [50] is a reading
comprehension dataset requiring discrete numerical reason-
ing, such as addition, counting, subtraction, and comparison.
MuSR [51] is a benchmark for multi-step symbolic reasoning,
comprising two subsets: MuSR-op (operational reasoning) and
MuSR-ta (temporal and abstract reasoning).
2) Baselines: We compare RAG-FGO with three groups
of representative methods. Retrieval-based baselines: Direct
lets the LLM answer questions without retrieval. ReAct [52]
integrates CoT reasoning with retrieval tool use. Self-Act [53]
(Self-Ask with Search) decomposes a query into sub-questions
and retrieves evidence for each. Aflow [54] employs an
adaptive flow strategy that dynamically decides when to reason
or retrieve, making it effective for multi-hop QA. Reasoning-
based baselines: Zero-shot CoT elicits reasoning by adding
a trigger phrase (e.g., “Let us reason step by step”) to the
query without in-context exemplars. Few-shot CoT guides
reasoning by providing a small set of annotated exemplars,
allowing the model to learn from in-context demonstrations.
USC [55] generates multiple CoT reasoning paths and selects
the final answer by majority voting. CoT-WP [56] weights
reasoning paths by confidence scores from token-level proba-
bilities and aggregates them via weighted voting. LEAP [57]
enhances few-shot prompting by inducing errors on exem-
plars and using self-reflection to extract transferable reasoning
principles. ReFeri [58] recycles few-shot exemplars to score
and verify candidate outputs, selecting responses that are
both confident and contextually coherent without additional
training. Query Rewriting Baselines: We cover a range of
query rewriting baselines, from prompt-driven LLM rewriting
to supervised fine-tuning and advanced training paradigms.
We include LLM-based methods (LLM Rewrite, HyDE [9],
Query2Doc [59], and Crafting [60]) that generate retrieval-
oriented query variants via prompting. We also evaluate SFT,
which fine-tunes the rewriter on labeled data. In addition,
we consider stronger training-based approaches, including
RaFe (DPO) [61], RRR (PPO) [27], and Q-PRM [62]. These
methods optimize query rewriting through preference learning,
reward signals, or process-level supervision. All approaches
are evaluated under a unified setting on benchmarks that span
multi-hop reasoning, ambiguous queries, and open-domain
question answering.
3) LLM Generators: We evaluate the real-world perfor-
mance of RAG-FGO using a diverse range of proprietary
and open-source LLMs as backbones. These include: GPT-
4o-mini and GPT-4.1-nano, representing compact yet powerful
proprietary models; Qwen-Long and Qwen-Max, designed for
long-context reasoning and high-performance tasks; Qwen2.5-
7B-Instruct, a versatile open-source model used to assess
instruction-following and reasoning capabilities in practical
applications; and Gemma2-27B, a large-scale open-source
model optimized for both efficiency and accuracy. We utilize
these backbone models to test the end-to-end performance
of our optimized query-rewriting agents in realistic retrieval-
augmented generation scenarios.

4) Hyperparameter Sensitivity Analysis: To evaluate the
robustness of RAG-FGO with respect to its search-related
hyperparameters, we conduct a one-factor-at-a-time sensi-
tivity analysis on HotpotQA. We examine five parameters
that control the behavior of agent evolution: the minimum-
change threshold δ used in global perturbations, the local
and global perturbation scales (σl, σg), and the corresponding
range control coefficients (kl, kg). Each parameter is varied
independently within a practical neighborhood around the
default configuration, while all other settings are kept fixed.
For each setting, we report the relative change in answer-
level F1 on HotpotQA and the total token cost incurred
during optimization, normalized with respect to the default
configuration. The reported ranges reflect variability across
multiple independent runs (Table VIII). Overall, the results
indicate that RAG-FGO is relatively insensitive to moderate
variations of these hyperparameters, with performance changes
typically within ±1% in a broad neighborhood of the default
setting. Notably, more aggressive exploration settings (e.g.,
larger σg or kg) can yield marginal F1 improvements, but
at a substantially higher token cost. Conversely, overly con-
servative configurations reduce both cost and performance.
These trends suggest that the default hyperparameter values are
chosen as a balanced trade-off between retrieval effectiveness
and optimization efficiency, rather than as per-dataset or per-
task optimal settings.

V. CONCLUSION
In this work, we present RAG-FGO, a dynamic optimiza-
tion framework that reframes generative query rewriting in
retrieval-augmented generation as a joint optimization prob-
lem over discrete prompt templates and continuous decod-
ing parameters. By integrating fungal growth-inspired ex-
ploration–exploitation with an elite-preserving memory pool,
RAG-FGO effectively navigates noisy, non-stationary, mixed
discrete–continuous strategy spaces in real-world RAG sys-
tems. This study highlights two core lessons: treating query
reformulation as an iteratively learnable agent rather than a
fixed preprocessing step unlocks substantial gains in retrieval
quality under complex semantics; and balancing global se-
mantic jumps with local refinements, stabilized by historical
elite retention, is essential to overcome stochastic feedback and
escape suboptimal local optima. Looking forward, promising
directions include lightweight online adaptation for continu-
ous agent evolution during deployment, extension to multi-
modal knowledge bases and continual learning under distri-
bution shifts, and more efficient surrogate objectives or meta-
optimization to reduce computational overhead while retaining
robustness.
VI. LIMITATION
Despite its effectiveness, the proposed RAG-FGO frame-
work has several limitations. The optimization procedure is
conducted in an offline stage that requires an additional train-
ing budget prior to deployment, which may reduce flexibility
in scenarios involving rapid iteration or frequent updates.
Although the optimized query-rewriting agent operates ef-
ficiently at inference time with a single forward pass, the
current framework does not support online or continual adap-
tation to evolving queries or dynamically changing knowledge
bases. Future work will explore lightweight online adaptation
