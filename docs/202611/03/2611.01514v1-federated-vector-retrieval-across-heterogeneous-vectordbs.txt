## ABSTRACT 

Vector databases are now core infrastructure for AI retrieval sys-tems, yet many real deployments must serve queries over multiple independently constructed vectorDBs built with different embed-ding models. This creates a systems problem of federated retrieval over heterogeneous vectorDBs: vectors and similarity scores are not directly comparable across embedding spaces, while fully re-solving this incompatibility through corpus-wide re-embedding and re-indexing is often operationally prohibitive. Existing meth-ods therefore face a difficult trade-off between retrieval quality, construction cost, and query throughput. We present HVMap, a lightweight compatibility layer for federated retrieval across hetero-geneous vectorDBs. Instead of rebuilding databases into a unified space, HVMap learns bidirectional mappings between embedding spaces using a fuzzy contrastive loss, enabling calibrated cross-database scoring without corpus migration or index reconstruction. HVMap is system-agnostic and further scales to multi-vectorDB settings while preserving ranking fidelity through a hub-based ar-chitecture. We evaluate HVMap in open-domain, cross-domain, and scale-out settings, and also construct a new benchmark for hetero-geneous cross-domain federation. Across these settings, HVMap consistently achieves a better quality–cost–throughput trade-off than baselines, achieving up to 3.59 × higher nDCG@10, up to 2.01 ×

higher query throughput, and construction time as low as 0.3% of baseline methods. These results show that HVMap makes hetero-geneous vectorDB federation practical at scale.

Vector databases (vectorDBs) have become a core component of modern AI data infrastructure, supporting efficient similarity search over dense embeddings at scale. They are now widely used in applications such as semantic search, recommendation systems, and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) pipelines [2, 4, 27, 31, 34, 67]. In practice, this evolution rarely results in a single, unified vector space. Instead, many real-world systems maintain multiple vec-torDBs built with different embedding models. A production de-ployment may preserve legacy indexes while gradually adopting a new embedding model, or simultaneously serve multiple domains, each using a specialized encoder. Supporting a single query over such databases naturally leads to the problem of federated retrieval across heterogeneous vectorDBs : executing retrieval over multiple independently managed vectorDBs whose embeddings are not di-rectly comparable. In many production deployments, heteroge-neous vectorDBs are not an exception but an operational conse-quence of model upgrades, domain specialization, and incremental migration. A system design that assumes immediate corpus-wide re-embedding is therefore often impractical. This setting arises naturally in at least two common scenarios. First, Embedding Model Evolution and Upgrades is now routine. To achieve better semantic representation and retrieval performance, embedding models are frequently updated through better archi-tectures, larger training data, or higher embedding dimensions [32, 33, 38, 83]. However, such upgrades often change the geometry, dimensionality, or semantics of the embedding space, making newly generated embeddings incompatible with existing ones. As a result, arXiv:2611.01514v1 [cs.DB] 1 Nov 2026 organizations may need to operate multiple vectorDBs simultane-ously rather than re-embedding and re-indexing the entire corpus at once. For example, a system transitioning from a BERT-based model [59, 61] to a stronger GPT-based embedding model [48] may need to preserve access to both legacy and newly indexed data during the transition period. Second, Cross-Domain Knowledge Retrieval is also increasingly common [1, 21, 42, 50]. Many applications require retrieving in-formation across multiple specialized domains, each with its own domain-specific embedding model [5, 6, 18, 64, 71]. For example, a biomedical assistant may need to retrieve information jointly from genomics, pharmaceutical, and scientific literature databases, even when those corpora were embedded using different models optimized for different domains. From a database systems perspective, this is a new federation problem. Federated query processing has been extensively studied for structured databases [43, 44, 66], and recent work explores federation over vector databases [84, 85]. However, existing vector federation systems typically assume a shared embedding model and avoid the central challenge of heterogeneous settings: score comparability across incompatible embedding spaces . With different encoders, vectors cannot be directly compared and similarity scores are uncalibrated across databases. Na"ively merging top-𝐾 results therefore yields unstable global rankings and poor retrieval quality. The key question is whether heterogeneous embeddings can be aligned to preserve retrieval quality while respecting real vectorDB construction and serving constraints. A straightforward way to address this issue is to integrate all databases into a common embedding space by re-embedding the underlying corpus and rebuilding indexes using a unified model [28, 45, 63, 65, 82]. While this can ensure compatibility, large-scale re-embedding and re-indexing incur substantial offline cost, system disruption, and migration complexity, especially for large or contin-uously updated corpora [74, 80]. More importantly, heterogeneous vectorDB federation introduces a fundamental systems trade-off among three competing objectives: retrieval accuracy , construction cost , and query throughput . Aggressive integration can improve retrieval quality but increases construction overhead, whereas light-weight approaches that avoid integration reduce upfront cost but often sacrifice ranking quality or runtime efficiency. A practical heterogeneous federation mechanism must therefore achieve score compatibility without paying the full cost of database unification. To this end, in this paper, we propose HVMap (Heterogeneous 

Vector Map ping), a lightweight, system-agnostic compatibility layer that enables federated retrieval across heterogeneous vec-torDBs without corpus migration or index reconstruction. It learns bidirectional mappings between embedding spaces to support cross-database score comparison, integrates with retrieval pipelines, and scales to multiple databases through a hub-based design. At its core, 

HVMap uses a retrieval-oriented fuzzy contrastive objective to align semantically related vectors across incompatible spaces while accommodating ambiguity across models and domains. We evaluate HVMap in open-domain, cross-domain, and large-scale settings, and construct a benchmark of financial, biomedi-cal, and scientific literature because existing benchmarks do not capture heterogeneous cross-domain federated retrieval. Figure 1 shows that HVMap occupies a favorable region of the quality–cost– throughput trade-off space. Compared with existing alternatives, it achieves up to 3.59 × higher nDCG@10, reduces construction time to 0.3% of that required by baseline methods, and improves query throughput by up to 2.01 ×.Our contributions are summarized as follows. 

• We formulate federated retrieval across embedding-heterogeneous vectorDBs as a systems problem and identify cross-space score comparability as its central challenge. 

• We propose HVMap , a compatibility layer that preserves native vectors and indexes, combines bidirectional candidate transfor-mation with global ranking, and scales to multiple vectorDBs through a bounded-depth hub topology with batched execution. 

• We construct and publicly release the first benchmark for cross-domain retrieval across heterogeneous vectorDBs, supported by human validation and independent LLM-based evaluation. 

• We demonstrate that HVMap achieves a favorable trade-off among retrieval accuracy, construction cost, and query through-put across open-domain, cross-domain, and large-scale settings. 

• We characterize the operating conditions of bidirectional mapping-based federation by analyzing embedding-space structural simi-larity, capacity, training objective, and score weighting. 

## 2 MOTIVATION 

As vectorDBs evolve over time and across domains, federated re-trieval over heterogeneous vectorDBs becomes increasingly com-mon. Previous approaches such as compatible learning [25, 28, 53, 58, 65] and domain alignment methods [36, 62, 76, 81] have shown promise in representation alignment. However, they are not well suited to our target setting. Compatible learning typically assumes that a new embedding model is explicitly trained to remain com-patible with an existing embedding space, while domain alignment methods usually require projecting stored data into a shared space. In our setting, where heterogeneous vectorDBs have already been constructed independently, these assumptions are often unrealistic or operationally expensive. Recent work has explored more efficient integration techniques for heterogeneous vectorDBs [80], but this approach fundamentally assumes that the underlying databases can be integrated into a unified retrieval system. This assumption is often difficult to sat-isfy when vectorDBs are independently managed, deployed across separate systems, or specialized for different domains [19, 39, 60]. We therefore focus on a distinct systems problem: enabling fed-erated retrieval across heterogeneous vectorDBs without corpus migration or index reconstruction. The goal is not to unify all databases into a single embedding space, but to enable comparable scoring across autonomous vectorDBs while balancing retrieval accuracy, construction cost, and query throughput. This motivates 

HVMap , a lightweight compatibility layer that learns bidirectional mappings between embedding spaces and enables effective cross-database retrieval with modest construction and serving overhead.

## 3.3 Overview of HVMap 

Existing approaches struggle to balance construction cost, retrieval accuracy, and query throughput in federated retrieval. MIG pre-serves retrieval quality by unifying heterogeneous vectorDBs into a common space, but requires costly re-embedding and index recon-struction. In contrast, fusion-based federations avoid these construc-tion costs by querying each vectorDB independently, but suffer from limited score comparability and the overhead of running multiple embedding models at query time. HVMap addresses this trade-off through a lightweight compatibility layer based on bidirectional embedding-space mappings learned with fuzzy contrastive loss, enabling heterogeneous vectorDBs to remain physically separate while being exposed as a single logical retrieval system. 3.3.1 Budget under Skewed Relevance. Determining which sources to search and how many candidates to retrieve from each has long been studied as a resource-selection problem in federated retrieval [68, 69]. In particular, this problem becomes important when relevant documents are unevenly distributed across sources. 

HVMap handles such skew without imposing a fixed final quota on each vectorDB, while allowing every source to contribute up to all 

𝐾 final results. Given a requested output size 𝐾 , it retrieves up to 𝐾 

candidates from every participating vectorDB and globally reranks the resulting candidate pool using SCORE 2. Each source therefore has an equal opportunity to contribute all 𝐾 final results, and the fi-nal top-𝐾 documents may originate entirely from a single vectorDB even when relevance is highly skewed. Unlike interleaving-based aggregation, HVMap does not assume that relevant documents are evenly distributed across sources; Section 5.4.6 evaluates this property empirically. However, HVMap inherits the candidate-generation limitation of retrieve-and-rerank systems: documents excluded from a source’s local top-𝐾 cannot be recovered glob-ally. Retrieving 𝐾 candidates per source is a conservative default that does not restrict any source’s final contribution. For larger federations, adaptive resource selection could reduce retrieval and mapping overhead by allocating smaller budgets to unlikely sources. We leave this extension to future work.

In this design, one embedding space acts as the hub, while the remaining spaces serve as spokes. When a query requir-ing retrieval of the top-𝐾 relevant vectors arrives, it is first encoded by the hub embedding model 𝜙 𝑡 , yielding the query vector q𝑡 . The query vector is then propagated to each spoke embedding space via the corresponding mapping functions, following the red-arrow paths (Figure 3. 1 ), to produce query representations in all embed-ding spaces. Once the query vectors are available in every space, each of the four vectorDBs independently retrieves 𝐾 candidate vectors (Figure 3. 2 ), yielding 4 𝐾 candidates in total. To perform ranking, these vectors must be made comparable across embedding spaces. HVMap performs this through a two-stage gather-and-propagate process. First, the retrieved vectors from each spoke are mapped back to the hub space (blue arrows in Figure 3. 3 ), where they are gathered into a unified candidate pool. The entire candidate pool is then propagated from the hub to each spoke space in a single batch, excluding the vectors originally retrieved from that spoke (red arrows in Figure 3. 4 ). This ensures that every embedding space receives candidate vectors originating from all other spaces. Finally, the vectors and their corresponding queries are passed to the ranker, which extends SCORE 2 to select the final top-𝐾 documents. A fixed weighting provides a practi-cal default, while federation-specific tuning may further improve performance; see Section 6. 

3.4.2 Advantages of the Hub-Based Architecture. The hub-and-spoke design improves scalable federated retrieval in construction cost, accuracy, and throughput. 

Construction Cost. From the perspective of construction cost, the hub architecture significantly reduces the number of required mapping functions. To guarantee vector compatibility, transforma-tions must be supported between any pair of embedding spaces and the most intuitive approach is to maintain pairwise connec-tions among 𝑁 embedding spaces, which requires 𝑂 (𝑁 2) mapping functions, where 𝑁 denotes the number of vectorDBs. In contrast, the hub-based design composes transformations between two ar-bitrary embedding spaces 𝑠 and 𝑟 via the hub. As a result, only 

𝑂 (𝑁 ) mappings are required, keeping the construction overhead manageable even as the number of vectorDBs increases. Moreover, choosing a lightweight embedding model as the hub can further reduce mapping function training time, since the hidden dimension of each mapping function is determined solely by the input and output embedding dimensions. 

Retrieval Accuracy. The hub-based architecture also bounds the propagation of mapping errors, since each mapping step may intro-duce a small amount of distortion. Any cross-space transformation requires at most two steps (spoke → hub → spoke), limiting the accumulation of such errors. By contrast, a linear chain of embed-ding spaces could lead to a mapping depth of up to 𝑁 − 1. Because transformations between non-hub spaces are mediated through the hub, the choice of hub embedding model can influence the final retrieval accuracy. A stronger hub model may improve alignment quality, although at the cost of higher query embedding latency. This design therefore allows system designers to select the hub model based on their preferred trade-off between accuracy and query efficiency. 1

Query Throughput. The hub-based architecture also improves query throughput in two respects. First, it allows a lightweight embedding model to be used at the hub, reducing the cost of query embedding. Second, it supports an efficient two-stage gather-and-propagate mechanism for cross-space vector transformation. Rather than sequentially exchanging vectors between every pair of spaces, HVMap first gathers retrieved vectors into the hub space and then redistributes them to the spokes. This design enables batch map-ping of the full set of 𝑁 𝐾 candidate vectors, thereby improving computational efficiency. More detailed empirical measurements and quantitative analysis are presented in Section 5.3.

## 4.1 Cross-Domain Query Generation Using LLM 

The source corpora comprise independently curated datasets from distinct knowledge domains, selected so that the generated queries require retrieval across domains. For each sample, one corpus from each domain-specific dataset is randomly selected and provided to an LLM, which generates a query requiring information from all se-lected corpora. This design ensures that no individual corpus alone is sufficient to fully answer the query. The benchmark is expanded by varying domain combinations to create cross-domain evalua-tion sets. Using OpenAI’s GPT-4o [51], we generate 500 queries for each benchmark set. The resulting datasets cover four domain com-binations: Financial–Science, Financial–Biomedical, Biomedical– Science, and Financial–Science–Biomedical. 

## 4.2 Validation of Relevant Corpus Set 

The corpora used to generate a query are not necessarily its most semantically relevant sources. We therefore refine each relevant corpus set through vector-based retrieval. For each domain, we construct a Chroma vectorDB [72] using OpenAI’s text-embedding-3-small model [52], retrieve additional candidate corpora for each generated query, and add a retrieved corpus when it is more relevant than an initially selected one. Because corpus-set expansion uses a single commercial embedding model, relevant corpora outside its retrieval pool may remain unjudged, potentially disadvantaging methods with different retrieval behavior. 

Human validation. To assess the consistency of the resulting labels, we randomly sampled 100 queries from each of the four cross-domain settings, yielding 400 queries and 2,564 associated query–document pairs. Three authors independently made binary relevance judgments for every pair. They labeled 99.0%, 98.8%, and 98.3% of the pairs as relevant, respectively, with a mean pairwise agreement of 99.1% and Fleiss’ 𝜅 = 0.64 [16]. ## 5.4 Ablation Studies 

5.4.1 Effectiveness of Fuzzy CL. To evaluate the fuzzy contrastive loss, we compare HVMap trained with Fuzzy CL, CL, MSE, and RKD combined with MSE (hereafter, RKD+MSE), following the original framework. Table 3 reports nDCG@10 across six open-domain datasets and three embedding-space federation scenarios. Fuzzy CL achieves the highest nDCG@10 in all 18 settings and significantly outperforms the strongest alternative in 13, while RKD+MSE consistently improves over MSE or CL alone. More importantly, the significant advantage over RKD+MSE in 13 of 18 settings indicates that Fuzzy CL is not merely a combination of pointwise and relational losses. Instead, it provides a unified, retrieval-aware objective that learns the cross-space score structure required by an unchanged target vectorDB, yielding more precise alignment than conventional CL or RKD+MSE.

5.4.3 Embedding-Space Similarity and Mapping Quality. We ana-lyze how embedding-space similarity affects mapping quality and retrieval accuracy. ## 6 LIMITATION & DISCUSSION 

Deployment assumptions. HVMap avoids corpus-wide migra-tion, but it requires offline training of mapping functions. This training uses paired embeddings generated by applying the partici-pating embedding models to the same auxiliary samples. Accord-ingly, HVMap assumes access to the embedding models or their APIs during construction. Deployment may be difficult when such access is unavailable due to privacy, licensing, or API restrictions. 

Score weighting and calibration. HVMap combines native-space and mapped-space similarities using a weighted SCORE function. Our analysis supports 𝑤 1 = 0.7 for the two-vectorDB setting, but a fixed weight may not be optimal for all deployments. In larger fed-erations, suitable weights may depend on the number of vectorDBs, the hub model, mapping quality, and embedding-space similarity. 

Cross-modal federation. HVMap is not inherently limited to text embeddings and could potentially support federation across modali-ties while preserving modality-specific representation spaces. How-ever, cross-modal mapping requires paired data, such as image– caption pairs, and may benefit from modality-specific mapping architectures or training objectives. We therefore leave general multimodal federation as future work. 

## 7 RELATED WORK 

Embedding Compatibility and vectorDB Integration. Prior work has extensively studied vector compatibility under embedding model updates and heterogeneous representation spaces [25, 28, 53, 58, 65]. Most methods enforce compatibility during training or project embeddings into a shared space, primarily treating the prob-lem from a representation-learning perspective. They therefore do

not explicitly address system-level concerns in federating indepen-dently constructed vectorDBs, including construction cost, query throughput, and scalability. More recently, integration-oriented methods have used locally isometric mappings to combine het-erogeneous vectorDBs [80]. However, they target database inte-gration rather than query-time federation and mainly report post-integration retrieval effectiveness, leaving integration overhead and scalability largely unexplored. 

Federated Retrieval Systems. Federated databases have long been studied as a classical topic in database systems, with the goal of integrating distinct database systems while preserving local au-tonomy [12, 66]. Prior work on federated relational databases has focused on issues such as query optimization [22], schema inte-gration [3, 46], and consistency management [24]. More recently, several studies have explored federated architectures for vector retrieval [84, 85]. These works primarily focus on distributed execu-tion, data privacy, or infrastructure-level concerns, rather than the compatibility problem caused by heterogeneous embedding spaces. In contrast, our work treats retrieval compatibility as a first-class systems problem and specifically targets federated retrieval across vectorDBs built on incompatible embedding models. 

## 8 CONCLUSION 

We presented HVMap, a lightweight compatibility layer for feder-ated retrieval across heterogeneous vectorDBs with incompatible embedding spaces. By learning bidirectional mappings with fuzzy contrastive loss, HVMap avoids costly corpus re-embedding and in-dex reconstruction while maintaining strong retrieval performance. Our results show that heterogeneous vectorDB federation is a sys-tems trade-off among retrieval quality, construction cost, and query throughput, and that HVMap provides a practical solution in this design space. Its hub-based architecture enables scalable retrieval across multiple vectorDBs without quadratic mapping complexity.
