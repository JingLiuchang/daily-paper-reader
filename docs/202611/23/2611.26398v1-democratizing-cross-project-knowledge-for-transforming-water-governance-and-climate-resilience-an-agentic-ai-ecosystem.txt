# Democratizing Cross-Project Knowledge for Transforming Water Governance and Climate Resilience: an Agentic AI Ecosystem 

Oriol Alàs 1*[0009-0001-6875-922X] , Ian Palacín 1*[0009-0000-0148-7437] , Iván Cester 1[0009-0005-6326-0146] and Edgar Rubión 1[0000-0003-3886-3170] 

> 1

Eurecat, Centre Tecnològic de Catalunya, Barcelona, Spain 

edgar.rubion@eurecat.org 

Abstract. EU-funded projects produce extensive evidence in the form of de-liverables, reports, and case studies. However, this knowledge is typically scat-tered across projects and document structures, making it difficult to locate rele-vant, contextual information when making decisions. This paper introduces an Agentic AI Ecosystem that consolidates heterogeneous project documentation into a domain-aware knowledge base and provides adaptive access through an agentic Retrieval-Augmented Generation pipeline. Documents from three EU-funded projects (IMPETUS, NATALIE, and InnWater) are reconstructed into hi-erarchical section trees and indexed with structured metadata. Retrieval is sup-ported by two domain-specific embedding models fine-tuned on domain data (water and climate). On top of this knowledge base, a ReAct-style controller it-eratively orchestrates retrieval actions by allocating retrieval budget across do-main collections and accumulating evidence until a stopping criterion is met. In a retrieval benchmark over the corpus, the proposed approach improves Mean Reciprocal Rank by 26.7% (relative) compared to a single-domain embedding baseline, indicating more consistent retrieval of relevant evidence to support cross-project knowledge synthesis and climate-resilient decision-making. 

Keywords: Agentic AI, Knowledge Democratization, Embedding Fine-Tuning, Water Governance. 

# 1 Introduction 

Horizon Europe and related EU programmes have generated substantial technical evi-dence for climate adaptation and water governance in the form of technical deliverables and reports. In practice, these outputs are distributed across projects and embedded in long documents with heterogeneous structures, which makes it costly for local author-ities, NGOs, and policymakers to identify the specific, contextual fragments needed for planning and decision-making. As recognized by the EU Climate Adaptation Strategy 1

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) provides a natural paradigm for querying such corpora by grounding model outputs in external sources [1]. However, standard one-shot RAG pipelines remain brittle in cross-project settings. Relevant evidence may be spread across multiple documents and projects, section-level context is often re-quired for correct interpretation, and user queries frequently span multiple domains (e.g., climate adaptation and water governance). Under these conditions, a single re-trieval call may either miss key context or surface partially relevant fragments without mechanisms to iteratively refine the search or consolidate evidence across sources [2]. Recent work has addressed adjacent limitations in two directions. First, hierarchical and tree-based retrieval methods build multi-level representations to retrieve both local de-tail and global context from long documents, mitigating the shortcomings of flat chunk-ing [3]. Second, agentic and adaptive RAG variants interleave reasoning with retrieval actions, deciding when to retrieve, refining queries or tool parameters, and accumulat-ing evidence before answering, rather than committing to a fixed evidence set from a single call [4]. Despite this progress, cross-project decision-support scenarios impose an additional constraint: retrieval must be both domain-aware and provenance-preserv-ing, with explicit control over how retrieval effort is allocated across heterogeneous project collections. Generic embedding models often underperform on specialized project corpora where key distinctions depend on domain terminology; synthetic query-passage generation can provide supervision for retriever adaptation when labeled data are scarce [5]. To train the models efficiently under practical compute constraints, parameter-efficient fine-tuning methods such as Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) offer a common strategy to update a small number of trainable parameters while keeping the base model largely frozen [6]. To address these limitations, we propose an Agentic AI Ecosystem for cross-project knowledge access, demonstrated on documentation from three EU-funded projects: IMPETUS 3, NATALIE 4, and InnWater 5. The ecosystem combines (i) a domain-aware knowledge base that reconstructs project documentation into hierarchical section trees and indexes fragments with structural and provenance metadata, and (ii) an agentic or-chestration layer that performs adaptive, multi-step retrieval over domain-specific col-lections. The main contributions of this work are: (1) a cross-project, domain-aware knowledge base that preserves document hierarchy and provenance metadata; (2) two domain-specific embedding spaces trained with evidence-grounded synthetic supervi-sion, improving the discoverability of policy-relevant knowledge expressed in project-

# 2 System Architecture 

The proposed Agentic AI Ecosystem is designed to enable contextual, cross-project knowledge access across heterogeneous climate and water governance documentation. The architecture comprises two tightly coupled components: (i) a domain-aware knowledge database, responsible for ingesting, structuring, and indexing project docu-mentation and (ii) a ReAct-style agent orchestration layer, which dynamically coordi-nates retrieval actions across domain-specific collections. 

2.1 Domain-aware Knowledge Database 

The ingestion pipeline turns unstructured and heterogeneous project documentation into a structured, section-aware knowledge base that preserves the original logical or-ganization of each document. Our approach explicitly reconstructs the document’s hi-erarchical structure and uses this structure as the backbone for downstream retrieval. The content is then indexed in a vector database using two domain-specific embedding models fine-tuned using project materials. 

Multimodal structure detection and traditional content extraction. 

Document ingestion employs a dual-parsing approach that combines AI-driven structural analysis with robust content extraction. A multimodal large language model (Google Gemini) is used to infer document structure through multimodal analysis, iden-tifying section hierarchies, heading levels and organizational patterns. This approach improves robustness when processing documents with inconsistent formatting, visual noise, or irregular layouts, where rule-based parsers typically fail. In parallel, OpenParse, an open-source library designed for document parsing, is used to extract raw textual content, tables, and visual elements while preserving layout information. This enables filtering of non-content artefacts such as headers or page numbers and ensures accurate content recovery. Outputs from both parsers are aligned and merged into a unified representation by matching structurally inferred sections with their cor-responding extracted content. 

Hierarchical Content Representation and Summarization 

The merged output is organized into a content-tree, where each node corresponds to a document section or subsection, and contains both raw content and associated 4metadata (project, document identifier and section path). To support retrieval at multi-ple levels of abstraction, the system generates hierarchical summaries using a depth-first search (DFS) post-order traversal. Leaf nodes are summarized directly from their raw text, while parent nodes recursively aggregate their own content with the summar-ies of their children. 

Domain Specific Embedding 

Generic pre-trained embedding models often fail to capture domain-specific termi-nology and relationships crucial for specialized retrieval tasks [7]. To address this lim-itation, we fine-tune two domain-specific embedding models (one for water governance and one for climate adaptation) that better align with the semantic spaces. 

Silver Dataset Construction 

To obtain supervision without manual labeling, a synthetic data generation pipeline was implemented to create high-quality question-answer-passage (QAP) triplets auto-matically. This pipeline utilizes Google Gemini to ingest raw project documentation and generate QAP samples only when they can be validated by verbatim evidence pre-sent in the text. Each generated entry consists of a) a concise question, b) a direct answer derived exclusively from the document content and c) extracted passage, consisting of exact quotes from the source text that serve as ground truth. Few-shot technique was applied to improve quality and style of the responses of the model. This process yielded 1064 high-quality triplets for the water domain and 846 for the climate domain.

This section evaluates the impact of domain-specific embedding fine-tuning by com-paring a generic embedding model within an information retrieval setup. The evalua-tion focuses on the distinction of the embedding space topology and their effect on downstream retrieval performance.

# 4 Conclusion 

By transforming static project documentation into a living knowledge ecosystem that evolves as new projects emerge, this work helps address the urgent need identified in EU policy frameworks to make research outputs accessible and actionable for local decision-makers, ultimately supporting the transition toward climate-resilient societies. Experimental results demonstrate a 26.7% relative improvement in MRR over a single-8domain baseline, confirming that domain alignment and hierarchical scoping materially enhance early retrieval precision and discriminative separation in the embedding space. Building upon these findings, future research will focus on expanding the ecosystem’s capabilities through three primary avenues. First, the system will be extended with multi-modal retrieval capabilities to support richer evidence grounding and multimodal retrieval. Second, the ecosystem will be extended to additional EU-funded projects and adjacent policy domains to assess scalability, cross-domain generalization and embed-ding transferability. Finally, human-in-the-loop evaluations are planned to assess the faithfulness and reasoning quality of the agentic responses in the real-word policy-mak-ing scenarios.
