# LSP-RAG: Latent Scoring Perspectives for Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Abstract —Large Language Models (LLMs) have demonstrated strong capabilities in open-domain question answering, but often struggle with factual accuracy and multi-hop reasoning due to the incompleteness of the training corpora. A promising solution is Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG), which supplements LLMs with structured knowledge retrieved from external knowledge graphs (KGs). However, existing KG-RAG methods either rely on large-scale language models (e.g., ChatGPT) to guide the retrieval process, which leads to high computational costs, or suffer from limited retrieval quality when using lightweight language models, particularly under multi-hop scenarios. We propose LSP-RAG (Latent Scoring Perspectives for Retrieval-Augmented Generation over Knowledge Graphs), a novel KG-RAG framework that enhances hop-wise KG knowledge retrieval through multi-perspective prompted scoring. Each latent scoring perspective is guided by a combination of two types of soft prompts: a perspective-specific soft prompt induces a distinct prompt-conditioned scoring function over candidate triplets, while a contextual soft prompt evolves with each reasoning hop by encoding the query and previously explored KG triplets, enabling the model to preserve consistency and relevance across multi-hop retrieval. Rather than assuming manually defined semantic roles, these latent perspectives are adaptively aggregated to capture heterogeneous relevance signals during retrieval. This design allows LSP-RAG to perform accurate and robust retrieval using lightweight language models. LSP-RAG achieves superior performance across multiple KG-based Question Answering benchmarks compared to existing retrieval systems, including those that rely on much larger language models, demonstrating its effectiveness under limited computational budgets.

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Large Language Models (LLMs) [1], [2] have demonstrated impressive capabilities across a wide range of natural language processing tasks, including text comprehension [3], open-domain question answering [4], and natural language gen-eration [5]. Despite these advances, LLMs remain limited due to the incompleteness of knowledge encoded during pretraining, which restricts their ability to provide factually accurate and up-to-date answers [6], [7]. This limitation is particularly pronounced in knowledge-intensive scenarios that demand factual precision, reasoning over complex multi-hop relations, and adaptation to continually evolving information [6], [7]. Consequently, LLM generations often contain hallucinated content, outdated facts, or unverifiable claims, undermining their reliability in real-world applications. To mitigate these issues, Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [8] has been proposed to enhance LLM reliability by incorporating external knowledge during inference. RAG allows models to access up-to-date and factual information beyond what was memorized during pretraining. While early RAG systems primarily relied on unstructured text corpora, recent efforts have explored structured sources of knowledge for more precise and interpretable reasoning. Among these, Knowledge Graphs (KGs) offer a highly structured and semantically rich format, representing facts as triplets in the format of 

(subject , relation , object ). This format supports explicit relation modeling and multi-hop relational reasoning, making KGs especially suitable for question answering tasks that require factual consistency and transparency [9], [10]. In the KG-RAG paradigm [11], models typically follow a two-step process: (1) knowledge retrieval , where a subgraph containing query-relevant triplets is constructed by expanding from entities appearing in the query; and (2) prompt integration , where triplets in the retrieved subgraph are presented as input to the LLM as evidence for answer generation. The performance of KG-RAG methods largely relies on the ability to retrieve and rank relevant triplets effectively, especially under multi-hop settings where irrelevant information can accumulate and mislead reasoning. Despite recent progress, current KG-RAG approaches still face limitations that hinder their effectiveness. First, most methods assess candidate triplet relevance through a single lens, such as semantic similarity or prompt-based scoring [9], [12]. This rigid design fails to accommodate heterogeneous relevance signals across different candidate triplets, lacking the flexibility to account for diverse relational semantics [13]. A unified scoring strategy cannot effectively handle such diversity, leading to suboptimal triplet selection. Second, many candidate triplets may appear irrelevant when judged in isolation, especially those involving underspecified relations, but actually play a critical role in connecting earlier reasoning steps to the final answer. Existing methods typically treat each KG triplet independently and fail to model such multi-hop dependencies [14], [15]. As a result, they struggle to maintain contextual continuity, often discarding useful evidence prematurely. Third, while most recent systems attempt to address these issues by leveraging large-scale language models such as ChatGPT to guide retrieval, which are computationally expensive and impractical for scalable or real-time applications [16]. To address these challenges, we propose LSP-RAG, a lightweight but effective KG-RAG framework that enables lightweight language models to perform accurate and multi-hop retrieval. LSP-RAG contains a multi-perspective prompted scoring module, which scores candidate KG triplets based on the query and previously explored knowledge. Each latent scoring perspective is parameterized by a learnable perspective-specific soft prompt , inducing a distinct prompt-conditioned scoring function over candidate triplets. Since supervision is provided at the level of triplet relevance rather than manually defined perspective labels, these latent scoring perspectives are not intended to correspond to predefined semantic categories. Instead, they serve as complementary scoring components that are adaptively aggregated for flexible relevance estimation. In addition, LSP-RAG incorporates a contextual soft prompt that is dynamically constructed from the triplets in previous reasoning steps. This prompt provides evolving contextual signals that help the scoring of new candidate triplets using earlier reasoning steps, allowing the model to assess each candidate not in isolation, but in light of the evidence already accumulated, addressing the limitation of prior work. By combining latent scoring perspectives with contextual adaptation, our model robustly filters irrelevant or noisy triplets. Notably, this frame-work is fully compatible with lightweight models, avoiding the reliance on expensive LLMs such as ChatGPT for retrieval, thereby offering both efficiency and strong performance. > •

LSP-RAG We propose LSP-RAG, a compact and adaptive framework for Knowledge Graph Retrieval-Augmented Generation (KG-RAG), designed to enhance multi-hop reasoning and knowledge retrieval using lightweight language models. LSP-RAG operates in a multi-hop retrieval setting, where relevant triplets from the knowledge graph are selected iteratively based on both the input query q and the reasoning context of explored knowledge. As shown in Figure 2, the framework introduces two key components: (1) a multi-perspective prompted scoring module, where multiple latent scoring perspectives guided by dis-tinct perspective-specific soft prompts provide complementary prompt-conditioned scoring functions; and (2) contextual soft prompts that are dynamically constructed from the query and previously explored knowledge, enabling hop-aware retrieval and maintaining coherence across multi-hop reasoning steps. These components work together to provide a flexible and context-sensitive evaluation of candidate triplets, allowing LSP-RAG to robustly retrieve useful knowledge without relying on enormous-scale LLMs.

B. Multi-Perspective Prompted Scoring 

To robustly evaluate candidate triplets in multi-hop retrieval, we design a compact neural scoring module based on multi-perspective prompted scoring with contextualized soft prompts. A soft prompt refers to a learnable continuous embedding prepended to the input of a language model, allowing efficient task adaptation without fine-tuning the model parameters. This module addresses two key challenges in KG-RAG by applying different soft prompts: (1) modeling heterogeneous relevance signals with multiple latent scoring perspectives, and (2) maintaining contextual coherence across hops. To this end, each latent scoring perspective is equipped with two types of trainable soft prompts: a perspective-specific soft prompt 

that parameterizes a prompt-conditioned scoring function, and a contextual soft prompt that evolves over retrieval steps by summarizing the perspective’s prior output representations. These components allow the model to flexibly score candidates while remaining sensitive to context evolution across hops. Each latent scoring perspective operates as an independent scorer built on a shared, lightweight language model. We evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed approach across multiple QA benchmarks. 

A. Datasets 

We evaluate our method on four Question Answering datasets: WebQuestionsSP (WebQSP) [17], WikiMovies [18], and the datasets of MetaQA [19] in both 1-hop and 2-hop settings. WebQSP contains 4,737 natural language questions annotated with corresponding SPARQL queries over Free-base [20], a broad-coverage knowledge graph. This dataset requires both single- and multi-hop structured reasoning and is commonly used to evaluate complex KG-based QA. WikiMovies is a domain-specific dataset focused on the movie domain, constructed from WikiData and Wikipedia, with around 100k question-answer pairs over 75,000 movie-related entities targeting factual reasoning over a curated movie KG. MetaQA builds upon WikiMovies by introducing paraphrased and more compositional questions. In the 1-hop setting, each question is grounded on a single KG fact, while the 2-hop version requires 2-hop inference to connect two supporting facts. Compared to WikiMovies, MetaQA includes more natural language variation and higher reasoning complexity, making it suitable for evaluating generalization under compositionality. Together, these datasets span both broad-domain and specialized-domain KGs, and cover reasoning from simple fact retrieval to multi-hop compositional queries. We follow the standard train/validation/test splits provided by each benchmark. 

B. Baselines 

We evaluate our method against a broad set of baselines. These baselines differ in how they retrieve and reason over KG evidence, ranging from retrieval-free prompting to iterative graph reasoning with large LLMs. In contrast, our method achieves effective multi-hop reasoning using lightweight mod-els. We evaluate our method against several widely-used and representative baselines. IO-prompt [1] uses ChatGPT-3.5 to directly generate answers without the assistance of any external evidence, often resulting in hallucinations or factual errors due to limited LLM internal knowledge. CoT-prompt [4] improves upon this by encouraging the model to generate intermediate reasoning steps, but its performance is still bounded by the LLM’s pretraining knowledge. Self-Consistency [21] further extends CoT by sampling multiple reasoning paths and selecting the most frequent answer, improving robustness but still lacking access to external facts. Sim-Retrieve [22] retrieves KG triplets by computing embedding similarity between questions and candidate triplets using LLaMA or Flan-T5. While simple and efficient, it often retrieves noisy or irrelevant facts, especially under multi-hop queries. G-Retriever [23] formulates graph retrieval as a prize objective to select a compact, relevant subgraph as context, improving scalability and reducing hallucinations. GNN-RAG [24] uses a GNN as a dense subgraph reasoner to retrieve answer candidates, extracts KG paths connecting question entities to candidates, verbalizes these paths, and feeds them to an LLM for final reasoning. This improves faithfulness without extra LLM calls for retrieval. GNN-Scoring introduces a graph neural network (GNN) with the same size as GNN-RAG to score and filter triplets with a lightweight language model encoding the query within the input of the GNN, but it still struggles with long-range dependencies and lacks semantic adaptability. 

Think-on-Graph (ToG) [9] improves retrieval reasoning by iteratively selecting and scoring subgraphs using LLaMA, Flan-T5, or ChatGPT-3.5, and generating answers with ChatGPT-3.5. While effective, it incurs a higher computational cost and still depends heavily on the size of LLMs. Reasoning on Graphs (RoG) [25] adopts a planning-retrieval-reasoning pipeline: it first generates KG-grounded relation-path plans, then retrieves instantiated reasoning paths from the KG to support faithful and interpretable reasoning. In contrast, our method uses lightweight models to score and retrieve, achieving accurate reasoning without relying on expensive models. 

C. Experimental Setup 

To evaluate the effectiveness of our proposed framework, we conduct experiments using ChatGPT-3.5 [26], LLaMA2-7B ,

LLaMA3-8B [27], and Flan-T5-3B [28] as backbone language models for retrieval and answering. In our framework, LLaMA and Flan-T5 are used as retriever models for scoring and selecting KG triplets, while ChatGPT-3.5 is used only during the final answer generation stage for some baselines. For triplet selection, the multi-perspective prompted scoring module is configured with K = 3 latent scoring perspectives by default. The multi-hop expansion process is controlled by a hop budget 

H = 1 for MetaQA-1hop and H = 2 for other datasets, balancing retrieval depth and noise. We cap the number of retrieved triplets to 20 per query to maintain prompt length constraints. Perspective-specific and contextual soft prompts are jointly used to condition perspective scoring and improve relevance estimation under multi-hop contexts. All experiments are conducted with 5 random runs, and we report the average accuracy to mitigate variance. 

D. Backbone Models 

Our retrieval module operates on top of lightweight pre-trained language models, enabling efficient and scalable scoring without relying on large generative models during retrieval. We experiment with three representative retriever backbones: LLaMA2-7B, LLaMA3-8B, and Flan-T5-3B. 

LLaMA. LLaMA [27] is a family of decoder-only transformer models pre-trained on large-scale public corpora. We adopt the 7B/8B variants of LLaMA2/3 as feature extractors for triplet scoring. In our setup, the LLaMA parameters are frozen, and only the soft prompts, projection layers, and lightweight multi-perspective scoring components are trained. As a decoder-only model, LLaMA encodes the concatenated prompt and triplet input into hidden representations, from which we use the latent output representation for downstream scoring. 

Flan-T5. Flan-T5 [28] is an instruction-tuned extension of the T5 encoder-decoder architecture. The Flan variant is trained on a large collection of prompting tasks, making it effective under instruction-style input formats. We use the 3B variant in encoder-only mode to encode the concatenated soft prompts, question, and candidate triplet. Similar to LLaMA, the backbone parameters of Flan-T5 are frozen throughout training, with only the soft prompts, projection layers, and lightweight scoring components updated. 

Soft Prompt Integration. For both model families, we prepend soft prompts as continuous embedding vectors at the input layer. This design does not modify the tokenizer or the internal architecture of the backbone language model. During training, gradients are propagated through the perspective-specific soft prompts, contextual soft prompts, projection layer, and adaptive aggregation module. This makes LSP-RAG compatible with different pre-trained language models while keeping the backbone frozen. 

E. Training Details 

We train all models using the Adam optimizer with learning rate 1 × 10 −4, batch size 20, and linear learning rate decay over 5 epochs. Prompt parameters are initialized randomly and trained from scratch. Each latent scoring perspective uses two separate prompt embeddings: one perspective-specific and one contextual. The underlying language models are kept frozen throughout training, and only the soft prompts, projection layers, and lightweight scoring components are updated. We train all experiments on 4 NVIDIA A100 GPUs with 80GB memory using mixed-precision training enabled via PyTorch. During inference, retrieval is performed up to H = 1 hop for the MetaQA-1hop dataset and H = 2 for others, with M = 20 

triplets selected per hop. We set the number of latent scoring perspectives to K = 3 . The dimension of the latent embeddings in our method is set to the same dimension as the retrieval LLM, i.e., 2,048 for Flan-T5-3B and 4,096 for LLaMA2/3. F. Evaluation Protocol 

We follow standard entity-level accuracy for all datasets, considering a prediction correct if it matches any of the gold answers. Each model is evaluated with 5 different random seeds, and we report the average accuracy and standard deviation across runs. For fair comparison, all baseline methods use the same KG and entity linker. The knowledge graph used for WikiMovies and WebQSP is provided by [29], while for MetaQA we use the set from [30]. ToG baselines are re-implemented with the same number of hops and selection budget as LSP-RAG. Our implementation is based on PyTorch and Python 3.11.7. We release our code at https://github.com/HaochenLiu2000/ LSP-RAG.

H. Parameter Study 

We further study the impact of the number of latent scoring perspectives K in the multi-perspective prompted scoring module, focusing on WebQSP and WikiMovies with both LLaMA2-7B and Flan-T5-3B as the LLMs for retrieval. The results in Figure 3 show a clear upward trend when increasing 

K from 1 to 3: the models consistently achieve better QA accuracy as multiple latent perspectives provide additional prompt-conditioned scoring capacity. However, the benefit quickly saturates beyond this point. In fact, while K = 4 

performs slightly better than K = 3 on WikiMovies, the improvement is marginal, and on WebQSP with LLaMA2-7B the accuracy at K = 4 even drops compared to K = 3 .This observation suggests that a moderate number of latent scoring perspectives is sufficient to provide flexible relevance estimation for effective triplet scoring. Using too many perspectives may introduce redundancy and increases training as well as inference cost, without yielding consistent performance gains. For this reason, we set K = 3 as the default configuration in our method, which achieves nearly the same accuracy as K = 4 while being more cost-effective and requiring lower computing cost. 

I. Ablation Study 

We examine the impact of removing two key components: the perspective-specific soft prompts and the contextual soft prompts. Results in Table II show that across all datasets and LLM settings, removing either consistently degrades performance. Without perspective-specific prompts, the model collapses multiple latent scoring perspectives into a less flexible scoring function, leading to weaker discrimination especially on multi-hop datasets such as WebQSP and MetaQA-2hop where heterogeneous relevance signals must be handled. Without contextual prompts, the model loses continuity across hops and struggles to select the right chain of evidence. This becomes severe in multi-hop reasoning QA settings. Notably, removing either type of prompt leads to consistent performance drops across both models. Removing perspective-specific soft prompts results in a larger degradation on both models, particularly on complex reasoning settings like WebQSP, indicating the importance of maintaining multiple prompt-conditioned scoring functions. In contrast, the removal of contextual soft prompts hurts both models on multi-hop datasets, highlighting the impor-tance of preserving inter-hop coherence for complex reasoning. Both components play complementary and indispensable roles in enabling robust multi-hop retrieval. V. R ELATED WORKS 

A. Retrieval-Augmented Generation 

Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) [31] combines LLMs with external memory to address knowledge staleness and hallucination issues. The framework retrieves relevant pas-sages from external corpora and conditions generation on both the input query and retrieved context. Early variants include end-to-end training approaches [32], dense passage retrieval opti-mization [33], and fusion-in-decoder architectures [34]. Recent advances have addressed key limitations through sophisticated retrieval strategies: Self-RAG [35] enables adaptive retrieval decisions through reflection tokens, RAPTOR [36] constructs hierarchical tree structures via recursive clustering, Anthropic’s Contextual Retrieval [37] reduces retrieval failures through con-textual chunk embedding, GraphRAG [38] leverages knowledge graphs for global reasoning, and CRAG [39] introduces retrieval quality assessment mechanisms. Other developments include HyDE [40] and agentic systems [41], [42]. However, most methods are text-centric and do not fully leverage structured knowledge graphs, limiting the reasoning transparency and multi-hop capability. 

B. Conditional Computation and Multi-View Scoring 

Mixture of Experts (MoE) has emerged as a powerful architecture for scaling model capacity while maintaining computational efficiency through conditional computation [43], [44]. Originally proposed to divide problem spaces into homogeneous regions handled by specialized sub-networks [45], MoE has been successfully adapted to large language models where different experts are routed conditionally based on the input. Recent advances in MoE architectures include Mixtral 8x7B [46], which demonstrated that sparse MoE models can outperform dense counterparts on knowledge-intensive tasks, and Switch Transformer [43], which scales to trillion-parameter models by routing tokens to different expert networks through learned gating functions. However, MoE models have been shown to excel at memorization-intensive tasks but struggle with complex reasoning compared to dense models [47], suggesting that conditional computation alone does not necessarily provide interpretable reasoning ability. In knowledge graph reasoning specifically, MoMoK [48] introduces relation-guided modality knowledge experts for multi-modal knowledge graph completion to handle different relational contexts and modality-specific signals. Different from conventional MoE architectures that rely on specialized expert networks, our work uses lightweight latent scoring perspectives induced by perspective-specific soft prompts. These perspectives are not designed as human-defined semantic experts; instead, they provide multiple prompt-conditioned scoring functions that are adaptively aggregated for KG triplet relevance estimation. This design preserves the efficiency benefits of lightweight conditional scoring while keeping the reasoning trace explicit through retrieved KG evidence paths, offering an effective way for KG-RAG. VI. L IMITATIONS 

Although LSP-RAG achieves strong retrieval and QA per-formance with lightweight language models, it still has several limitations. First, its computational cost increases with the number of latent scoring perspectives, since each candidate triplet is evaluated by multiple prompt-conditioned components. Second, LSP-RAG relies on hop-wise KG traversal with a fixed hop budget, which may miss evidence requiring long, indirect, or weakly connected reasoning paths. Third, our experiments mainly focus on relatively clean and well-structured KGs; extending the framework to incomplete, noisy, or large-scale heterogeneous graphs remains future work. Finally, latent scoring perspectives are not intended to correspond to human-defined semantic categories. The interpretability of LSP-RAG mainly comes from explicit KG triplets and hop-wise evidence paths, while controllable or semantically supervised scoring perspectives are left for future work. VII. C ONCLUSION 

In this work, we proposed a novel knowledge graph-augmented retrieval framework for LLM-based question an-swering called LSP-RAG. Our approach introduces a multi-perspective prompted scoring module that leverages both perspective-specific and contextual soft prompts to evaluate candidate triplets, enabling robust multi-hop retrieval across knowledge graphs. Through the designed hop-aware retrieval and selection process, our method ensures contextual and adaptive evidence exploration while maintaining efficiency. Importantly, the design does not require fine-tuning large backbone language models; instead, it relies on a compact scoring architecture while still fully exploiting the capabilities of LLMs for final answer generation. Extensive experiments on the benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of our approach. It consistently outperforms retrieval-based baselines, including ToG/RoG, even when the latter are powered by larger models such as ChatGPT. Our ablation studies further confirm the critical roles of perspective-specific and contextual prompts in improving multi-hop retrieval performance. Overall, our retrieval and scoring mechanisms enhance the reasoning ability of LLMs without relying on large models. We believe that LSP-RAG provides a general and cost-effective paradigm for future research in knowledge-enhanced LLM reasoning. VIII. A CKNOWLEDGEMENT
