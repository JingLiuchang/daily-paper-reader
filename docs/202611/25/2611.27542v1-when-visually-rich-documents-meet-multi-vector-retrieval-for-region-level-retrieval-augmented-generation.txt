# When Visually Rich Documents Meet Multi-Vector Retrieval for Region-level Retrieval-Augmented Generation 

Xu Yuan, Liangbo Ning, Yujuan Ding, Qing Li *, Wenqi Fan *,

The Hong Kong Polytechnic University, HK SAR

Abstract 

Visually rich documents (VRDs) convey knowl-edge through textual, visual, and layout infor-mation that text-centric Retrieval-Augmented Generation (Text-RAG) struggles to fully ex-ploit. Recent Vision-RAG approaches enable retrieval and generation directly from docu-ment images, retaining this multimodal infor-mation. However, earlier Vision-RAG meth-ods commonly adopt coarse retrieval represen-tations and full-page visual contexts, which can obscure local evidence and introduce sub-stantial redundancy and computational over-head. To address these challenges, we pro-pose MV-VisRAG, a region-level Vision-RAG framework that uses semantically complete re-gions for both document representation and vi-sual augmentation. Specifically, MV-VisRAG first constructs these regions through a Content-Adaptive Regionalization module. It then en-codes them into local region vectors and a page-level global vector using a VLM-based retriever trained through multimodal recon-struction pretraining and retrieval fine-tuning. With this compact multi-vector representa-tion, MV-VisRAG performs late-interaction re-trieval and estimates region contributions from the resulting matching scores. These con-tributions guide the selection of relevant re-gions, which are then rearranged into a com-pact visual context for generation. Experi-ments across five benchmarks demonstrate re-trieval and generation gains over correspond-ing baselines, while region-level augmenta-tion substantially reduces visual tokens and computational cost. Our code will be made publicly available at https://github.com/ xandery-geek/MV-VisRAG .

However, most ex-isting RAG frameworks (Ram et al., 2023; Asai et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2025b) are predominantly text-centric (referred to as Text-RAG) and struggle to handle Visually Rich Documents (VRDs) that intertwine text and figures, such as textbooks and manuals, where substantial real-world knowledge resides. As shown in Figure 1(a), for VRD-based data, Text-RAG typically relies on OCR-based pars-ing pipelines to acquire textual content. The pars-ing process can introduce recognition errors and text–layout misalignment, while text-only represen-tations omit visual information conveyed by figures and charts (Guo et al., 2025). To better leverage VRDs, Vision-RAG uses 1arXiv:2611.27542v1 [cs.CV] 20 Nov 2026 Vision-Language Models (VLMs) (Liu et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2025; Bai et al., 2025) to retrieve and answer questions directly from document images, retaining visual and layout information beyond parsed text. Representative methods such as Vis-RAG (Yu et al., 2025) and VDocRAG (Tanaka et al., 2025) use page-level retrieval representations and visual contexts, as illustrated in Figure 1(b). Recent work explores finer-grained processing through patch-level multi-vector retrieval (Faysse et al., 2025) and region-level visual augmenta-tion (Li et al., 2026). However, effectively using fine-grained visual evidence for retrieval and gen-eration still presents two challenges: (1) Compact Fine-grained Retrieval: Encoding an entire page into a single vector can obscure local evidence, while retaining numerous patch vectors increases storage and retrieval costs. An effective represen-tation should preserve fine-grained matching with a compact document index. (2) Coherent Visual Augmentation: Full-page inputs contain substan-tial query-irrelevant content and consume many visual tokens. Selecting smaller regions can re-duce this input, but may separate evidence from the surrounding text and layout needed to interpret it. Effective visual augmentation thus requires se-lecting relevant content while preserving coherent context for answer generation. To address these challenges, we propose MV-VisRAG , a region-level Vision-RAG framework that uses semantically complete regions as shared units for indexing, retrieval, and generation. As illustrated in Figure 1(c), MV-VisRAG first con-structs query-independent regions from textual and visual layout cues through Content-Adaptive Re-gionalization (CAR). To encode these regions for retrieval, we pretrain a VLM-based encoder to re-construct regional text and page-level visual fea-tures, and then fine-tune it for query–document matching. The resulting local region vectors and page-level global vector form a compact document representation for fine-grained late-interaction re-trieval. Since each local vector corresponds to a document region, the matching scores also indi-cate which regions contribute to document rele-vance. These contributions guide the selection of regions from retrieved documents, which are then rearranged into a compact visual context for gener-ation. This process preserves the selected regions’ content and relative spatial order while reducing query-irrelevant input. Experiments across five benchmarks show that MV-VisRAG outperforms single-vector and effi-cient multi-vector retrievers while offering the most compact multi-vector representations and competi-tive online retrieval latency. Our page- and region-level generation variants also outperform their re-spective Vision-RAG baselines. Compared with full-page augmentation, the proposed region-level augmentation reduces visual token usage by 43– 62% and lowers generation latency. Our contributions are summarized as follows: • We present MV-VisRAG, a region-level Vision-RAG framework that unifies indexing, retrieval, and generation around semantically complete regions constructed by a CAR module. • We develop a region-based multi-vector retriever with multimodal reconstruction pretraining and retrieval fine-tuning, and a selective region-augmented generation strategy that uses region contributions to identify critical visual evidence. • Extensive experiments demonstrate the effec-tiveness and efficiency of MV-VisRAG in both retrieval and generation tasks. 

2 Related Work 

2.1 Retrieval-augmented Generation. RAG has expanded from text domain (Fan et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2025) to heterogeneous knowledge sources, including images (Lin et al., 2023; Yuan et al., 2026b,c; Jiang et al., 2026a,b) and videos (Luo et al., 2026; Liu et al., 2025), code (Gao et al., 2024; Dutta et al., 2024), and graphs (Wang et al., 2025a, 2026; Ding et al., 2026). However, RAG for visually rich documents (VRDs), where knowl-edge is conveyed jointly by text, layout, tables, and figures, remains underexplored. A central concern is how to represent documents for retrieval and organize relevant evidence for generation. 

2.2 Visual Document Retrieval. 2.3 Document Visual Question Answering. 

Document VQA answers questions over docu-ments containing textual, tabular, and visual ele-ments (Mathew et al., 2021, 2022; Van Landeghem et al., 2023). While conventional methods assume the target document is given (Ye et al., 2023), Vis-RAG (Yu et al., 2025) and VDocRAG (Tanaka et al., 2025) extend this setting by retrieving rele-vant document images from a large-scale corpus and using them as full-page visual contexts for gen-eration. A recent approach, RegionRAG (Li et al., 2026), uses patch-level embeddings for retrieval and groups salient patches into regions for genera-tion. MV-VisRAG instead encodes each semanti-cally complete region into a single vector, reducing index size while preserving coherent regional con-tent as visual evidence for generation. 

3 Methodology 

This section introduces the task definition and an overview of the proposed MV-VisRAG, followed by a detailed description of each component. 

3.1 Overview Task Formulation. Given a knowledge-intensive question Q and a set of N visually rich documents 

D = {D1, ..., D N }, vision-based RAG (Yu et al., 2025) requires the model to retrieve related doc-uments as external knowledge and then generate responses. In general, the task can be decomposed into two key components: (1) Document Retriever 

R : ( Q, D) → DK , which takes Q and D as in-puts and picks top-K candidate documents DK ⊂

D; (2) Answer Generator G : ( Q, DK ) → A,which utilizes both its inner knowledge and outer context DK to generate answer A for the query Q.

The proposed MV-VisRAG. As shown in Fig-ure 2, MV-VisRAG uses semantically complete regions as shared units for indexing, retrieval, and generation. First, a Content-Adaptive Regional-ization (CAR) module converts the input docu-ment into query-independent regions using tex-tual and visual layout cues. Then, a Region-based Multi-Vector Retriever encodes each document of-fline into region-level local vectors and a page-level global vector, while representing queries as token-level vectors. The retriever ranks documents through late interaction and estimates region contri-butions from the resulting matching scores. These contributions guide the selection of regions from retrieved documents, which are rearranged into a compact visual context for answer generation. 

3.2 Content-Adaptive Regionalization 

To support both fine-grained retrieval and visual augmentation, document regions should preserve coherent, semantically complete content. How-ever, patch-level partitioning (Faysse et al., 2025) and fixed grid-based splitting (Ye et al., 2023; Hu et al., 2025) may fragment semantically related con-tent across multiple crops. We therefore introduce Content-Adaptive Regionalization (CAR), which integrates text-aware Optical Character Recogni-tion (OCR) with vision-based Connected Compo-nent Detection (CCD) to adapt region partitioning to document content, as illustrated in Figure 3. 

Text-aware OCR. Textual layout provides struc-tural cues for grouping document content. Given a document image D ∈ RH×W , we extract part-level OCR bounding boxes B = {B1, . . . , B L}

and iteratively merge spatially close boxes into text regions. Formally, two boxes Bi and Bj are merged when d(Bi, B j ) < τ dis , where d(·) is the 3Patchified Image Visual Connected Components Text Regions Cropped Regions Document Image Semantically Complete Regions

Vision-based CCD. Purely OCR-based grouping may overlook non-textual content such as figures and diagrams. To capture this complementary in-formation, we also recognize visual elements by analyzing pixel-level similarity between neighbor-ing image patches. Specifically, we divide the document image into a uniform patch grid and model it as a 4-connected graph whose nodes rep-resent patches and edges link neighbors. Then, a Union–Find strategy is applied to group adja-cent patches with similar visual characteristics, yielding a set of visual connected components

Finalization with Region Merging. We treat text regions Rtxt as the structural backbone and incor-porate overlapping visual components from Cvis 

to bring related textual and visual content together. Each component Cv is represented by its bound-ing box and merged with the text region having the highest overlap ratio, provided that this ratio ex-ceeds τmerge . To control region granularity, regions taller than τheight are split into horizontal bands, while regions smaller than τarea are discarded. The resulting region set R = {R1, . . . , R K } is then used for region-based document encoding and sub-sequent region-level visual augmentation. 

3.3 Region-based Multi-Vector Retriever 

We design a region-based multi-vector retriever built on a VLM backbone. Although VLMs exhibit strong generative capabilities due to next-token prediction training (Vaswani et al., 2017), they of-ten suffer from limited representation power, lead-ing to suboptimal performance on retrieval bench-marks (Lee et al., 2025). To address this issue, we introduce a two-stage training strategy to enhance the representation capabilities of the retriever. 

Multi-vector Multimodal Reconstruction Pre-training. In the pretraining stage, the retriever learns two complementary representations of doc-ument D: region-level local embeddings that cap-ture fine-grained textual semantics and a page-level global embedding that preserves the holistic visual context of the entire document. We first introduce a text reconstruction task to learn local representations. As shown in Figure 4, we construct an image–text pair (vk, t k) for each region Rk ∈ R, where vk is the region image and 

tk is its OCR-extracted text. We insert an [EOI] 

token between vk and tk and use its final-layer hidden state, the [EOI] embedding, as the local representation. To encourage this embedding to capture textual semantics from vk, we apply a cus-tomized attention mask M that allows text tokens in tk to attend to the corresponding [EOI] token and preceding text tokens, while blocking direct access to image tokens in vk. 4 Experiments 

4.1 Setups Pretraining and Fine-tuning Datasets. Our pro-posed retriever is trained in two stages. In the pretraining stage, we leverage DocStruct4M (Hu et al., 2024), from which we collect over 300k samples and convert each document image into a sequence of sub-images paired with their corre-sponding OCR text. The fine-tuning stage is based on OpenDocVQA (Tanaka et al., 2025), which con-tains approximately 41k query–document pairs. 

Evaluation Datasets. We evaluate Visual Docu-ment Retrieval and Document VQA on representa-tive benchmarks covering diverse document types: 

InfoVQA (Mathew et al., 2022), DUDE (Van Lan-deghem et al., 2023), DocVQA (Mathew et al., 2021), TATDQA (Zhu et al., 2022), and the practi-cal subset of ViDoRe (Faysse et al., 2025), denoted as ViDoRe-P , since its academic tasks largely over-lap with the first four benchmarks. TATDQA and ViDoRe-P are out-of-domain test sets, while the others are used for in-domain evaluation. 

Evaluation Metrics. We adopt nDCG@5 and 

Recall@5 to evaluate document retrieval perfor-mance, following Tanaka et al. (2025). For the Document VQA task, we report two standard met-rics: ANLS (Biten et al., 2019) and F1 score. More details are provided in Appendix B.

5 Conclusion 

In this work, we presented MV-VisRAG, a Vision-RAG framework that decomposes visually rich doc-uments into semantically complete regions for fine-grained retrieval and generation. MV-VisRAG com-bines a region-based multi-vector retriever with se-8lective region-augmented generation, which selects query-relevant regions based on their contributions to late-interaction similarity and rearranges them into a compact visual context. The results high-light the value of semantically complete regions as a common basis for document matching and evi-dence selection, supporting effective retrieval and answer generation with reduced visual input. 

6 Limitations 

Despite the effectiveness of MV-VisRAG, this work has three main limitations that could be addressed in future research. (1) The proposed multi-vector retriever is built on multi-image VLMs, whose ca-pacity remains limited when processing a large number of image regions. Consequently, we im-pose an upper bound of 20 on the number of regions per document. Future work could extend this ca-pacity to better handle documents with more com-plex layouts. (2) MV-VisRAG relies on Content-Adaptive Regionalization (CAR) for document pre-processing, using predefined grouping and merging rules. Region partitioning is therefore not jointly learned with the retriever. A promising direction is to unify region partitioning and encoding within the multi-vector retriever, enabling the learnable identification of semantically complete regions. (3) Region-level augmentation may miss necessary context distributed across regions. Our analysis on DocVQA and DUDE identifies table-related numeric queries as particularly affected: region selection and cropping can omit row or column headers or separate them from the associated val-ues (Appendix D.4). Future work could improve region selection by better modeling cross-region dependencies.

B Experimental Details 

B.1 Baselines 

To ensure a fair comparison with representative single-vector and multi-vector methods, we re-produce VDocRAG (Tanaka et al., 2025) and ColQwen (Faysse et al., 2025) using their official implementations, with the same Qwen2.5-VL (Bai et al., 2025) backbone and OpenDocVQA (Tanaka et al., 2025) fine-tuning data as our method. DocPruner (Yan et al., 2025), Light-ColPali (Ma et al., 2025), and PRUNE-THEN-MERGE (Yan et al., 2026) perform post-hoc pruning or compres-sion on the document embeddings produced by this ColQwen model. Since their code was not publicly available, we implement these methods following the descriptions in their respective papers. For RegionRAG (Li et al., 2026), we use the of-ficial implementation and the released RegionRet checkpoint without retraining. Retrieval is evalu-ated on the same benchmark splits and candidate document pools as the other retrievers. For gen-eration, RegionRAG uses Qwen2.5-VL-3B with the top three retrieved documents and is evaluated using ANLS and F1, whereas the original Region-RAG paper reports relaxed answer accuracy under top-1/top-4 retrieval settings. For the remaining baselines, we use publicly released checkpoints. Table 6 lists the checkpoint URLs and licenses. 

B.2 Benchmark 

Table 7 summarizes the evaluation datasets, includ-ing query counts, document counts, and document types. We remove duplicate queries and documents from the original datasets. The main retrieval ex-periments use a separate candidate document pool for each benchmark. InfoVQA, DUDE, and DocVQA share dataset sources with the OpenDocVQA fine-tuning data and are used for in-domain evaluation. TATDQA and ViDoRe-P are used for out-of-domain eval-uation because their dataset sources are not in-cluded in fine-tuning. E Qualitative Results 

Strength cases. Figures 11 and 12 present suc-cessful examples from InfoVQA and DocVQA. In long infographics and scanned documents, page-level visual augmentation often includes substan-tial irrelevant content, while the answer evidence occupies only a small portion of the page. MV-VisRAG identifies these regions and rearranges them into compact layouts, removing irrelevant content, blank space, and redundant visual areas. In these examples, the input image area is reduced by 60.9%–98.9% while preserving sufficient evi-dence for correct answer generation. These cases show that region-level augmentation is especially effective when the answer can be localized to a few semantically complete regions, consistent with the long-document results in Figure 7. 

Retrieval failure cases. Figure 13 presents two retrieval failure cases. (1) The retriever finds doc-uments related to the Ashes, but not the answer-bearing statistic, leading to the incorrect predic-tion “31” instead of “132”. (2) It fails to retrieve documents about Great Hill Partners and predicts an unrelated person. These examples show that region-level multi-vector retrieval can still confuse topical relevance with answer-bearing relevance, especially when documents share similar entities, layouts, or visual patterns. 

Generation failure cases. Figure 14 shows two generation failure cases. (1) The evidence for the expected growth rate of physical therapist jobs is partially present, but the compact region-level con-text misses surrounding information needed to dis-ambiguate nearby values, resulting in “36%” in-stead of “39%”. (2) The answer region is relevant but blurred, causing the generator to predict “Hol-lywood Reporter” rather than “AJC”. These cases indicate that generation may fail even with relevant regions when the compact layout omits necessary context or the visual evidence is poor. 

Discussion. Overall, MV-VisRAG is most effec-tive when the answer evidence is concentrated in a few semantically complete regions. The fail-ures reflect common challenges in retrieval-based Vision-RAG systems: retrievers may confuse topi-cal relevance with answer-bearing relevance, and generators may fail when visual evidence is in-complete, ambiguous, or lacks necessary context.
