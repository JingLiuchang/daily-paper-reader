# Composing Independently Distilled Memory and Retrieval Policies for Long-Term Agents 

## Grigorii Davydenko, Artem Sakhno, Igor Pavlov, Julia Belikova, Andrey Savchenko, Maksim Makarenko 

Abstract 

Long-lived Web assistants require effective memory and retrieval mechanisms to access information from past user interactions. While these capabilities can be learned through specialized policies, it remains unclear whether specialists trained independently in dif-ferent environments can be effectively composed without joint op-timization. This paper investigates compositional transfer through policy distillation. Memory-writing and sequential retrieval policies are independently distilled from frontier-model action trajectories into 3B models and combined with a fixed answer generator without downstream training. On a LongMemEval-based benchmark, the distilled composition achieves 73.1% accuracy, compared with 36.2% for a composition of existing RL-trained specialists, and also outper-forms the evaluated RL-trained compositions on PersonaMem-v2. Component ablations reveal that improvements in individual poli-cies depend on their composition, highlighting the importance of cross-specialist compatibility. Further task-specific adaptation im-proves accuracy to 75.2% on LongMemEval through RL and 48.9% on PersonaMem-v2 through supervised distillation. These findings demonstrate the effectiveness of independent specialist distilla-tion for modular multi-agent systems. Source code and datasets of teacher-generated memory-editing and retrieval trajectories are released to facilitate reproducibility and further research.

1 Introduction 

Web assistants increasingly operate across multiple sessions, search-ing, browsing, and acting on behalf of users [3, 12]. Providing per-sonalized assistance requires access to information unavailable on the open Web, including users’ preferences, past decisions, and corrections. Long-term conversational memory therefore becomes an essential part of the retrieval infrastructure: agents must decide what information to retain, how to retrieve relevant evidence from accumulated interactions, and when sufficient evidence has been collected to answer a query. Prior work has explored reinforcement learning for memory construction [9], sequential re-trieval [2, 8], and unified memory and answering architectures [6]. In parallel, multi-agent research has investigated specialist train-ing, cooperation, and knowledge distillation [1, 4, 7], including ap-proaches combining distillation and reinforcement learning within individual policies or predefined workflows [5, 10, 11]. However, an important question remains: can specialist policies trained independently in different environments be effectively com-posed without joint training? Independent training enables special-ized capabilities to be developed and reused separately, potentially reducing the need for costly downstream optimization. Yet success-ful composition is not guaranteed. In long-term memory systems, the retriever operates over information selected and structured by the memory writer, making the effectiveness of each specialist dependent on its interaction with other components. To investigate this question, memory-writing and sequential retrieval policies are independently distilled into 3B models using frontier-model action trajectories collected in separate environ-ments. The resulting specialists are composed with a fixed answer generator without downstream training. 2 Method 2.1 Specialist training and composition 

Memory-writing and retrieval policies are distilled independently from frontier-model action trajectories collected in separate envi-ronments (Figure 2, I). 

Memory writing. The teacher processes source chunks chronolog-ically, observing each chunk and the history of previous memory actions before generating memory-edit operations. The original Mem-𝛼 framework [9] is retained, including its core, semantic, and episodic memory types, editing operations, and prompts. 

Sequential retrieval. The retrieval policy follows the Search-R1 interface [2], with the prompt adapted to require reasoning at each search step. The teacher interacts with a Wikipedia search envi-ronment, observing the question, previous queries, and retrieved documents before issuing another search query or terminating retrieval. 

Figure 3: Standalone accuracy of retrieval (left) and memory (right) specialists before composition. 

Teacher trajectories are converted into observation–response pairs, and two separate copies of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct are fine-tuned to imitate the corresponding actions. Both specialists preserve their original action interfaces and are trained independently. 

Composition. The distilled specialists are combined into a dialogue system without downstream training (Figure 2, II). The memory writer processes conversational sessions to construct a persistent memory store indexed by BM25. Given a question, the retriever iteratively searches this store until it decides to stop or exhausts the search budget. A fixed reader then generates an answer conditioned on the question and retrieved records. 

2.2 Task-specific adaptation 

Two adaptation strategies are investigated while keeping the reader fixed (Figure 2, III). 

Supervised distillation. Additional teacher trajectories are col-lected on the downstream dialogue tasks. The memory writer is first adapted through supervised fine-tuning, followed by the retriever using teacher search trajectories over the resulting memory stores. 3 Experiments 3.1 Training and standalone evaluation 

Both specialists are initialized from Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct and dis-tilled from GPT-6.1-sol trajectories with medium reasoning effort. Memory training uses the Mem-𝛼 collection [9] with its original split of 445/150 train/test episodes. Retrieval uses Search-R1 data [2]: 5,700 training questions and 400 held-out questions, balanced across Natural Questions, HotpotQA, 2WikiMultiHopQA, and MuSiQue. We first evaluate the specialists separately, before composition (Figure 3). The distilled retriever achieves 37.75% accuracy, com-pared with 35.75% for Search-R1 and 36.25% for CoRAG (both based on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct). The distilled memory writer achieves 80.58%, compared with 74.79% for the released Mem-𝛼 [9] check-point based on Qwen3-4B. For a controlled comparison, we addition-ally trained Mem-𝛼 using the same Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct backbone as the distilled writer. This configuration achieved 9.51% accuracy on the original Mem-𝛼 test set, compared with 8.34% for the un-trained model. Since this configuration did not yield a competitive baseline, subsequent comparisons use the officially released Mem-𝛼 

checkpoint based on Qwen3-4B. GPT-6-luna with maximum rea-soning effort serves as the judge for both standalone evaluations. 

3.2 Evaluating specialist composition 

We evaluate conversational QA on 147 LongMemEval S questions covering three users and 370 official PersonaMem-v2 multiple-choice questions covering 20 users. Training and test sets have no overlapping sessions, including those repeated across users. Table 1 compares three groups: (1) specialist compositions with-out downstream adaptation: Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct in both roles, the released Mem-𝛼 writer paired with Search-R1 [2] or CoRAG, and our independently distilled pair; (2) full systems: MemoChat, di-rect SFT of Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct without prior distillation, and our distilled pair with retriever-only RL or SFT of both specialists; and (3) frontier pipelines using GLM-5.3-Flash or GPT-6.1-sol for both memory writing and retrieval without task-specific fine-tuning. MemoChat (initialized from Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct) is fine-tuned separately for each benchmark using GPT-6.1-sol examples. All systems share a GPT-6.1-sol reader with medium reasoning effort, supplied with all retrieved records; this replaces MemoChat’s answering stage. Released specialists retain their original action interfaces. Se-quential retrieval allows up to four searches per question, returning up to three records each. We report user-averaged accuracy, search counts, memory size, and runtime. LongMemEval answers are judged by GPT-6-luna with max reasoning effort and PersonaMem-v2 uses its official answer key. Without downstream adaptation, the distilled pair achieves 73.11% accuracy on LongMemEval, compared with 36.23% for Mem-𝛼 

+ Search-R1. On PersonaMem-v2, the corresponding accuracies are 40.31% and 32.79%. The distilled pair also outperforms the MemoChat-based pipeline on LongMemEval. Among non-frontier systems, Distilled & Adapted (RL) achieves the highest LongMemEval accuracy, while Distilled & Adapted (SFT) achieves the highest PersonaMem-v2 accuracy at 48.86%. The distilled specialists there-fore form an effective composition even without downstream adap-tation.

4 Conclusion 

This work demonstrates that independently distilled memory-writing and retrieval policies can be effectively composed into a multi-agent system without joint training. The distilled specialists outperform the evaluated compositions of existing RL-trained policies on Long-MemEval and PersonaMem-v2. Component ablations reveal that downstream performance depends on cross-specialist compatibil-ity, rather than individual policy quality alone. Further adaptation
