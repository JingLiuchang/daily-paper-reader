## Abstract 

Benchmarks judge language-model agents mainly by whether they reach the correct final state, not by how they got there. Endpoint scoring cannot separate an agent that retrieved exactly the evidence a decision required from one that read most of the environment, nor an agent that never located the evidence from one that stopped a single action short. Settling this needs a reference cost, and authored solutions establish only that a task is solvable, not that no shorter route exists. We present DetourBench, which constrains the environment until that minimum is computable. Each task is a finite, partially observable transition system with unit-cost accesses whose resources become globally addressable after discovery. Exhaustive search yields the full-information minimum action count Lo with a replayable witness; from graph-recoverable terminal states it also yields exact residual work RT , which separates an episode that stopped one access short from one that wandered, while an irreversible wrong commitment is recorded as having no finite residual distance rather than assigned one. Success, attainment, resid-ual progress, and normalized detour are reported separately. Across 375 scored episodes — five model endpoints, 25 integrated tasks, three repeats — success ranges from 58.7% to 96.0% while conditional attainment ranges from 0.803 to 0.890 and orders the models differently. Among recoverable failures, residual work separates near-complete trajectories from costly incompletion and from irreversible commitment. We open-source the instances, oracle witnesses, and evaluation harness at https://github.com/DatagridsAI/DetourBench. 

## 1 Introduction 

Interactive-agent benchmarks commonly score whether an agent completed the task [Zhou et al., 2024, Xie et al., 2024, Mialon et al., 2024, Trivedi et al., 2024, OpenAI, 2024]. While necessary, endpoint scoring is silent about process (Figure 1): it conflates direct and exhaustive paths, and gives identical zeros to agents that never located evidence and those stopping a single action short [Ma et al., 2024, Michelakis et al., 2025]. Measuring these differences requires a defensible reference cost. Authored solutions establish solvability but rarely prove minimality, while cost-weighted evaluations require assumptions about the relative prices of tools [Liu et al., 2026]. We restrict each task to a finite transition system in which resources are partially observable but become globally addressable after discovery: once an identifier is revealed, the agent can invoke it directly without navigation or backtracking costs. Exhaustive search over the complete transition graph then computes the minimum number of charged accesses required for success, denoted Lo, and produces a replayable shortest-path witness. The same search supports failure-aware evaluation. For an unfinished episode whose terminal state remains graph-recoverable, it computes the exact residual work RT : the minimum number of further accesses needed for success. This allows success, oracle-relative attainment, normalized detour, and residual-distance progress to be reported separately. An irreversible wrong commitment is represented explicitly rather than assigned a finite residual distance. Lo is an offline optimum over the declared transition system, not the cost of an optimal policy under partial observability; §3 bounds what that leaves out. DetourBench applies this measurement contract to two complementary collections. The Controlled Mechanism Suite holds graph structure, targets, and oracle bounds fixed while varying a local information cue. The Integrated Workflow Suite combines multiple records, constraints, and workflow actions in longer authored tasks. Both use the same transition semantics, solver, evaluator, and artifact-versioning protocol. Mechanical replay, provenance, necessity audits, pair diffs, and hash consistency gate which artifacts and trajectories may be reported. The measurement finds cases endpoint scoring cannot. On eight of twenty-five tasks every model succeeds on every repeat, so success separates nothing at all, and conditional attainment still spreads the models by 0.07 to 0.19 — three to nine times the variation across repeats of the same model. On the failing side, residual work tells a trajectory that stopped one access short from one that exhausted its budget far from the goal and from one that committed irreversibly — three situations a binary score records identically. Our contributions are: 1. Exact oracle-relative trajectory measurement. We compute instance-specific full-information minima and replayable witnesses, together with exact residual work for graph-recoverable failures. 2. A shared benchmark and validation contract. Controlled and integrated tasks use unified execution semantics and fail-closed checks for mechanical validity, provenance, pair integrity, and artifact consistency. 3. A diagnostic multi-model evaluation. Across 375 episodes from five endpoints on 25 tasks, eight tasks are solved by every model on every repeat while cost still orders them, 2and residual work decomposes the failures a binary score records identically. §5 states the selection screen these tasks passed. DetourBench is deliberately formulated as a controlled diagnostic probe with exact graph-theoretic bounds, designed to isolate unnecessary and missing evidence accesses, rather than as a representative sample of natural workflows. 

## 2 Related Work 

Agent and trajectory evaluation. Interactive-agent benchmarks commonly emphasize final task completion, while process-oriented approaches inspect intermediate behavior. AgentBoard [Ma et al., 2024] measures progress through annotated subgoals, and CORE [Michelakis et al., 2025] encodes each task as a deterministic automaton over a manually verified set of acceptable tool-use paths, scoring correctness, ordering, prefix criticality, harmful calls, and efficiency against it. AgentRewardBench [Lù et al., 2025] instead studies the reliability of automatic trajectory evaluators. Both AgentBoard and CORE admit several acceptable routes rather than one canonical ordering, so the difference is not rigidity but provenance: their references are authored and establish that a route is acceptable, while ours is search-derived and establishes that no shorter one exists. 

Interaction and path efficiency. Success weighted by Path Length (SPL) in embodied navigation [Anderson et al., 2018] established the principle our attainment measure inherits: compare an observed successful trajectory with an instance-specific shortest path. Two things differ. SPL scores a failed episode zero, so it cannot distinguish an agent that stopped one action short from one that never located the evidence, and it has no representation for an irreversible wrong commitment; those three cases are what our failure-side measurement separates, using the same search that produces the bound. This is not a corner case on our suite — roughly a third of episodes fail on three of the five models, and the ordering those failures induce differs from the ordering on success. The shortest path is also over a different object: SPL’s optimum is a distance in a metric space, ours is over a combinatorial state of visited resources, acquired credentials, and an outcome flag, in which prerequisites make reachability history-dependent and one transition is absorbing. CORE’s efficiency measure is the closest contemporary comparison. It divides the longest acceptable path not exceeding the agent’s own length by that length, which makes the reference adapt to the trajectory being scored and leaves efficiency undefined for an agent shorter than every acceptable path. Our reference is instead a fixed per-instance minimum that no legal route can beat, so attainment is defined for every success and comparable across episodes of very different length. CostBench [Liu et al., 2026] evaluates planning under heterogeneous tool prices and dynamic failures, while work on efficient tool-integrated reasoning directly optimizes models to reduce unnecessary calls [Wang et al., 2025]. DetourBench instead uses uniform access costs to isolate structural interaction count; it does not measure latency, token consumption, or economic cost. 

Partial observability and benchmark validity. Comparing observed trajectories to full-information optima connects to online search and POMDPs [Papadimitriou and Yannakakis, 1991, Kaelbling et al., 1998]. Without current-location costs, search depends on proximal cues or infor-mation scent [Pirolli and Card, 1999], motivating our controlled cue intervention. Audits show underspecified tasks and scoring shortcuts threaten benchmark validity [Zhu et al., 2025, Shao et al., 2026, Tian et al., 2026, OpenAI, 2024]; DetourBench addresses these via replayable witnesses, provenance, pair diffs, and cryptographic manifests.

What an omniscient reference does and does not concede. Lo is computed by a searcher that knows the whole graph, so it is fair to ask whether NDR charges an agent for discovery no policy could avoid. The construction rule in §4 bounds how much of it can be: evidence a decision requires is encoded as a prerequisite, so the solver reaches the target only by traversing it. We verify this rather than assert it: every annotated evidence node in the evaluated suite lies on the shortest successful route, with no exceptions. What the oracle keeps is not the ability to skip evidence but the ability to pick the right lookalike candidate first. We do not net that advantage out, so NDR is excess relative to hindsight-optimal selection rather than a claim that any particular access was unreasonable; separating the two needs an informed reference policy the released suite does not contain. 3.5 Access budgets 

The exact oracle is a reference cost rather than an episode limit. Each instance also declares an information-complete witness of length Li, which exposes all resources marked necessary for an informed terminal decision. Its frozen access budget is 

B = ⌈m max( Lo, L i)⌉ , (10) where the multiplier m is fixed before the primary evaluation; we use m = 1 .5 throughout, so an instance with Lo = 12 is run under a budget of 18. On every instance in the released suite Li = Lo,because necessity is enforced through prerequisites that the shortest route must already satisfy; the maximum is therefore inactive here and B = ⌈mL o⌉ in practice. We keep the general form because an instance whose informed witness exceeds its oracle route would otherwise receive a budget that forbids an informed decision. Neither Li nor B is used as an efficiency numerator. The release reports 

Lo, Li, m, B, and B/L o for every evaluated instance; no budget is increased during an episode. Of the 99 failures in the integrated suite, 95 exhaust this budget. They end a median of three accesses from the goal (mean residual 3.02, mean progress 0.766), and 47 of them end within two accesses. Duplicate and invalid accesses make up 7.6% of their charged steps, so the budget is spent mostly on valid, non-repeated accesses. This is consistent with resolving uncertainty among lookalike candidates, but we do not separate reasonable uncertainty resolution from avoidable exploration, and we do not vary m; budget sensitivity is left to future work. 

## 4 Benchmark Construction and Validation 

4.1 Collections 

A task specifies a construction procedure, an instance is one frozen executable realization, and an 

episode is one model run on an instance. DetourBench contains two collections. The Controlled Mechanism Suite builds paired instances across six mechanisms; only the 40 Currency pairs survived the equivalence audit in a form usable for a controlled comparison (§B), and they are what §5 reports. The Integrated Workflow Suite contains longer authored tasks grounded in software, clinical, financial, banking, logistics, regulatory, legal, corporate-registry, transit, customs, and security records. These tasks combine multiple sources, scenario constraints, credentials, and workflow actions. Each integrated task contributes one frozen instance, and the twenty-five evaluated here are listed in Appendix G. 

4.2 Construction and controlled pairing 

Construction begins with source records rather than a prompt alone. A builder emits the resource and action graph, prerequisites, terminal conditions, and authored scenario facts. Source-derived content is linked to supporting spans, while authored overlays are labeled separately. A removal audit deletes each node on the oracle route and re-runs the exact search; for tasks 21–24 we also execute content counterfactuals that change one fact and check that the decision flips. Elsewhere semantic roles are documented, not executed (Appendix C). The solver computes Lo, generates a shortest-path witness, and replays it under the model-facing evaluator. Each released task ships its graph, initial state, task specification, source corpus, oracle, replayable witness, budget, verifier report, and freeze manifest, and every trajectory records the graph and oracle hashes it ran against. 4.3 Validation and freeze 

Every reported artifact passes four gates: mechanical validity requires schema checks and witness replay; construct validity requires source provenance, labeled overlays, and a structural removal audit, with documented semantic roles for most tasks and executed content counterfactuals for four (Appendix C); generation validity checks uniqueness, answer degeneracy, and controlled-pair integrity; and artifact validity freezes the graph, oracle, prompt, metric, evaluator, and budget, with the graph and oracle carrying cryptographic hashes that every trajectory records and every analysis re-checks before reporting. Development pilots identify malformed graphs, shortcut answers, ambiguous prompts, and evaluator defects. A repair creates a new artifact version and invalidates earlier trajectories. Because model behavior informed some revisions, freezing guarantees artifact consistency but does not make the benchmark an untouched test set. Experimental selection based on model behavior is disclosed separately in Section 5. Analysis fails closed on a version or hash mismatch, a runtime budget change, or an incomplete model–task–repeat cell. 

## 5 Experiments and Results 

We evaluate whether outcome-only scoring masks differences in execution efficiency and partial progress. Two experiments do this from opposite directions: a controlled intervention on the mechanism suite, where one sentence is added or withheld and everything else is held fixed, and an observational run over the integrated suite. They use different instances and are reported separately, never pooled; qwen3.6-plus and glm-5.2 appear in both.

5.2 Experimental setup 

The Integrated Workflow Suite contains 25 versioned tasks: 20 frozen before review (tasks 1–20), four authored afterwards (tasks 21–24), and one run alongside tasks 1–20 but left out of their manifest (task 25). We evaluate five model endpoints ( claude-opus-5 , deepseek-v4-pro ,

qwen3.6-plus , glm-5.2 , kimi-k3 ) attempting every task three times through a shared scaffold (25 × 5 × 3 = 375 episodes). Fixed budgets are enforced without runtime unlocking. Infrastructure aborts were rerun; all valid, invalid, and duplicate accesses are charged. In this suite, all completions occurred through charged terminal accesses. All 375 runs carry cryptographic graph and oracle hashes; six tasks (17, 20, and 21–24) ran under a later prompt revision (Appendix G). The task is the unit of comparison: repeats characterize within-task variability, not 75 independent samples.

5.4 Matched success with different costs 

Eight tasks—2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 14, 19, and 25—are solved in all three repeats by all five models. Their between-model attainment spreads range from 0.074 to 0.190. Because every episode succeeds, these differences cannot be attributed to unequal success counts. The widest is task 3 ( Lo = 15 , spread 0.190), where mean accesses run from 17.0 to 21.7 across models. The extra cost is not repetition: the best and worst models both record RSR = 0 .000 (zero repeats or invalid calls) yet differ in attainment by 0.189. RSR detects overt repeats; attainment also catches valid but unnecessary exploration (Appendix D). These tasks establish cost variation exists, not prevalence, as separation was a screening criterion. 

5.5 Failure-aware analysis 

Of the 375 scored episodes, 99 fail: 95 exhaust the access budget, two stop in a recoverable state, and two enter an absorbing wrong state. Table 5 (Appendix E) reports progress and NDR over the 97 failures that remain active and recoverable. Wrong-terminal episodes are excluded because they have no finite residual distance. These columns are not a model comparison: the five models fail on largely disjoint task sets (Claude on 1 instance, Kimi on 4, DeepSeek on 13, GLM on 14, Qwen on 16). Across all 97 recoverable failures, progress ranges from well short of the goal to one access away, and the two absorbing commitments have no residual distance at all. On task 18 ( Lo = 20 ), six failures collapse to one binary score; our decomposition separates two that stopped one access from success, three that exhausted budget at 0.85–0.95 progress, and one absorbing commitment with no residual distance (Appendix F). 

5.6 Selection boundary 

The 20-task manifest was frozen on August 28, 2026; model behavior informed authoring for eight tasks and replacement of two uninformative instances. Tasks 21–24 were authored after review; two of them (21 and 22) were revised after earlier model runs, and only the final revision, on which all five models were run, is reported. Task 25 was run with tasks 1–20 under the original prompt and passes the same screen, but was left out of the frozen manifest; we add it here. The 8/17 cost/outcome split reflects selection choices rather than prevalence. Retention rules, excluded candidates, and hashes are in Appendix B. 

## 6 Conclusion and Limitations 

DetourBench evaluates interactive agents through trajectory-level transition systems with provable minimum-action bounds Lo and exact residual work RT , preventing efficient success, exploratory success, recoverable near-misses, and irreversible error from collapsing into one score. Experiments confirm this distinction is practical: one withheld sentence moves the same model on the same task, eight tasks separate models purely by cost under 100% success, and binary failure scores decompose into near-complete, costly-incomplete, and irreversible cases. Four limitations bound this: model-informed screening reflects released suite properties rather than population prevalence; three repeats limit stochastic analysis; the omniscient oracle does not net out lookalike selection advantage; and exactness holds under authored semantics, whose semantic necessity is executed for only four tasks. Future work should evaluate pre-registered held-out suites, widen repeat and model coverage, add human baselines, and develop informed reference policies separating discovery from planning error. 9References

## C Necessity audits: what is checked 

Structural removal. For every task we delete each node on the oracle route (all edges into it and out of it) and re-run the exact search of Appendix I. Of the 314 such nodes across the 25 tasks, removing 309 leaves the target unreachable. The other five have substitutes: one section in task 1 (the bound rises from 9 to 10), three records in task 9 (bounds 13, 11, and 11), and one search page in task 19 (bound unchanged at 16). This check shows that the route needs these nodes; it does not show that their content matters to the decision, because necessity is enforced through prerequisites. 

Executed content counterfactuals. Tasks 21–24 each ship a frozen necessity_audit.json 

in which a domain decision function is re-run on edited inputs. Each task runs four edits: change one key fact, change a relation or scope, remove a required piece of evidence, and, as a negative control, replace the distractor records. In all four tasks every edit gives the expected result, and an independent reference implementation agrees on the baseline and all four edits. In task 23 (customs ruling revocation), for example, the baseline determination is reliance protection at a 5.3% duty rate. Moving the entry date from 1993-04-15 to 1993-06-01, or setting good-faith reliance to false, changes it to the revocation tariff at 20.0%. Removing required evidence leaves no determination. Replacing the twelve distractor records leaves the baseline unchanged. These counterfactuals are authored edits to the inputs, not revised observations, and they cover a finite set of edits rather than every node. 

Documented roles. For tasks 8–20 the release records an authored semantic role for each evidence node or edge, without an executed content counterfactual; tasks 1–7 and 25 carry the structural check only. For these 21 tasks semantic necessity rests on the authored scenario, which is part of the limitation stated in Section 6.

## F Task 18 failure decomposition 

Task 18 makes the distinction concrete. Its exact oracle requires Lo = 20 accesses, with a budget of 30. Claude and Kimi succeed in all three repeats, using 21.7 and 23.7 accesses on average. DeepSeek, Qwen, and GLM each succeed once, but their failed trajectories terminate in different states. DeepSeek and GLM each produce one recoverable gave_up trajectory after reaching 

committed_a , only one required access from success. Both have progress 0.95, with NDR values of 0.30 and 0.35, respectively. Their budget-exhausted runs are less efficient: DeepSeek ends with progress 0.85 and NDR 0.65, while GLM reaches progress 0.95 but incurs NDR 0.55 through additional exploration. Qwen likewise has one budget-exhausted run with progress 0.85 and NDR 0.65, but its other failure commits to rejected_b , an absorbing wrong state for which residual metrics are undefined. Binary scoring assigns all six trajectories the same failure value. The graph-based decomposition instead separates a one-step-away recoverable execution, inefficient budget exhaustion, and an irreversible incorrect commitment.
