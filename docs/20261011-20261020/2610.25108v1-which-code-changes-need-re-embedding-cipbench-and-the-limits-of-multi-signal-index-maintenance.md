## 摘要
仓库级检索增强生成系统必须使向量索引与持续演化的代码保持同步，然而，对于哪些发生变更的代码单元实际上需要新的嵌入，目前尚无原则性的判断依据。我们研究代码变更的检索影响能否在计算该嵌入之前被预判。在 Python、Go 和 JavaScript 的 10 个开源仓库中，我们刻画了变更如何从句法实质性经由嵌入位移传播到检索影响。这些联系具有信息量但并非确定性的：句法类别能清晰区分嵌入位移，但影响既取决于单元表示的移动幅度，也取决于该单元的检索暴露度，而且反复出现的反例限制了任何单一信号所能决定的范围。我们将该决策形式化为 CIPBench，一个在严格的嵌入前信息边界下评估维护策略的基准。预测是可行的，而一个信号就已将其用尽。仅使用单元变更历史的排序器，相对于随机调度和基于新近度的调度，能显著降低检索损害。将其与另外三个信号——各自具有区分度且彼此基本不相关——结合后，在我们评估的每个预算下，决策在统计上都没有变化；我们将原因追溯到评分结构而非其调参。真正有效的是过滤而非排序：文本相对于索引状态未发生变化的单元可以无风险地跳过，并且在回退最频繁的项目中，可节省近一半的嵌入成本。因此，信号层面的信息量并不能证明决策层面的价值；要区分二者，需要评估信号所产生的决策，而不是信号与结果之间的关联。

## Abstract
Repository-level retrieval-augmented generation systems must keep vector indexes synchronized with continuously evolving code, yet there is no principled basis for deciding which changed code units actually need new embeddings. We ask whether the retrieval impact of a code change can be anticipated before that embedding is computed.   Across 10 open-source repositories in Python, Go, and JavaScript, we characterize how a change propagates from syntactic substantiality through embedding displacement to retrieval impact. The links are informative but not deterministic: syntactic class separates displacement sharply, yet impact depends on a unit's retrieval exposure as much as on how far its representation moves, and recurring counterexamples bound what any single signal can decide. We formalize the decision as CIPBench, a benchmark that scores maintenance policies under a strict pre-embedding information boundary.   Prediction works, and one signal exhausts it. A ranker using only a unit's change history substantially reduces retrieval harm relative to random and recency-based scheduling. Combining it with three further signals--individually discriminative and largely unassociated with one another--leaves the decision statistically unchanged across every budget we evaluate, for reasons we trace to the structure of the score rather than to its tuning. What pays off is not ranking but filtering: units whose text is unchanged from the indexed state can be skipped with no risk and, in the most reversion-heavy project, at nearly half the embedding cost. Signal-level informativeness is therefore not evidence of decision-level value, and separating the two requires evaluating the decision a signal produces rather than its association with the outcome.
