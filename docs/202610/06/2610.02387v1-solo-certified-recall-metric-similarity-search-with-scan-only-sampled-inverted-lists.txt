SOLO: Certified-Recall Metric Similarity Search
with Scan-Only Sampled Inverted Lists
Edgar Ch´avez
Computer Science Department, CICESE
elchavez@cicese.edu.mx
Draft of September 25, 2026
Abstract
We present SOLO, an index for approximate nearest-neighbor search in general metric spaces whose
serving path contains no ranking heuristic of any kind: a query is routed to the ks nearest points of
a random sample of the database, and every object in the touched posting lists is evaluated with the
true distance. Because nothing must outrank anything, recall equals a coverage probability computable
from the stored index: one ground-truth pass over a query sample certifies every operating point at once,
without serving any of them — a recall certificate, and for a navigable graph no analogous object exists
at any price. The whole index is one recursive rule — sample the collection, post each object to its b
nearest sample points, split any list that outgrows a bound, always scan the leaves — and its operating
surface obeys an equal-work law, recall ≈f(b · ks), whose level is a one-scalar signature of the dataset.
The same scan-only structure gives a serving floor no graph architecture reaches once the router is itself
indexed by the same rule: Deep-100M served at recall 0.9977 from 1 GB of resident memory (enforced
cap, 10.7 bytes per object) and at 0.9964 from 256 MB, Deep-1B at recall 0.9925 from 512 MB (and from
96 MB at depth 3), inserts that are one search, and deletes that are exact. Throughput is competitive
where the hardware allows it — up to 1.8× a tuned HNSW at 108 on the machine of §5, with operating
points to the right of where that graph saturates — and the tables report it against HNSW, DiskANN,
GRAFT, NAPP, misi, and SPANN’s assignment rule on the same hardware and ground truth.

Introduction
At 108 objects, the practical frictions of nearest-neighbor serving are no longer about raw speed. First,
recall dials saturate: an exhaustively tuned HNSW on Deep-100M stops improving at recall@10 = 0.9989
(ef=4000); beyond that point there is no operating point, at any cost.
Second, recall is unpredictable:
a graph’s recall is an emergent property of its construction, measurable only against ground truth after
the fact.
Third, serving floors are heavy: disk-resident graph systems pin gigabytes of navigation state
(DiskANN needs 12 GB for Deep-100M in our measurements and fails below 9.6 GB). Fourth, dynamism is
hard: deleting from a navigable graph is famously delicate, and update layers are systems in their own right
[15, 20].
SOLO (Scan-Only Lists, One rule)1 is built from the opposite end: make the serving path so simple that
its recall is a theorem about the index rather than an outcome of search heuristics. The index is an inverted
file over a vocabulary that is a uniform random sample S of the database itself, |S| = αn. Each object is
posted to its b nearest sample points; a query is routed to its ks nearest sample points; the touched lists are
scanned exhaustively with the true distance. There is no vote, no scoreboard, no beam, no pruning bound:
the only approximation in the entire path is which lists are touched. Consequently (§2.2) recall equals a
coverage probability computable from the stored signatures by integer set intersection: exact neighbors for
one sample of queries — paid once, on the operator’s own workload — certify the recall of every (b, ks)
configuration simultaneously, with no query served and no index rebuilt.

One rule completes the design. Random sampling does not balance list sizes, and a list too large to
scan would break the cost model — so a list that outgrows a bound is treated as what it is, a database:
sampled, split by the same rank-b assignment, routed the same way. Leaves are always scanned. On the
most hub-skewed dataset we have (GloVe), coverage inside the oversized lists is, if anything, easier than
globally (up to 0.9994 at depth 2), and the recursive index matches its own composed prediction to 2 × 10−4
end to end.
Scanning more candidates than a graph probes is the deliberate trade: the scan is a streaming, prefetch-
able, SIMD-friendly computation, and replication forgives local error exponentially (a covered neighbor is
re-found in a median of ∼13 touched lists). Both properties are cashed in twice. In RAM, a scalar-quantized
screen with exact re-ranking of R survivors is lossless at every operating point we measure: 8-bit codes
lift throughput up to 2.8×, and a list-major stream of 4-bit codes, scanned in the order objects rank their
sample points, serves SIFT-1M at 0.9992 at 8,836 queries/s on a laptop — 2.1× a saturated HNSW. On
disk, the same codes duplicated into posting-ordered leaf blocks turn a query into ∼ks sequential reads plus
∼R row fetches — serving Deep-100M under an enforced 1 GB memory cap at full recall — and, with the
router indexed by the same rule, under 256 MB.
Contributions.
1. A scan-only serving model for sample-vocabulary inverted files with a recall certificate: recall = cov-
erage, measured to hold within 5 × 10−4 at 106–108 on three datasets (§2.2), and carrying a per-query
provision for queries drawn from outside the certified distribution (§4).
2. The equal-work law: coverage is a function of the touched posting mass b·ks, nearly independent of its
factorization; its level is a one-scalar dataset signature, and the distance evaluations needed at fixed
recall grow only as n0.4–0.6 over four measured decades (§3).
3. The one-rule recursion that restores bounded lists without clustering, validated end to end against an
ablation that attaches a graph index to each oversized list: the recursion wins on recall and throughput
simultaneously, with a build cheaper by 25× (§2.3, §5.2).
Two things carry these claims into practice rather than standing beside them as separate contributions.
The serving stack — rank-truncated serve-time replication, bitmap-deduplicated fused scan kernels, lossless
SQ8/SQ4 screen-and-refine, a list-major code stream with rank-ordered postings as a query-time dial, an
mmap-able router, posting-ordered leaf blocks — is what makes the scan-only model of (1) fast enough to be
interesting and small enough to serve 108 vectors from 1 GB resident (§2.5, §5.3) and, with the rule pointed
upward once, 109 from 512 MB at recall 0.9925 (§5.8). And every number in this paper is measured on the
same machines, queries, and ground truth as HNSW, DiskANN, GRAFT, NAPP, vote-based serving of the
identical postings (misi), and SPANN’s closure assignment rule (§5.6); that comparison is the evidence for
the three claims, not a fourth one.
What this paper assumes.
Nothing outside itself. Section 2 defines the index completely — vocabulary,
assignment, serving, recursion, kernels — over a black-box distance [1] and one interchangeable subroutine:
an approximate nearest-neighbor index over the αn sample points, used only to route. That subroutine is
a constant factor in the design, not a dependency of it, and the paper measures the difference: replacing
it with exhaustive search over the sample moves no recall reported here by more than 10−4 (§2.1). The
representation SOLO inherits — a vocabulary drawn from the database itself at a fixed fraction of it, and
each object’s ranked signature over it — was introduced by misi [6], which served it by voting; SOLO keeps
that representation, discards that serving theory, and everything an implementer needs is in §2.

Two properties are visible in the pseudocode and are the source of everything that follows.
Build
contains no loop that revisits a decision: no clustering iteration, no graph refinement, no insertion order —
the objects’ signatures are independent, so construction is embarrassingly parallel and bitwise reproducible.
Serve contains no comparison between candidates other than the true distance: no vote, no scoreboard, no
beam, no bound. The second property is what makes recall a theorem (§2.2); the first is what makes the
index cheap to build and exact to update (§2.7).
2.1
Vocabulary and assignment
Given X with |X| = n in a metric space with black-box distance d, draw a uniform sample S ⊆X, |S| = αn
(all experiments: α = 2%). Build any high-recall ANN index over S (the router). For each x ∈X, one router
search yields its ranked signature: the kb nearest sample points in order (we build with kb=64). Assignment
is truncation: posting x to its b nearest sample points, b ≤kb, is a serve-time choice — the inverted file for
any b is a prefix-restriction of the stored ranked signatures, so one build serves every b. Mean list length
is b/α. Construction is n independent searches: embarrassingly parallel, deterministic for fixed seed, with
3


no iterative refinement of any kind. Sample points are indexed like any other object; each carries itself at
rank 1 of its own signature, so a query whose true neighbor is a vocabulary point retrieves it from that
point’s own list (measured: probability 1.0) — the double role costs 0.3% of scanned work in re-evaluated
routing distances and one of b replication slots for the α fraction of objects that are sampled.
Remark 1 (Router independence). The router is the only approximate subroutine in SOLO, and the index
does not inherit its error. On Deep-100M we replaced query routing with exact top-ks over the two-million-
point sample (chunked exhaustive search) and re-served every frontier configuration: the served recalls are
identical to four decimals at b ∈{16, 32} and ks ∈{256, 384, 512} (the routed term sets themselves overlap
at 0.9992–0.9999). Whatever recall SOLO attains is therefore a property of the assignment geometry, not of
the router — which we report with GRAFT [7], and which any high-recall index over αn points may supply.
The same holds on the build side by the insurance argument of §3.3: a signature that misses one sample
point forfeits one of b replicas, and coverage depends on the union of b.
2.2
Scan-only serving and the recall identity

exhaustively with the true distance (duplicates removed), and the exact top-k of the scanned set is returned.

The proof is short because the design is: a scan-only path has exactly one failure mode, and it is a
membership question about stored data rather than a question about search dynamics. Every quantity a
graph must be trusted on — entry points, beam width, pruning, the order in which candidates are met —
is absent, so there is nothing left for recall to depend on.
Empirically the identity holds to four decimals on GloVe and Deep; on SIFT the residual (≤9 × 10−4)
is fully attributed to ties (integer coordinates; zero membership misses; 28/2000 queries have tied 10-th
distances within the exact ground truth itself). The sharpest test is the hardest corpus at the largest scale:
on MS MARCO Web Search at 108 — 768-dimensional text embeddings under inner product, with queries
drawn from a different distribution than the passages — the certificate computed from the stored signatures
alone matches the served recall to 0.0001 at all 6 operating points, including those where the served recall is
poor (0.8987 at best, §5.7). An index that predicts its own recall before serving predicts it when the answer
is unwelcome too, which is the property that makes it worth deploying: routing exactly rather than with the
router costs a further 0.0194 of coverage, so the two sources of loss are separately visible in the certificate.
The certificate, and what it costs.
We call a recall figure certified when it is computed from the
stored index rather than observed by running the system, and Proposition 1 is what makes such a figure
available: it turns an empirical question into an arithmetic one. It is worth being precise about the exchange
rate. Evaluating cov requires knowing the true neighbors y, so it needs exact neighbors for a sample of
representative queries — one brute-force pass, paid once. What it does not require is serving: given that
one pass and the stored signatures, coverage at every (b, ks) is a set intersection over integers, so a single
ground-truth computation certifies the entire operating surface, including configurations never deployed and
4


b values never materialized (assignment is a prefix, §2.1). Two consequences distinguish this from ordinary
offline tuning. First, the certified quantity is the served recall rather than a proxy for it — Proposition 1
is an identity, not a bound or a correlation. Second, the certificate is transferable: an operator computes
it on their own query distribution, which is the distribution their recall will actually be drawn from, and
re-certifies after a workload shift without touching the index. For a navigable graph no analogous object
exists at any price: recall is a property of the traversal, so the only way to learn it is to run it.
Out of sample.
The certificate is an expectation over the query distribution it was computed on. A query
drawn from outside that distribution needs a provision, and the index carries one — the ks routed distances
are order statistics of a uniform sample of the query’s own distance distribution, so routing has already paid
for a probe of the query’s neighborhood. Section 4 develops that provision and measures it across seven
corpora and thirteen query sets, including a cross-modal shift where it fails.

local sample points, and route queries that enter the list to k′

2.4
Quantized leaves
The leaf scan is a streaming pass over posting-ordered candidates — the computation SIMD integer arith-
metic is best at. SOLO uses the simplest quantizer that could work: a global affine 8-bit grid (two constants,
one pass over the data, no training), or — halving the bytes streamed — a per-dimension affine 4-bit grid
(2d constants), packed two codes per byte. Serving screens with integer squared distances, keeps the R best
distinct candidates, and re-ranks them at full precision; the returned ranking is exact over the screened set.
At every operating point measured — three datasets, 106 to 108 — 8-bit codes with R=40 already reproduce
the full-precision results exactly, and we serve with R=100; the coarser 4-bit screen needs R=200 to match
on GloVe (SIFT is unchanged at R=100), and all SQ4 rows in this paper use R=200. For non-vector metric
spaces the same architecture runs with plain distances; quantization is an accelerator of the verifier, never
part of the core.
How far down does the stream go?
The screen is a sequential pass, so its cost is bytes, and the question
is how coarse a code still recovers the pool’s own ceiling after exact refine. We measured a third rung below
SQ4 — a 2-bit ternary code (keep the d/2 largest |ui| as ±1, zero the rest), scored against a float query,
which leaves the op count of an SQ8 dot product and divides its bytes by four [24]. Inside a routed pool at
ks=32, the pass set needed to recover 99.9% of the ceiling is R=20 for SQ8 but 200 at d=1024 and 2,000
at d=100: two bits genuinely rank worse, by one to two orders of magnitude in R. It is still the cheaper
design, because the pass set is two orders of magnitude smaller than the pool — counting bytes moved per
query, a 2-bit screen feeding an SQ8 re-rank feeding the exact refine moves ∼3.6× less than an SQ8 screen
at d=1024 and ∼2.9× less at d=100 — projected from the measured pool and pass-set sizes with the gather
penalty taken from this paper’s own SQ8-gather-versus-SQ4-stream measurement, not measured end to end.
The middle rung is what makes it work: it turns a wide exact gather into a wide SQ8 gather plus a narrow
exact one. Two practical corollaries: quantizing the query as well (a fully bitwise product) is never worth
it — the query is one vector and keeping it float buys one to seven points of coverage — and the object id
should leave the code stream for a parallel array, since the screen never reads an id and only the survivors
do, which is the difference between 146 and 170 records per 4 KB page at d=96, against 40 for SQ8. These
are 106-scale laptop measurements (§8); the serving stack in this paper is SQ8 and SQ4 throughout.
The screen/scorer distinction is load-bearing, and we measured its other side: SPANN built and served
natively in Int8 on the identical 8-bit grid — quantized distances as the final scorer — saturates at 0.9561
5


on Deep-100M against float ground truth (58.2 h build, same machine), four points below its own float build,
while the same grid used as a screen with exact refine is lossless. Quantization must screen, never score.
2.5
Serving kernels and the memory model
Serving state: router, postings, codes, vectors. Three facts matter at n ≥108.
Visited state must be bits. The scan deduplicates across ks lists. An epoch-stamped 32-bit visited array
costs 4 B/object/thread — 25.6 GB at 108 with 64 threads: invisible in RAM benchmarks, fatal under a
memory cap. A per-thread bitmap with touched-clearing costs 12.5 MB and is faster (cache-resident; +15%
throughput). Deduplication must occur before the screen’s R-heap: the most duplicated candidates are
exactly the nearest ones, and an undeduplicated heap fills with copies (we measured the failure: recall
0.9982 →0.8394, worsening with ks).
The scan must stream, not gather. With codes stored per object, scanning a list gathers one cache line
per posting from a random address; with the codes duplicated in posting order (list-major: the record of
list ℓ’s i-th member sits at position i of ℓ’s code stream) each of the ks lists is one sequential segment, and
the same layout is what the disk mode reads (below). At 106 on one laptop core the stream costs ∼1.7 ns
per record at either 8 or 4 bits (instruction-bound: the 4-bit form halves bytes, not instructions) plus 0.5–
1.5 µs per segment of prefetcher ramp-up, against 9–25 ns per gathered record; at all ten cores the stream is
bandwidth-bound and the 4-bit codes buy a further 1.4×. The layout is written once, at b=kb, with each list
ordered by the rank its members assign to it — the objects for which ℓis the nearest sample point first, then
those at rank 2, and so on. Two dials then cost nothing at build time: the served b is a prefix of every list,
and the prefix may taper with routing position, b(t) = ⌈b0(1 −t/ks)p⌉for the t-th routed list, p=0 being the
rectangle of Table 3. A tapered frontier reads fewer records than any rectangle of equal recall (on SIFT-1M,
0.9991 from 54% of the records of the (16, 128) rectangle) — the rank-t lists far from the query contribute
mostly rank-1 members, which the frontier keeps. The router is served at ef=ks: raising it to 4ks moves no
recall by more than 10−3 and costs four times the routing time.
Load must not rebuild. The router serializes to a flat, position-independent, mmap-able image; loading is
43–90 µs at every scale measured, versus seconds-to-minutes of reconstruction, with bitwise-identical search.
Under a memory cap the mapped router is file-backed and reclaimable; a rebuilt router is anonymous memory
and is not.
Disk wants layout, not paging. Serving the same index by letting vector reads fault through mmap reads
6 TB in 80 minutes under a 4 GB cap (a 4 KB page per 384 B row, evicted before reuse): an ∼85× amplifica-
tion. SOLO’s disk mode instead duplicates codes into the lists (SPANN’s trade — disk is the cheap resource):
leaf blocks of [id | code] records in posting order, read sequentially into bounded per-thread buffers, screened,
then refined by ∼R row reads from the single full-precision file. At (b, ks) = (16, 256) this is 37 MB sequential
+ 0.4 MB random per query, all explicit.
2.6
The codes as a proximity sketch
The ranked signatures serve a second role beyond routing: for objects already indexed, company overlap
is a calibrated proximity estimator computed by integer set intersection — no vector access. Measured on
SIFT-1M (800 anchors), mean overlap at b=64 falls smoothly from 38.8 shared terms at neighbor rank 1
through 23.4 at rank 100 to 16.3 at rank 1000, against a random-pair floor of 0.35 — a dynamic range of
∼110:1, monotone across three decades of rank; at b=16 a ≥1-shared-term test captures 95% of rank-100
pairs at a 1.8% false-pair rate. Three uses follow. (i) Result-set operations in the disk regime: re-ranking,
diversification, or clustering of a returned candidate set by code overlap costs zero reads where vector fetches
are the expensive resource (§5.3). (ii) Similarity self-joins: shared-term thresholds are a data-defined banding
scheme over the inverted file the index already maintains. (iii) Standing queries: a subscription encoded once
matches arriving objects’ codes — which their own inserts compute anyway — by intersection. A boundary
honestly drawn: for a fresh query, encoding is a search, so code comparison cannot accelerate what produces
it — data-adapted resolution costs a search to encode, the opposite end of the trade LSH [8] occupies; indeed
misi’s vote was exactly code-overlap used as a ranking, and §5.6 shows scanning dominates it.

2.7
Updates and determinism
Insert: one router search, b list appends. Delete: removal from b lists — exact, no tombstones, no graph
repair, no rebalancing protocol; no other object’s representation changes. Every build and every query is
bitwise deterministic for a fixed seed at any thread count; recall is machine-independent (our d0 and laptop
runs agree bit for bit, as they must).
3
Measured laws
3.1
The equal-work law and the regime spectrum
Coverage collapses onto contours of the touched posting mass: cov(b, ks) ≈fX(b·ks) (Figure 2, Table 1). On
GloVe at ∼140k scanned candidates, four different factorizations (b, ks) ∈{(4, 256), (8, 128), (16, 64), (64, 16)}
cover 0.9126–0.9332; the law reappears at Deep-100M ((16, 256) ≈(32, 128) at 0.998, equal throughput).
Inside a contour the split is free, so one chooses small b (storage, build) and buys recall with ks (routing over
αn points is cheap). The level of fX is a one-scalar regime signature — b · ks for coverage 0.99: SIFT ∼512,
Deep-10M ∼1–2k, GloVe ≳8k — the concentrated/spread dichotomy of metric spaces, measurable from the
index itself.
3.2

100M). The drift is geometry, not router error (Remark 1): finer cells at |S| = 2M dilute the true neighbor’s
membership. The remedy is the dial, and the dial is cheap because evaluations at fixed recall grow sublinearly.

3.3
Replication is insurance
Among covered GloVe true neighbors at ks=64, the count j of touched lists containing the neighbor has
median ∼13; only 2.5% have j=1. A neighbor is lost only if all j appearances are missed: a local miss
rate ε costs ∼P
j pjεj globally. This is why the lossless behavior of the SQ8 screen and the near-ceiling
composition of the recursion are not luck; the same j-structure recurses (j median 3–8 inside decomposed
lists).
3.4
Choosing the assignment rule
Multi-assignment can also be done by distance ratio (“closure”, SPANN’s rule: assign to cells within (1 + ϵ)
of the nearest). At equal mean replication, rank-b dominates closure on both datasets: SIFT, closure ϵ=0.1
(¯b=7.3) covers 0.9960 at ks=128 vs. 0.9996 for rank-8; GloVe, closure ϵ=0.1 (¯b=42.6) covers 0.9788 vs. 0.9902
for rank-32. The rank order of the signature is the better boundary detector at this vocabulary scale, and it
is the representation the build already produces.
4
The certificate out of sample
Proposition 1 certifies recall on the query distribution the certificate was computed on. Production query
distributions drift, and an index whose selling point is a recall figure must say what that figure is worth
when they do. This section develops the per-query provision sketched in §2.2, fits it on seven corpora and
thirteen query sets, and reports the one regime in which it fails.
The provision the index carries.
The certificate is an expectation over the query distribution it was
computed on; a query from outside that distribution needs a provision, and the index carries one. Because S
is a uniform sample of X, the ks routed distances d(q, s(1)) ≤· · · ≤d(q, s(ks)) are order statistics of a uniform
sample of q’s own distance distribution — an unbiased probe of the query’s neighborhood that routing has
already paid for. The local intrinsic dimension they estimate (the maximum-likelihood estimator of Levina
and Bickel [21], used as a query-hardness statistic by Amsaleg et al. [22]) stratifies coverage. On a 200,000-
object corpus of text embeddings with in-distribution and out-of-distribution query sets (experiment gate o
in the artifact; exact signatures, so the measurement is of the index rather than of the inner search), the shift
raises the miss rate 1.8× at (b, ks) = (8, 64) (0.9959 to 0.9926) and moves the median estimated dimension
from 35.6 to 40.0, while coverage conditioned on the estimate barely moves. A table of coverage per decile
8


of the statistic, fitted on the in-distribution certification sample alone, predicts the out-of-distribution recall
to 0.0009 at (8, 64) and 0.0035 at (8, 32), against 0.0033 and 0.0094 for the unconditioned certificate. The
same table drives the dial: choosing ks per query as the smallest value whose row meets a target of 0.99
meets it on the shifted queries (0.9934–0.9948 for b ∈{8, 16, 32}) with 26–34% fewer misses than a fixed
ks touching the same posting mass. A query whose statistic falls outside the calibrated range is served
and reported as uncertified.
Underneath the table is the level of fX resolved per query: the rate κ in
1 −cov ≈exp(−κ bks/|S|), fitted per decile of the statistic at (4, 8), runs monotonically from 479 to 124 and
agrees decile by decile across the two query sets. The triangle inequality also gives a deterministic exactness
radius — if 2 d(q, y10) < d(q, s(ks)) −d(q, s(b)) the served answer is provably exact in any metric space —
but at these dimensions it certifies under 0.1% of queries, and we do not rely on it.
The rate is a property of the query; the curve, of the corpus.
The certificate is per corpus; the
statistic behind it need not be. Writing the touched mass as bks n/|S| objects, 1 −cov ≈exp(−(κ/n) ·
mass): κ/n is the coverage bought per touched object, comparable across corpora of different n and |S|.
Table 2 repeats the measurement on the corpora of §5 and on three larger ones: LAION-10M (CLIP image
embeddings under cosine, the SISAP 2024 query set split in half), and two with a query shift built in —
PubMed23 (24M abstract embeddings, with an out-of-distribution query file), and Yandex T2I-10M under
ℓ2 and under inner product (maximum inner-product search through the standard augmentation), where
the in-distribution queries are images and the shifted queries are text. On every set the estimate stratifies
coverage (Spearman ρ from −0.46 to −0.71 at (4, 8)) and the rate falls monotonically from the first to the
last decile of the statistic, by 4–18×.
A decile table fitted on one query set predicts another to within
0.0039 on every in-domain transfer — the other half of a random split (0.0003 on LAION-10M), the shifted
wiki queries, and the shifted PubMed queries (0.0039 against 0.0084 for the unconditioned certificate). On
PubMed23 the dial meets its 0.99 target on the shifted queries (0.9926–0.9940 for b ∈{8, 16, 32}) touching
29–34% less posting mass than the fixed ks that reaches the same coverage.
Figure 3 plots κ/n against the absolute value of the statistic. Within a corpus the curve is invariant
to everything we varied. SIFT-1M and Deep-10M, ten times apart in n and |S|, agree bin by bin (median
ratio 1.00 over 13 shared bins); the two halves of the LAION split coincide (ratio 0.99); the shifted wiki and
PubMed queries land on their in-distribution curves (ratios 0.95 and 1.04); and T2I under inner product
lands on T2I under ℓ2 (ratio 1.11) — the rate is a property of the query in its corpus, not of the distance
function. Across corpora the curves are parallel but offset, by up to 3.27× at equal estimated dimension
(GloVe 2.72×, LAION 2.31× and PubMed23 2.17× above SIFT, T2I 1.29× above Deep-10M). The offset is
corpus-specific rather than a split by distance family — the three cosine corpora (wiki, LAION, PubMed23)
sit at three levels between the ℓ2 descriptors and the angular GloVe — so the estimate is one of at least two
statistics behind the local level of fX, and the table is fitted per corpus. The cross-modal shift is the case
the table does not cover, and the certificate says so: text queries against the image corpus fall beyond the
99th percentile of the image queries’ estimates for 86% (ℓ2) and 66% (inner product) of the set, and where
the two overlap the text queries sit 2.70× above the image curve at equal estimate: the shift changes the
neighborhood structure, not only its dimension. These queries are served uncertified; the decile table would
predict them to 0.0440 and 0.0233, and the dial reaches 0.9523 and 0.9762 at its ks cap against a target of
0.99. Certifying them takes a table fitted on a sample of text queries, which is the same construction with
a different calibration set. The exactness radius is inert on every corpus (at most 1.1% of queries certified).

5.3
Serving Deep-100M at 1 GB resident, and below
Table 4 is the disk mode of §2.5 under enforced cgroup caps: swap off, every input evicted from the page
cache before it is mapped, one operating point per process, the scope’s own memory.peak. The object level
is the same in every row — posting-ordered SQ8 leaf blocks on NVMe, read by explicit pread, results bit-
identical to RAM serving — and what changes is the router. At depth 1 it is the flat 2M-point image,
963 MiB, file-backed and reclaimable page by page. At depth 2 it is the index’s own rule pointed upward
(§8.1): a 2% sample of the vocabulary, 18.3 MiB resident (53× less), with the vocabulary’s own posting lists
as an SQ4 stream on NVMe read by the same kernel, so a query routes in two hops — 4.8 MB of counted
IO — and then scans the object level exactly as before.
Three readings. The flat router does not fit 1 GB at 108. At 2 GB depth 1 serves at its uncapped rate;
at 1 GB it reads 8.0 qps cold and 1.7 warm, with routing at 547.3 ms per query, recall unchanged: the beam
paging its graph while the scan’s own reads evict it. Its floor sits between 1.5 and 2 GB and depends on the
operating point — Table 5 — 16–21 B/object against the 8.96 B/object router term of §8.1, the difference
being the scratch and the reads in flight. Depth 2 removes the wall and costs nothing above it. At 2 GB the
two routes read the same recall and rate (0.9977 at 9.8 vs. 0.9979 at 10.5 qps at ks=512); at 1 GB depth
2 serves 0.9977 at 8.7 qps where depth 1 collapsed, and the route costs 18.4 ms of the query. That is 10.7
resident bytes per object at recall 0.9977. Below 1 GB the cap is on the threads, not the index. With an
18.3-MiB router the resident state is per-thread scratch and buffers — the dedupe bitmap alone is n/8 bytes
a thread, 200 MB at 16 threads — so the floor moves with the thread count: 512 MB at 8 threads serves
12.8 qps (5.4 B/object) and 256 MB at 4 threads 7.3 qps (2.7 B/object), both at recall 0.9964. The resident
budget is router plus threads times (scratch plus reads in flight), the three-term law of §8.1, and at 108 the
second term is the whole of it once the router recurses.

Where SOLO sits
Table 7 reports throughput against HNSW because a reader expects it, but a throughput contest is a contest
over a region both architectures already reach, decided by kernels and by the machine (§5). The claim this
paper actually makes is different: SOLO has operating points that no measured system has at all, and the
single rule that produces them extends them. Four legs, in the order the evidence supports them.
19


1. It serves where nothing else fits, and the paper measured why.
At Deep-100M SOLO serves
recall 0.9979 from 10.7 bytes of resident memory per object under an enforced cgroup cap, with peak equal to
cap at 1, 2 and 4 GB and bit-identical results (Table 4). Four other systems were run through the identical
protocol (Table 6): SPANN, DiskANN and vote-served misi are killed below 8, 12 and 6 GB respectively, and
the one baseline that does run at 1 GB, a Faiss IVF-PQ index at 56.1 B/object, saturates at 0.8747 recall. At
a 1 GB budget that is the difference between indexing 107 objects and indexing 108: an order of magnitude
in capacity, not in speed.
The reason is structural, and the counterfactual is on record. Paging a random-access navigation structure
amplifies reads ∼85× — letting vector reads fault through mmap pulls 6 TB in 80 minutes under a 4 GB cap
(§2.5) — because a beam goes where the graph leads, so the access order is not known until the query is over.
A scan’s access order is known before the query starts, which is what makes explicit, sequential, counted
IO possible at all. The alternative that would fit under the cap, scoring with quantized codes instead of
verifying with the true distance, cannot reach the recall: SPANN’s native Int8 scorer saturates at 0.9561 on
Deep-100M against float ground truth (§5.6) — on the identical grid that is lossless when used as a screen
with exact refinement (§2.4). So at high recall under a tight memory budget the field is empty, and it is
empty for a reason we can state as a mechanism rather than observe as a gap.
2.
The load decomposes vertically, which is what a graph cannot do.
Every non-leaf list in
SOLO is a complete SOLO index behind a two-argument interface: given a query and a local budget, return
candidates. Nothing crosses a level except a candidate set. A beam search exposes no such interface — it
goes where the graph leads — which is why distributed graph systems replicate whole indexes rather than
split them, and why a partitioned graph pays a network hop per traversal step.
The evidence that the decomposition behaves is already in this paper. Recall composes multiplicatively
across levels, and the depth-2 index lands on its composed prediction to 2×10−4 (§2.3). Coverage inside the
decomposed lists is not degraded but improved, up to 0.9994. The recursion beat the obvious alternative — a
graph index attached to each oversized list — on recall and throughput simultaneously, with a build cheaper
by 25× (§5.2). And the whole path is bitwise deterministic at any thread count and across machines (§2.7),
so a sharded deployment can be verified against a single-node reference, object for object. The consequence
for distribution is worth stating plainly: because per-level coverage multiplies and each level is certifiable on
its own, the recall of a distributed deployment is predictable from per-level certificates before
it is assembled. Split across two processes, with a 19.5 MiB routing tier and 1312 bytes per query on the
wire, the depth-2 GloVe index returns answers identical to the single-process index, object for object (§6.1).
3. Partitioning pays here and does not pay for the graph.
Four socket-local replicas lift SOLO by
2.2× and hnswlib by 1.6× on the same machine and queries (§5.6). The asymmetry is not incidental: a scan
is bandwidth-bound and a walk is latency-bound, so replication converts memory controllers into throughput
for one and buys much less for the other. The same asymmetry holds across sockets, NUMA nodes, threads
and machines, for the same reason.
4.
The same rule pointed upward is a trillion-scale architecture.
The one resident term that
grows with n is the router, so resident(n, L) = αLn cr and each recursion level multiplies the capacity ceiling
by 1/α = 50 (§8.1). That places n∗≈1.2 × 108 at depth 1 — exactly where the measured Deep-100M
deployment of Table 4 sits — 5.6 × 109 at depth 2, and 1011–1012 at depth 3, each level adding a few
megabytes of sequential IO per query. Depth 2 is no longer a projection: it is measured in §8.1 at 0.75× the
throughput and 0.0009 of recall, for 50× less resident routing state. Work per query grows sublinearly on
top of that: the scanned fraction of the database falls 5–8× per decade of n (§3), so the levels buy capacity
without the scan growing in proportion.
6.1
What was projection here, and what closed it
Three of these four legs rested on measurements already in this paper; the other parts were projections, and
we name them — and what has since been run — because a reader should know which is which.
The emptiness of the region: measured, and narrower than claimed. The projection here was that a table
of resident bytes per object under identical caps, every baseline swept to its own ceiling, would convert “no
20


system we ran” into “no system”. It was run (Table 6, §5.4), and it does not say that. IVF-PQ runs at 1 GB;
the graph and cluster indexes do not, by margins of 6 to 12× in bytes per object. So the correct claim is not
that nothing fits under the cap but that nothing else fits and answers: the one architecture small enough
is capped at 0.8747 recall by its own codes, and the architectures that reach 0.99 need 6–12 GB to do it.
The region SOLO occupies is high recall and a tight budget, and it is the conjunction, not either half, that
is empty. Measuring it also produced the mechanism a second time, in a system with no graph: capping
IVF-PQ costs it 40× in throughput where it costs SOLO 3%, because a probed list is a random read and a
scan is not.
Vertical distribution: measured. The depth-2 GloVe index was served with its two levels in separate
processes, the router tier holding only the level-1 router (19.5 MiB — each hub’s local sample belongs to
the hub’s own index, at the leaf tier) and the wire carrying ks vocabulary ids, 1312 bytes per query. The
split answers are identical to the single-process answers, object for object, at every operating point, which
is the acceptance test that matters: nothing crosses a level except a candidate set. The prototype’s hop is
synchronous and unpipelined and both tiers shared one machine’s cores, so its throughput cost (12–33%)
measures the prototype, not the design; what the run establishes is the interface and the wire, which is what
leg 2 claims.
The upward recursion: measured at the step that matters. Indexing the 2M-point Deep-100M router with
SOLO itself cuts resident routing state 50× (854 →17.1 MiB) for at most 0.0009 of end-to-end recall, with the
certificate composing to 0.0008 at both depths (§8.1). That moves 5.6×109 from projection to demonstrated
mechanism at depth 2; depth 3 remains arithmetic. The sublinear-work exponent the trillion-scale estimate
leans on is now fitted over four decades rather than two (§3), and the fourth changed its reading: work grows
as n0.4–0.6 with the steepest decade last, so a fitted exponent extrapolates optimistically, not conservatively.
The scanned fraction still falls throughout (0.070% at 109).

Related work
Cluster-routed lists. IVF and its quantized descendants [9] route to k-means cells and scan them; SPANN
[18] scales this to storage with balanced hierarchical clustering, closure multi-assignment (≤8 replicas),
and an in-memory centroid graph [19], with SPFresh [20] adding update protocols.
SOLO occupies the
same serving silhouette with a random vocabulary (no clustering; deterministic; metric-black-box), rank-b
assignment (measured to dominate closure, §3.4), serve-time replication, recursion instead of balancing, an
exact scan (its 8-bit screen re-ranks exactly, unlike lossy IVFPQ scoring), and a certificate. Permutation and
pivot indexes. Objects can be described by the order in which a fixed pivot set is seen from them [2]; MI-File
[3] indexes that description in an inverted file and NAPP [4, 5] keeps only the nearest pivots and intersects.
All of them draw the pivots from a set of constant size: NAPP attains perfect recall at 106 (Table 7, text)
but its resolution cannot grow with n, which is the property 108 demands. misi [6] made the vocabulary a
linear-size sample and served by vote-then-verify; SOLO keeps the representation and replaces the serving
theory: scan-only, certificate, recursion, quantized leaves. Navigable graphs. HNSW [13], DiskANN [14],
and GRAFT [7] are the throughput frontier in RAM below their recall ceilings; their recall is emergent, their
updates hard, and their disk incarnations pin navigation state. The comparison of §5.6 is deliberately against
the strongest of each.

But cr itself is compressible, and the first step is free.
The law above already admits an 8-bit router.
What that costs is a question about the certificate, not the throughput: quantizing the router changes which
vocabulary points a query routes to, and coverage is recall (Prop. 1). We measured it on four corpora
spanning dimension and intrinsic dimension, ranking the vocabulary under each code. The comparison is
paired — the same 2,000 queries under both routers — which is what makes it decidable: an absolute
coverage estimate from a few hundred queries has a standard error near a coverage point, larger than the
effect, while the paired difference is dominated by the many queries whose routed pool is identical under the
two codes.
An 8-bit router is free, and the pairing says so sharply: at ks=32 the paired cost is 0.0000 on
every corpus, with a bootstrap interval no wider than ±0.0008, and the number of queries whose routed pool
changes at all is 0 of 2,000 on wiki-1024, 5 on Deep-96, 4 on SIFT-128 and 49 on GloVe. Four bits is free on
three of the four (+0.0000, −0.0001, −0.0002) and costs −0.0072 on GloVe.
Two bits is a different matter, and the split is not the one dimension alone would predict. At essentially
equal dimension the penalty differs six-fold — −0.0121 on Deep-96 against −0.0731 on GloVe-100 — so what
governs it is how concentrated the data is, with dimension helping separately because the ternary code keeps
d/2 coordinates: 512 survive at d=1024, where the cost is only −0.0018, and 50 at d=100. A 2-bit router is
therefore safe on concentrated or high-dimensional corpora and expensive on spread ones, which is the same
regime split the equal-work law draws (§3). Quantizing the query as well is never worth it: it costs a further
−0.1456 on GloVe and −0.0335 on Deep, and the query is one vector.

What the law does and does not say about n.
Two readings of the floor are tempting and both are
wrong. The router term is still linear in n: recursion divides its constant by 50 per level, it does not remove
it, so the 189 MiB depth-2 router at 109 is ∼1.8 GiB at 1010 at the same depth (depth 3 makes it ∼38 MiB;
at 109 depth 3 is built and served, §5.8, with a 3.6 MiB router). And the scratch is independent of n only
at a fixed operating point, and holding recall fixed does not fix the operating point: the candidates a query
must touch at a recall target grow with n as the exponents of §3 say — n0.4–0.6 over the measured range,
steepest in the last decade — so T · s(op) grows the same way. It is small in bytes, a few MiB per thread
at 109, but it is not a constant. “Deliverable at any database size” therefore means that for every n there is
a depth L at which the resident state fits a fixed budget, each such L a build, and it is measured through
depth 3 at 109, where depth 3 serves from 96 MB and depth 2 no longer fits 128 MB (Table 11). What
would block 1010 in this implementation is not memory: the object identifiers are 32-bit in both kernels and
in the layout builder, the layout build sorts n · b identifiers in RAM, and the stream and base together are
∼7.6 TiB. Those are engineering, and they are stated here so that the ceiling is not read as a claim past the
last measured point.
The upward gate, measured.
The step from depth 1 to depth 2 multiplies an additional router-level
coverage factor into the certificate, and that factor is the whole of the projection. We ran it on Deep-100M:
the vocabulary S (2M points, a 854 MiB flat router) was itself indexed by SOLO — a 2% sample of S (40k
points, 17.1 MiB resident, 50× less) with the 2M vocabulary posted to it at br=8 — and queries routed in
two hops, the router level scanned by the same fused kernel as the object level. The object level was left
untouched.
Routing loses what one would expect and the object level absorbs it: at ks=64 the two-hop route returns
0.9843 of the exact top-ks vocabulary terms against the flat router’s 0.9995, yet end-to-end recall falls only
from 0.9874 to 0.9866 — at most 0.0009 across the grid, because a covered neighbour sits in a median of
several touched lists (§3.3) and losing one term rarely loses the object. Throughput costs 0.75× at ks=256
(1,793 →1,341 qps) here, where the flat router fits in RAM; at 109, where it does not, the sign flips (above).
And the certificate composes: coverage under the depth-2 route predicts the served recall to 0.0008, the
same residual as at depth 1. The mechanism behind n∗(L) = n∗(1)(1/α)L−1 is therefore measured at the
step where it starts, at 108 and at 109; beyond depth 2 it remains arithmetic.

Predicate-constrained search — the top-k among objects

and how the result compares with filtered-DiskANN and ACORN on the same corpus, is an experiment this
paper does not contain, and we decline to claim the outcome before running it.
8.3
Other limitations
The 108 throughput comparison is measured on one machine whose memory system (four 2 GHz Haswell
sockets, ∼50 GB/s to one process) bounds a streaming kernel more than a latency-bound graph walk; the
socket-local protocol removes most of that bound (§5.6), but the ratio between the two systems on current
hardware — where the same kernel runs ten times faster per core on the laptop — remains to be measured.
Query-batched scanning (int8 GEMM/ADC over per-list tiles, reuse nqks/|S|) remains unimplemented. Disk
throughput is bandwidth-bound at ∼40 KB mean reads; batching and SQ4 project 50–100 qps at 1 GB. Deep-
100M rows are single-seed. The out-of-sample provision (§4) is measured on 7 corpora, with one random
split (LAION-10M), two in-domain distribution shifts (wiki, PubMed23) and one cross-modal shift (T2I-
10M); the inner search adds a term the calibration table absorbs by construction, and the three corpora
with GRAFT signatures are the measurement of it. The corpus-specific offsets in Figure 3 say the dimension
estimate is one of at least two statistics, so the table is per corpus; the cross-modal shift lands beyond the
table and is flagged rather than certified, and whether a table fitted on a sample of the shifted queries then
transfers is untested. Per-object adaptive b (rank-gap statistics) may dominate both fixed-b and closure.
9
Conclusion
Making the serving path trivial — route, scan, return — turns recall from an emergent property into a
certificate, replication into insurance, quantization into lossless acceleration, and skew into a recursion. The
result serves a hundred million vectors past the saturation point of a tuned HNSW, from one gigabyte of
resident memory in its disk mode, with exact deletes and a build that is nothing but sampling and searching.
At scale, the right primitive may not be a better navigator, but a better bill of what must be looked at.
Artifact and reproducibility.
Every number in this paper was written by the run that produced it into
a versioned manifest recording the code commit, the machine, the complete parameter set, the seed, and
the raw timings; baselines were run by us on the same machines, queries, and ground truth as SOLO, under
saturation sweeps rather than at a single operating point. The implementation, the experiment drivers, and
the manifests are at https://github.com/zevahcle/SOLO.
