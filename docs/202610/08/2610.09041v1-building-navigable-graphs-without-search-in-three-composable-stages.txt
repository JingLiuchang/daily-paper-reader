# Building Navigable Graphs Without Search in Three Composable Stages 

Édgar Chávez 

> Computer Science Department, CICESE, Ensenada, México

Abstract 

Navigable graphs can be built without searching for neighbors: partition the data, evaluate every pair inside each part, and select each point’s edges from the candidates. We give such a construction in three separable stages and show that the middle one decides the quality. The pool is any partition with a few memberships per point. The ending turns a point’s candidates into out-edges; ours keeps a bounded heap, prunes by occlusion with a per-corpus slack, and appends reverse edges, re-pruning only where a list overflows. The 

spine is any edge set, exempt from the prune, that keeps the graph reachable from its entry; ours, half-space-proximal edges over a random sample, routes monotonically to every sampled point and replaces a spanning tree at 1/10 

to 1/500 of its cost. The ending composes with any partitioner: on PiPNN’s own candidate pool it beats PiPNN’s ending on each of six corpora from 10 6

to 10 8 points, by 3 to 14% in distance evaluations at equal recall, and with 60 to 120 memberships per point the composed build matches or beats a full dense construction at k = 10 and k = 100 on all six, in 0.5 to 0.9 of its build time, deterministically. The analysis explains why. Once a pool is localised its quality is set by the data: every pool built on GIST lands within 4% of the exact-kNN ceiling, and the pairs a block cover misses are predicted, point by point, by the local clustering of the kNN graph, whose zero-clustering tail sets the memberships a corpus needs and grows with n. All code, patches and logs are public. 

Keywords: approximate nearest neighbor, navigable graphs, index construction, k-NN graph, half-space proximal graph, determinism

1. Introduction 

This paper gives a construction of navigable graphs that never searches. Partition the points into overlapping blocks; evaluate every pair inside each block with a dense kernel; keep, for each point, its C nearest candidates; prune them to at most R out-edges by the occlusion rule with a slack α;append the reverse of every surviving edge and re-prune only the lists that overflow; and union a spine — an edge set exempt from the prune that keeps the graph reachable from its entry; ours is half-space-proximal edges over a random sample. Three stages — the pool, the ending, the spine — and no stage chooses a distance to evaluate from the result of a previous one. The construction is deterministic, and two of its stages are components that attach to any partitioner, including the fastest one published. The results are these. On PiPNN’s own candidate pool [1], our ending beats PiPNN’s ending on every corpus we measured, by 3 to 14% in distance evaluations at equal recall, for 8–16% more build time (Section 4). With 60 to 120 memberships per point the composed build — PiPNN’s partition, our ending, our spine — matches or beats a full dense construction on all six corpora at k = 10 and k = 100 , in 0.51 –0.86 × its build time; on Deep-100M it needs 6, 13 and 6% fewer evaluations than the dense build at recall 0.95 , 0.99 

and k = 100 in 0.60 × its time, and 11 –23% fewer than Vamana (Section 3). Our spine replaces the spanning tree of the original dense build at 1/10 to 

1/500 of its distance evaluations, is never more than 1.1% worse and up to 

5% better from 10 6 to 10 8, and sizes itself (Section 5). Every graph in the paper, including the baselines, is graded by one searcher on one machine. 

Why search-free. Most graph indexes are built by search: HNSW inserts points with a beam over the graph so far [2], NSG and Vamana run a beam over a full graph and prune [3, 4], ParlayANN batches the insertions [5], 

Graft harvests neighborhoods with a beam over a frozen forest [6]. Abeam’s distance evaluations are dependent random accesses, and a companion paper [7] measures that this bounds construction an order of magnitude below the machine’s dense arithmetic, independently of the algorithm and increasingly so with the dimension. Fixing the pairs before evaluating them is the alternative, and it is older than the indexes it competes with: recursive bisection with brute force in the parts [8], several random partitions [9], spanning trees inside random clusters [10], PiPNN’s recursive random-leader 2partition with a dense product per leaf [1], and on GPUs the kNN graphs of GGNN, CAGRA, cuVS and Tagore reworked into navigable ones [11–14]. These builders are fast. Which of their decisions set the quality of the graph had not been separated, and separating them is what makes two of the stages reusable. 

How quality is decided. The second half of the paper measures each stage with the other two held fixed. Where the partition is good the pool is not the lever: with the ending fixed, every pool we built on GIST — k-means with five memberships, random leaders with thirty, a shared-neighbor count — lands within 4% of the graph built from the exact kNN lists (Section 6). What a pool costs is written in the data: the pairs a block cover misses are predicted, point by point and in every corpus, by the local clustering coefficient of the kNN graph; the fraction of points with zero clustering orders the corpora by difficulty, grows with n on the same corpus ( 1.1% at 2 · 10 5

to 8.1% at 10 8 on Deep), and is what the 60 to 120 memberships above are paying for. Uniform memberships are within 4% of an oracle that knows each point’s clustering. The ending’s own two decisions, the order of prune and symmetrization and the slack against exact candidates, are each worth 8–43% 

on identical candidates. 

Contributions. 

1. A reusable ending. A bounded heap, an occlusion prune with a per-corpus slack, and prune-then-symmetrize order. On PiPNN’s own pool it improves PiPNN’s graphs on every corpus ( 3–14% ); on exact candidates it is 39 –43% better than Vamana’s slack (Section 4). 2. A spine without search. We define the spine by the reachability it must guarantee (Definition 1) and give an instance that needs no search: half-space-proximal edges over a random sample, which route monotonically to every sampled point (Proposition 2); with a degree cap of 16 and an automatic sample size they replace a spanning tree at 

1/10 –1/500 of its cost (Section 5). 3. The composed build. PiPNN’s partition with our ending and spine matches or beats a full dense construction on six corpora at both k,in 0.51 –0.86 × its time, and is bitwise deterministic at 10 6 once three sources of nondeterminism in PiPNN are removed (Sections 3 and 4). 4. Where quality comes from. Once a pool is localised its quality is set by the ending, not the partitioner (GIST: every pool within 4% of the 3exact-kNN ceiling); the cost of a pool is a property of the kNN graph’s clustering, dominated by its zero-clustering tail, which grows with n;uniform memberships are near-optimal (Section 6). 

Scope. The kernels need distances that decompose into inner products; for a black-box metric the evaluation count is the cost and search-based builders are the right design. PiPNN builds 6–15 × faster than our dense construction, and the composed build is PiPNN’s build plus 8–16% : the contribution is quality per evaluation and a component that composes, not a faster partitioner. The clustering result is correlational evidence over six corpora and three values of n (Section 6 states it as a conjecture). Partition-then-brute-force construction has a long history, cited above; this paper separates its stages and measures them. Section 2 defines the construction and the protocol. Section 3 reports the composed build against the dense build, Vamana, PiPNN and HNSW. Sections 4, 5 and 6 measure the ending, the spine and the pool in turn. Section 7 places the work, Section 8 states its limits. 

2. The construction 

The construction takes n points and produces a directed graph of out-degree at most R with an entry point, in three stages that can be run, measured and replaced independently. This section defines each stage, the configuration we recommend, the dense instance and its kernel, the determinism guarantee, and the protocol under which every number in the paper was measured.

The Dense candidate stage evaluates every unordered pair once, in its lowest shared leaf, offers the result to both endpoints’ heaps, and runs as a register-tiled symmetric scan scheduled in round-robin tournament order. On the 64-thread Xeon used throughout it runs at 2.36 G pair-evaluations per second, 53% of the machine’s SGEMM rate, and as a fused GEMM-plus-select on a 6 GB laptop GPU at 7.9 G/s (Appendix A). With balanced leaves the candidate stage costs 12 n2m2/L pairs: quadratic at a fixed fraction of the database, linear at a fixed absolute pool P = nm 2/L , which is how it was run at 10 8 (L = 24 ,000 , P ≈ 10 5; the pair count grew 11 .1× for 10 × the points). 

2.3. Determinism 

Proposition 1 (Schedule independence) . Fix the data, the seed and the parameters. The Dense graph is independent of the number of threads and of the order in which tiles execute. Proof sketch. The leaders are a deterministic function of the seeded sample and a fixed summation order; memberships are the m smallest (distance , index )

keys; every pair is evaluated exactly once, so the multiset of offers to each heap is fixed, and a heap retaining the C smallest (distance , id ) keys is a commutative aggregation of its offers; the prune is a per-vertex pure function of its list in that total order; the HSP spine is a pure function of the seeded sample and its distance matrix (Section 5). A composition of pure functions is pure. The gate we run is stronger: an adjacency hash and the total distance count must agree at 1 and 64 threads before any number is recorded; the distance count has caught a silently skipped parallel loop that left the graph byte-identical. Stock PiPNN is not deterministic (reruns differ by up to ±3% 

in query cost); Section 4 removes the causes.

4. The ending 

The ending is where the quality of a search-free graph is decided, and it is three decisions: a per-point heap bounded at C, an occlusion prune with slack α, and the order in which pruning and symmetrization are applied. Two of them are worth 8–43% on identical candidates (Section 4.1); the ending as a whole improves PiPNN’s graphs on every corpus when applied to PiPNN’s own pool (Section 4.2); and it makes the composed build deterministic (Section 4.3).

4.3. A deterministic composed build 

Our ending is deterministic (Proposition 1); PiPNN is not, and neither is anything composed with it. We found three causes and removed them behind a flag, leaving default PiPNN untouched: (i) the partition seed was drawn from std::random_device ; (ii) merging undersized buckets used a hash-based duplicate removal whose output order varies between runs, and the next level samples leaders by position , so the leaves themselves changed; (iii) the sketch-hash vectors were drawn by a parallel tabulate through one shared, stateful normal distribution, whose Box–Muller cache made the hash family depend on scheduling. We also made the reservoir merge order-free: candidates are totally ordered by (bfloat16 distance, id), a hash bucket keeps its minimum, a full table evicts its maximum. The composed SIFT graph is then byte-identical at 64 and 32 threads, at quality inside PiPNN’s run-to-run band (GIST 4,556 against 4,548 –4,672 ). Keeping the table’s maximum cached across merge calls makes the order-free rule cost ≈ 3% of PiPNN’s build even on 400-entry tables. At 10 8 two runs reproduce quality to the digit but not every byte (a 10 −9 residual, Section 8). 11 5. The spine 

A graph built inside blocks can be disconnected, and the prune can make it so even when the blocks overlap. This section says what a spine must guarantee (Section 5.1), compares the instances we built against that guarantee (Section 5.2), and then measures the one we recommend, HSP edges over a sample (Section 5.3). 

5.1. What a spine must guarantee 

A partition-built graph can shatter. On a synthetic set of 800 Gaussian clusters, k-means leaves coincide with the clusters and an α = 1 prune removes every cross-cluster candidate behind a nearer same-cluster one; the graph is a disjoint union and returns recall 0.002 . More pool does not help: the cross-leaf candidates are generated and then pruned. Whatever repairs this must therefore be added after the prune and be exempt from it. 

Definition 1 (Spine) . Let G = ( V, E ) be the graph after the prune, e ∈ V its entry point and T ⊆ V a target set. A spine for T is an edge set ES , unioned with E after the prune and exempt from it, such that every t ∈ T is reachable from e in (V, E ∪ ES ). It is monotone for T if, from e and from every vertex of 

T , greedy routing toward any t ∈ T along ES strictly decreases the distance to t at every step and ends at t.The definition separates what a spine guarantees (its target set, and whether reaching it is monotone) from how it is built and what it costs. Two costs matter: the distance evaluations of its construction, and the degree it adds to the vertices that carry it, which every beam expanding them pays (Section 5.3).

5.3. The HSP spine in practice The price of a spine is its degree. HSP degree is not bounded in high dimension. The median out-degree on real data grows as a power of the sample size up to 

10 5 with no saturation (Table 7), and the sample points’ extra out-edges are paid by every beam that expands them. Capping each row at its first c HSP neighbors (Table 8) removes the price: capped at 8 or 16, every sample size up to 30 k is within ≈ 1% of no spine, where uncapped spines cost +6% at 

30 k and +18 –26% at 100 k. The cap has a floor set by the guarantee: at a cap of 8 the clustered set falls to 0.66 recall at |S| = 10 k, because the dropped far neighbors are exactly the eliminators Proposition 2 uses, so a capped HSP spine is no longer monotone for S by construction; at 16 it keeps 0.968 at 10 kand 0.998 at 30 k.

7. Related work 

Graph indexes built by search. From navigable small worlds [21] through HNSW [2], NSG [3], FANNG [22] and Vamana/DiskANN [4] to ParlayANN [5] and Graft [6], a beam supplies each point’s candidates; the cost model that bounds them is measured in [7], after Hong and Kung [23] and the roofline [24]. 

Partition then brute force. Recursive Lanczos bisection [8], multiple random divisions [9], HCNNG [10], PiPNN [1] and graph-partitioned indexes [25] all evaluate pairs inside parts; SPANN partitions for disk-resident search [26]. On GPUs, GGNN [11], CAGRA [12], cuVS all-neighbors [13], Tagore [14] and FAISS [27, 28] build kNN graphs by blocked products and rework them. Our construction is a member of this family; the contribution here is the separation of its stages and the measurement of each, not the construction. ParlayANN’s builders are deterministic by design; what we add is the determinism of a composed build with a partitioner that was not. 

Refinement. NN-descent [29], EFANNA [30], SPTAG [31] and RNN-Descent [32] refine a graph by batched rounds; Yang et al. identify candidate acquisition as the dominant construction cost and accelerate RNG pruning [33]. 18 Pruning and connectivity. The occlusion rule descends from the relative neighborhood graph [34, 35]; the HSP graph [20] and the SAT [16] are the two structures we use as spines. The intrinsic-dimension dependence of search work is surveyed in [36, 37]; quantisers that compress the scan side [18, 38–40] are lossless filters inside a pool in our measurements and do not change its quality (Appendix A). 

8. Scope and limitations 

1. Vectors, not metric spaces. The kernels need distances that decom-pose into inner products. 2. Six corpora, three values of n. The clustering results are correla-tional; the conjecture of Section 6 is not proved. 3. One run per PiPNN configuration in the membership studies (two at 10 8, 120 leaves), and PiPNN’s stock build varies by up to ±3% ; the deterministic variant removes the variance at about 3% of PiPNN’s build time. 4. QPS is not explained. At equal distance counts Vamana’s graphs run faster; we report distances as the cost and QPS alongside. 5. hnswlib is measured as a lower bound (level-0 graph, flat). 6. The HSP/SAT recall-ceiling gap on GIST (0.9962 against 0.9977 )is unexplained. 7. Memory. Dense ’s heaps are nC keys of 8 bytes, 160 GB at 10 8;PiPNN’s reservoir at 400 entries is 320 GB at 10 8, and the deterministic 120-leaf build peaked at 679 GB. 8. Determinism at 10 8 is not bitwise. The composed build is byte-identical across thread counts at 10 6 (gated); two runs at 10 8 reproduce quality to the digit but differ in 38 of 3.8 · 10 10 candidates and 528 of 

4.1 · 10 9 edges. The source is not identified. 9. Beyond 10 8. A localiser over 2 · 10 7 random leaders was run at 10 9

(9.6 h); no graph is built past 10 8.19 9. Conclusion 

A navigable graph can be built without search, deterministically, from three separable stages, and the stage that decides its quality is the one that costs least: the ending. Attached to the fastest published partition it improves that partition’s graphs on every corpus, and with enough memberships per point it matches or beats a full dense construction at 10 6 to 10 8 points and at k = 10 and k = 100 , in half to nine tenths of the time. Connectivity needs a spine, defined by the reachability it guarantees, not by a construction; the instance we give, half-space-proximal edges over a sample, needs no search, costs |S|2 distances and routes monotonically to every sampled point. What remains with the data is the pool: how many memberships a corpus needs is written in the tail of its kNN graph where neighbors do not know each other, that tail grows with n, and spending memberships uniformly is as good as spending them where it is. The construction, the patches that make PiPNN deterministic and exportable, and every log are public.
